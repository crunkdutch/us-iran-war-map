🌍 VP Aref’s wartime coordination kept Iran’s government services on track

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s government maintained the flow of essential services and executive operations during the recent war as Vice President Mohammad Reza Aref coordinated closely with ministers and senior officials, overseeing efforts to secure vital supplies, sustain critical infrastructure and keep public services running despite the extraordinary conditions.


Wartime governance required simultaneous attention to essential goods, fuel, medicine, energy, transportation, banking services and other critical infrastructure.
​
Aref had previously said the administration had prepared plans for managing the country under wartime conditions, emphasizing the need to maintain development while preparing for difficult scenarios.
​
During the conflict, Aref held phone discussions with several ministers and senior officials to review the latest situation and follow up on measures related to essential supplies, infrastructure stability, market conditions and public services.
​
His coordination also extended to transportation and infrastructure organizations. During visits to the Ports and Maritime Organization and the railway company, Aref highlighted the continued performance of these institutions and their role in preventing disruptions to people’s daily lives.
​
The experience also underscored the complementary roles of President Masoud Pezeshkian and the first vice president. While the president remained the country’s principal political and executive decision-maker, Aref focused on coordination and implementation across government bodies.
​
Following the war, the government also turned its attention to reconstruction and addressing damage to infrastructure and affected areas.
​
Aref’s wartime role can therefore be viewed across three stages: preparedness before the crisis, coordination during the conflict, and follow-up on reconstruction afterward.

📡 Air Defense Force commander: Iran to expand successful defense systems

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The commander of the Iranian Army’s Air Defense Force says Iran is moving forward by relying entirely on domestic capabilities, expanding its air defense systems and replicating those that performed successfully during the 40-day war.


Brigadier General Alireza Elhami made the remarks on Wednesday at a ceremony honoring doctors at Iranban Hospital, a specialized hospital operated by the Iranian Army’s Air Defense Force.
​
He stressed that Iran’s air defense efforts are based on domestic capabilities, saying the country is increasing the number of its systems and replicating those that performed well during the 40-day war waged by Israel and the United States on February 28.
​
Elhami added that new systems and equipment are also being developed and manufactured based on the lessons learned, emphasizing that all such measures rely entirely on Iranian knowledge and expertise.
​
Regarding the changing nature of air threats, Elhami said enemy threats are highly advanced technologically and that some air defense equipment must be modified in line with the nature of emerging threats to ensure it can respond effectively.
​
He said the developments place responsibilities on the country’s scientific centers, knowledge-based companies and industrial sector to continuously upgrade Iran’s capabilities and prepare for a fully integrated defense.
​
Also in his remarks, the commander said that Iran’s air defense force is now at “100-percent and full readiness” to defend the country’s airspace.
​
He added that both the Army’s air defense forces and those of the Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) are fully prepared, while the country’s airspace is being continuously monitored along all borders.

📡 Exclusive | Iran-Russia relations gain momentum under President Pezeshkian: Ambassador Jalali

📌 Moscow, IRNA – Iranian Ambassador to Russia Kazem Jalali says relations between the two countries have gained significant momentum under President Masoud Pezeshkian, with the two sides pursuing a strategic partnership based on long-term interests.


Speaking to IRNA in Moscow on Wednesday in an exclusive interview, the ambassador said Iran’s approach toward Russia is strategic and independent of its relations with Western countries, rejecting perceptions that closer ties with Moscow are primarily linked to Tehran’s relations with the West.
​
According to the ambassador, Iran has always sought constructive relations with Western countries, but deception of the Western side undermined those relations.
​
Jalali emphasized that Iran considers Russia an important neighbor and major global power. Bilateral ties have grown under the strategic vision of the martyred Leader of the Islamic Revolution Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, whose deep and insightful approach to relations with neighbors, particularly Russia, has helped strengthen ties over the past two years, the ambassador noted.
​
He stressed that Iran’s foreign policy toward Russia, China, and Western countries is based on maintaining national independence and observing the principles of dignity, wisdom and prudence.
​
Sixth Pezeshkian-Putin meeting
​
The ambassador also said Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and his Russian counterpart Vladimir Putin would hold their sixth face-to-face meeting next week on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. The two presidents have also held six phone calls since Pezeshkian took office in August 2024.
​
Foreign ministers maintain close contact
​
Jalali said Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and his Russian counterpart Sergey Lavrov have held 12 in-person meetings and 17 phone conversations during the same period, with their latest meeting taking place two weeks ago in Bishkek.
​
Five pillars of economic cooperation
​
Jalali said five key pillars have driven the expansion of ties between Iran and Russia over the past two years: the signing and implementation of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty between the two sides, free trade with the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU), the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), financial and banking mechanisms, and energy cooperation.
​
He said the free trade agreement, effective since May 15, 2026, has eliminated tariffs on 87 percent of goods traded between Iran and the Eurasian Economic Union, creating new opportunities for Iranian exporters.
​
Jalali also highlighted the North-South Corridor, energy cooperation, gas projects, and nuclear power development as major areas for expanding economic relations.
​
The envoy said Iran and Russia are pursuing direct banking links and independent payment mechanisms to boost bilateral trade, adding that many financial and banking challenges are being resolved through ongoing talks.

💬 Pezeshkian stresses full coordination to preserve Iran’s economic stability

📌 Tehran, IRNA – President Masoud Pezeshkian has stressed the need for full coordination among Iran’s economic institutions to maintain economic stability and contain inflationary expectations amid mounting external pressures.


Pezeshkian made the remarks during a Wednesday visit to the Central Bank of Iran, where he attended a meeting with Economy Minister Ali Madanizadeh, Central Bank Governor Abdolnaser Hemmati, and a group of prominent economists.
​
The meeting reviewed the latest monetary, foreign exchange, and financial indicators and the Central Bank’s decision-making processes.
​
Economists also presented their views on addressing trade barriers, managing the country’s new economic conditions, fiscal and budgetary policies, monetary and foreign exchange policies, trade and economic relations, and management of enterprises.
​
Referring to the special circumstances arising from the US-Israeli war of aggression, economic blockade, and foreign pressures, Pezeshkian underscored the importance of maintaining economic stability and containing inflationary expectations.
​
“Management of key markets, particularly the foreign exchange and monetary markets, in such circumstances requires complete cohesion and synergy among the monetary policymaking institution, the government and other economic pillars of the country,” he said.
​
The meeting was held as part of the second periodic session between the Central Bank governor and economists, following the announcement of the US economic pressure campaign against Iran.
