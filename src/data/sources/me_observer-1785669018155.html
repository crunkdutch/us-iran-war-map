🛰The expansion of electronic warfare and disruption of satellite navigation systems is a common measure to reduce the accuracy of weapons, drones, and systems dependent on satellite positioning during military tensions.
📡If a missile or drone relies significantly on GPS for part of its trajectory and lacks effective compensation methods for disruption, its impact accuracy may decrease, and the Circular Error Probable (CEP) may increase under electronic warfare conditions. In contrast, systems that use a combination of inertial navigation, terrain contour matching, image matching, or other GPS-independent methods may be less affected.

⚡️ The expansion of disruption in satellite positioning systems (GPS) by the Zionist regime, which has extended to parts of Lebanon and Syria, indicates that electronic warfare has become a constant component of Israel's layered defense. In such an environment, any weapon system that relies on satellite navigation for part of its trajectory and does not possess effective compensatory methods may face reduced accuracy and an increased circular error probable (CEP). Therefore, electronic warfare can be one of the effective factors in reducing the accuracy of some attacks, although the extent of its impact depends on the guidance system architecture of each missile.

A significant difference is also observed in comparing impact patterns. In attacks over recent weeks on targets in eastern and central Jordan, published reports of impact locations showed higher accuracy compared to some attacks in the southern Jordan axis and the Aqaba area—adjacent to Eilat and an area where Israel's electronic warfare coverage is expected to be stronger. This difference is consistent with the hypothesis of the electronic warfare environment's impact on some guidance systems but is not sufficient on its own to prove the cause; factors such as missile type, flight profile, attack trajectory, guidance method, and operational conditions can also affect CEP.
