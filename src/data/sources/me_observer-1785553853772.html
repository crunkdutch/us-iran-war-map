⚡️ Anti-radiation missiles (ARM):
An anti-radiation missile is a type of missile designed to detect and fire at the source of enemy radio waves. These missiles are usually used to destroy enemy radars, but they can also be used against jamming sources and enemy radio communication systems.
Anti-radiation missiles are usually built as air-to-ground missiles and are used by specialized fighters to destroy enemy air defenses and thus ensure air superiority for friendly forces.
The AGM-45 Shrike was the first surface-to-air missile specifically built as an anti-radiation missile for the US Armed Forces. Its design began in 1958 to counter the new Soviet surface-to-air missiles known as SA-2, and its mass production began in 1963. The US Air Force and Navy used this missile in large numbers until it was replaced by the AGM-88 missile in the 1980s.

⚡️ Guidance and Navigation Systems:
The navigation system of anti-radar missiles is usually a combination of inertial guidance and satellite guidance in the initial phase, and a passive seeker in the secondary phase. Passive seekers are responsible for detecting enemy radar waves, and after enemy radar or radio waves are identified, they lock onto that target and destroy it.
Method of Operation: The anti-radar missile first moves towards the target using inertial guidance and satellite guidance. This means that the coordinates of the enemy's radar location or other targets are given to the missile in advance, and the missile moves towards the target using inertial guidance. When the missile approaches the target, the passive seeker turns on and begins searching for radar and radio waves, and as soon as it detects the waves, it discovers the precise coordinates of the target, locks onto it, and moves towards the target. Of course, in new generation missiles, the missile can also destroy the target using satellite and inertial guidance without locking onto the target, but it usually has less accuracy than a passive seeker.
In some anti-radar missiles, due to the short range of the passive seeker, the fighter jet itself also used a radar wave detection pod to compensate for the seeker's range deficiency.
If the enemy radar is turned off in the final moments, the missile moves towards the last received coordinates.

⚡️ Older anti-radiation missiles lacked inertial and satellite guidance, and the missile relied solely on its passive seeker to destroy its target. Therefore, the fighter had to approach the radar and then fire, which posed a risk of the fighter being destroyed.
However, in new anti-radiation missiles, enemy fighters can fire from longer distances using inertial guidance, eliminating the need to approach the target and reducing the risk of fighter destruction.
Anti-radiation missiles are not only used to destroy radars but also against communication systems, jamming sources, and any source that emits radio waves. Anti-radiation missiles are usually solid-fueled and use rocket engines.
