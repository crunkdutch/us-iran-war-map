⚡️ 🔴Nasser Eddine: The Ministry of Public Health will continue to provide all available resources to enhance health services.
The Minister of Public Health, Dr. Rakan Nasser Eddine, inaugurated the new emergency department and primary healthcare center at Tripoli Governmental Hospital, in a step aimed at strengthening the health sector in northern Lebanon and raising the hospital's readiness to provide more efficient and sustainable medical services.
Nasser Eddine affirmed that the opening of the center sends a message of hope despite the difficult circumstances Lebanon is experiencing, pointing out that the Ministry initiated support for the health sector and worked to secure the necessary funding through the World Bank and the Islamic Bank at a cost of two million dollars.
He stressed that Tripoli deserves all support, affirming that the Ministry of Public Health covers 100 percent of patients' treatment at the hospital, and that health will remain outside any political polarization because it is a right for every citizen, especially the most vulnerable groups. He added that the Ministry will continue to provide all available resources to enhance health services, pointing to efforts to increase the Ministry of Public Health's budget to ensure the preservation of the human right to access healthcare.
The inauguration ceremony began with a speech by the Director-General of Tripoli Governmental Hospital, Fawaz Al-Hallab, who thanked the Minister of Public Health for his support to the hospital, equipping it, and enhancing its capabilities, which contributes to improving services provided to thousands of patients in the city and the North.
In turn, the French Ambassador to Lebanon, Hervé Magro, affirmed France's continued support for Lebanon in various fields, noting that Tripoli receives special attention due to its humanitarian and developmental importance.
For her part, the Head of the International Committee of the Red Cross mission in Lebanon, Ignace Dur, presented the importance of the new project, affirming that the emergency department and primary healthcare center will contribute to improving access to medical services, especially for residents of popular areas and the most vulnerable groups.

⚡️ 🚫Post 3 | Head Injury; The Signature Injury of the Iran War?
⚠️ At least 170 cases of head injury among American forces
⏺The most common injury recorded in U.S. Department of Defense documents is not shrapnel wounds or bone fractures, but head injury. From the beginning of the war until early April, at least 170 cases of head injury have been reported; an issue that has increased the concern of military medical specialists about blast-induced traumatic brain injury becoming the "signature injury of the Iran War."
⏺A significant portion of these injuries is likely related to the blast waves from missiles and drones. Even so-called "mild" brain injury may not be visible in initial imaging but may manifest weeks or months later as persistent headaches, memory impairment, vision problems, imbalance, speech disorders, or reduced cognitive ability.
🚫The U.S. Department of Defense was previously forced to gradually increase the number of those suffering from brain injuries after Iran's 2020 missile attack on Ain al-Asad. Now, the widespread nature of the attacks and the recording of at least 170 head injuries indicate that physical protection alone has not prevented the effects of blast waves on forces stationed at bases.
