🎙 Iranian envoy: Keeping dialogue open, pursuing national interests key diplomatic achievement

📌 London, IRNA – Iran’s Ambassador to Norway Alireza Jahangiri has said that keeping channels of dialogue open and actively pursuing the country’s interests in a complicated and rapidly changing environment have been the most important achievements of the administration of President Masoud Pezeshkian over the past year.


The ambassador made the remarks in an exclusive interview with IRNA, published on Tuesday, on the occasion of Government Week, which began a day earlier.
​
“Briefly speaking about the foreign policy performance over the past year, the most important achievement has been keeping the path of dialogue open and actively pursuing the interests of the Islamic Republic of Iran under very complicated and rapidly changing conditions,” he said.
​
On his embassy’s contribution, Jahangiri said the mission pursued the country’s positions while preventing political differences from undermining cooperation opportunities at a time when Iran’s relations with European countries were affected by developments in West Asia and beyond.
​
“Iran’s relations with European countries, including Norway, have been affected during this period by regional and international developments, the continuation of sanctions, and the political and media environment surrounding Iran. In such circumstances, one of the embassy’s main responsibilities has been, on the one hand, to clearly and firmly pursue the country’s positions and interests and, on the other, to ensure that political differences do not lead to the closure of channels of dialogue or the loss of opportunities for cooperation,” the ambassador said.
​
In this context, the embassy has maintained consultations with the Iranian Foreign Ministry in Tehran and relevant institutions in Norway, he said, citing as examples a visit to Iran by a Norwegian Foreign Ministry delegation, during which issues of mutual interest were discussed.
​
He also pointed to Norway’s invitation to Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi to participate in the Oslo Forum 2026, saying the move demonstrated the two countries’ determination to maintain consultations and dialogue at the highest level.
​
“In diplomacy, particularly during difficult periods, achievements are not always measured by big numbers or headline-making developments. At times, maintaining an effective channel of dialogue, preventing misunderstandings from taking shape, and preserving opportunities for future cooperation are themselves important elements of safeguarding national interests,” the envoy said.
​
He added that the Iranian embassy in Norway had also sought to keep economic channels open despite restrictions caused by sanctions and banking problems.
​
Asked about the views of Norway’s political circles on Iran’s regional position following developments in recent months that showed lasting security in the region is unattainable without Iran playing its role, Jahangiri said Norway’s political, academic and media circles have differing perceptions of Iran and its regional policies, with some holding critical views.
​
“However, one reality has received greater attention than before: Iran is one of the region’s key and influential players, and no sustainable plan for security in the Middle East can afford to ignore this reality,” he said.
​
Jahangiri pointed to Iran’s geographical position, natural and human resources, economic capacities and historical ties with neighboring areas, saying these factors had linked Iran closely to developments in the Persian Gulf, the Sea of Oman, the Caucasus, Central Asia and West Asia.

📄 Pezeshkian pledges full support for domestic solar panel producers

📌 Tehran, IRNA – President Masoud Pezeshkian has pledged full support for investors and domestic manufacturers of solar panels, saying there will be no restrictions on backing investment in the sector.


Pezeshkian made the remarks on Tuesday during a ceremony to inaugurate two solar power plants with a combined capacity of 95 megawatts in Tehran Province.
​
Thanking domestic and foreign engineers and contractors involved in solar panel projects, the president said the measures taken to expand the use of clean and renewable energy could be further developed and lead to new records.
​
He said the administration expects to generate 30,000 megawatts of electricity from clean and renewable sources within the next two years.
​
He further said electricity supplies to industrial units should never be cut off, emphasizing the importance of reliable power for production.
​
Pezeshkian also stressed the need to improve the efficient use of available space and resources alongside the development of renewable energy, calling for coordination with university scholars and agricultural experts to explore growing medicinal plants alongside solar power plants and making use of the water used to wash solar panels.
​
Attending the ceremony, Energy Minister Abbas Aliabadi said President Pezeshkian had simultaneously ordered the launch of 600 megawatts of solar-generated electricity across the country. The minister said the progress made in the sector was the result of the president’s guidance and recommendations.
​
Central Bank Governor Abdolnaser Hemmati also said $1.7 billion has so far been allocated to advance solar energy technology, stressing that there were no restrictions on providing foreign or national currencies to support domestic solar panel manufacturers.
​
Tehran Governor Mohammad-Sadeq Motamedian said the president’s emphasis on expanding solar power and other new energy sources had encouraged greater public participation and investment. He said that before Pezeshkian’s administration took office two years ago, Tehran had 23 megawatts of solar power capacity, while the figure has now reached 400 megawatts, representing a 20-fold increase.

📄 WWII-era Allied occupation remains etched in Iranian memory: Foreign Ministry spokesperson

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei has mentioned the 85th anniversary of the Allied invasion and occupation of Iran, saying the painful experience remains deeply rooted in the collective memory of the Iranian people.


In a post on X marking the anniversary of the invasion of Iran on August 24, 1941, Baqaei recalled on Tuesday that Iran was occupied despite its official declaration of neutrality during World War II. Soviet forces entered from the north and east, while British forces attacked from the south and west by land and air.
​
He said the Iranian army collapsed within days, major cities were occupied and the entire country came under foreign military control. Reza Shah Pahlavi abdicated 22 days after the invasion and was subsequently exiled to Mauritius, then a British-controlled island.
​
Despite the Shah’s government’s formal declaration of neutrality—which Mohammad Reza Pahlavi later, in a speech on November 5, 1974, apologetically described as the illusion of a “golden cage of neutrality” in “a world governed by the logic of force,” he said.
​
Baqaei said the occupation, which lasted six years, inflicted extensive damage and suffering on Iran. Beyond the destruction, loss of life and looting of Iranian assets, he said, the deepest wound was the humiliation inflicted on a nation that had no role in the wars imposed on the world by European powers.
​
The painful experience will never be erased from the memory of Iranians, Baqaei said, stressing that the historical episode continues to shape Iran’s understanding of national independence and security.
​
He argued that Iran, having overcome major crises with resilience and national resolve, is once again facing a decisive test.
​
According to Baghaei, protecting Iran’s national dignity and independence requires self-confidence, determination, and firm commitment to resistance.
​
He further maintained that national strength, in all its dimensions, remains the only effective deterrent against foreign demands and aggression.
