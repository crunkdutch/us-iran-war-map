🇾🇪 | Yemen War Update: September 10, 2026 | Complete Battlefield Timeline:

— Saudi escalation across Yemen

Saudi Arabia launched a major escalation across Yemen as drones struck the Kitaf and Al-Buqa districts in Saada Governorate in northern Yemen. At the same time, Saudi warplanes carried out a large wave of airstrikes across the country. According to Al Masirah, Saudi aircraft launched at least 70 airstrikes across six Yemeni provinces during recent hours, targeting Taiz, Al-Jawf, Marib, Al-Hudaydah, Saada, and Al-Bayda. Saudi fighter jets also bombed the surroundings of Jabal Al-Nar, east of the strategic coastal city of Al-Mokha, in an attempt to stop the advance of the Yemeni Armed Forces.

— The Yemeni Armed Forces begin a major western coast offensive

As Saudi airstrikes intensified, the Yemeni Armed Forces opened a major offensive along the western coast. Ballistic missiles, drones, artillery, and rocket barrages targeted Saudi-backed PLC troop concentrations around Al-Mokha, while simultaneous ground assaults advanced through western Taiz and southern Al-Hudaydah. The missile and drone strikes were directed at troop gatherings, military camps, defensive positions, and reinforcement routes around Al-Mokha as the battle expanded along the coast.

— Camp Khalid bin Walid falls

One of the first major breakthroughs came with the capture of Camp Khalid bin Walid, a strategic military base on the western coast. Following its capture, AnsarAllah forces continued advancing westward toward Jabal Al-Nar, opening another route toward Al-Mokha from the east. The capture of Camp Khalid was accompanied by the withdrawal of Saudi-backed PLC positions surrounding the base.

— The collapse of the Hays front

The western coast front rapidly collapsed after Saudi-backed PLC forces withdrew from Hays. AnsarAllah entered the city following the withdrawal, with footage showing fighters arriving in military vehicles before taking positions across the city. Images later emerged showing AnsarAllah fighters standing in front of the Security Directorate building in Hays, confirming complete control over the city. The Governor of Al-Hudaydah later confirmed the complete liberation of Hays district. This marked one of the major urban gains of the western coast offensive.

— Tariq Affash orders a withdrawal

As the Hays front collapsed, Tariq Affash released an audio recording addressing his forces. In the recording, he instructed his forces to withdraw from Hays and Al-Khawkhah toward Al-Mokha. He also spoke about what he described as betrayal by Saudi Arabia and other coalition factions, saying coalition partners had abandoned forces fighting on the western coast. The audio circulated as withdrawals accelerated across the front.

— Mass withdrawal across the Hays axis

The withdrawal expanded far beyond Hays. Saudi-backed PLC forces withdrew from the entire Hays direction on the western coast, abandoning a long list of military positions and towns. The withdrawal included Al-Maqbanah Heights, Hays, Port of Al-Haymah, Al-Khawkhah, Abu Musa Military Camp, Southern Shar’ab Heights, Nakhlah, Al-Mafraq, Khalid bin Walid Military Camp, Al-Matahif, and Jabal Al-Nar. Battlefield maps released afterward showed the entire withdrawn zone becoming unmarked and expected to come under AnsarAllah control in the following hours.

— AnsarAllah liberates Al-Khawkhah

Immediately after securing Hays, AnsarAllah continued advancing south along the coast. AnsarAllah entered Al-Khawkhah after Saudi-backed PLC forces abandoned the city. Media later emerged showing AnsarAllah fighters at the entrance of Al-Khawkhah, confirming the city’s liberation. The Governor of Al-Hudaydah officially confirmed the complete liberation of both Hays and Al-Khawkhah. Al-Khawkhah had been one of the last major coastal positions on the road toward Al-Mokha under Saudi-backed control.

— Al-Mafraq and Jabal Al-Nar are freed and captured

Following the withdrawal from Al-Khawkhah, AnsarAllah continued advancing inland. Saudi-backed PLC forces withdrew from Al-Mafraq and Jabal Al-Nar, with PLC-linked sources confirming the withdrawals. AnsarAllah then secured both locations, giving Yemeni forces control over the mountain approaches east of Al-Mokha. The capture of Jabal Al-Nar opened another axis toward the strategic port city.

— Shuqayrah becomes the next objective

The offensive expanded into Al-Wazi’iyah District, where the Yemeni Armed Forces captured Shuqayrah, the administrative center of the district. The advance extended Yemeni control over western Taiz while shortening the distance to Al-Mokha from inland positions.

— Saudi airstrikes attempt to stop the advance

As AnsarAllah advanced toward Al-Mokha, Saudi fighter jets intensified their air operations. Repeated airstrikes targeted the surroundings of Jabal Al-Nar, east of Al-Mokha, specifically aiming to slow the Yemeni ground advance through the mountain corridor leading to the city. Additional Saudi strikes continued across Taiz, Al-Hudaydah, Marib, Al-Jawf, Saada, and Al-Bayda during the same period.

— The battle of Al-Mokha begins

With Hays, Al-Khawkhah, Camp Khalid, and Jabal Al-Nar captured, the Yemeni Armed Forces launched the next stage of the offensive, opening the battle for Al-Mokha. Ground forces advanced simultaneously from two directions, from Jabal Al-Nar toward eastern Al-Mokha and from Yakhtal toward northern Al-Mokha. Missile, artillery, rocket, and drone attacks intensified against Saudi-backed PLC positions inside Al-Mokha and the surrounding areas. The city came under continuous bombardment as Yemeni forces attempted to weaken defensive positions before entering.

— Ballistic missile and drone strikes on Al-Mokha

The Yemeni Armed Forces launched heavy ballistic missile salvos and drone attacks against Saudi-backed troop concentrations around Al-Mokha. Continuous ballistic missile strikes, drone attacks, artillery bombardments, and rocket strikes targeted military gatherings and defensive positions around the city. The bombardment focused on Al-Mokha and its surrounding countryside as Yemeni ground forces advanced.

— Yakhtal comes under assault

The next battlefield objective became Yakhtal, just north of Al-Mokha. AnsarAllah forces entered the area after advancing along the western coast. Clashes erupted on the eastern outskirts of Yakhtal while Yemeni forces simultaneously approached Al-Mokha from the direction of Jabal Al-Nar. Later battlefield updates confirmed that AnsarAllah had captured Yakhtal and continued advancing toward central Al-Mokha. Reports stated that Yemeni forces had entered the town as Saudi-backed forces withdrew and abandoned their positions.

— Al-Mokha comes under pressure from two directions

Following the advance into Yakhtal, Yemeni forces approached Al-Mokha simultaneously from Yakhtal in the north and Jabal Al-Nar in the east. Reports stated that AnsarAllah forces had taken positions around Al-Mokha for approximately 90 minutes, while fighting continued around the city’s outskirts. The offensive was aimed at isolating Al-Mokha before entering the city center.

— Panic and withdrawals inside Al-Mokha

Reports described widespread panic among Saudi-backed PLC fighters inside Al-Mokha. Large numbers of forces reportedly withdrew toward Aden as AnsarAllah approached, while other reports stated that some fighters abandoned military equipment and heavy weapons during their retreat. Videos reportedly showed Saudi-backed fighters fleeing Al-Khawkhah while leaving heavy weapons along roadsides after Yemeni forces entered the town.

— Mahdi Al-Mashat announces a general amnesty

Mahdi Al-Mashat, a member of AnsarAllah’s Political Bureau, announced a general amnesty for Yemenis who had been deceived into joining Saudi-backed forces. He said anyone who laid down their weapons and returned to the homeland would be covered by the presidential amnesty. The announcement was directed at fighters serving with Saudi-backed militias across the western coast.

— Al-Hudaydah Governorate begins implementing the amnesty

Following Mahdi Al-Mashat’s announcement, Al-Hudaydah Governorate announced the start of practical measures to implement the conditional presidential amnesty. Local authorities called on Saudi-backed fighters on the western coast to lay down their weapons, leave Saudi-backed fronts, and return safely to their families without fear of arrest or punishment. Officials said the measure was intended to prevent further bloodshed, facilitate the return of fighters, and strengthen security and social stability.

— Leadership collapse inside Saudi-backed forces

The battlefield losses triggered resignations. Youssef Al-Sharaji, commander of Saudi-backed mercenary forces on the Taiz front, submitted his resignation, citing heavy battlefield losses, the loss of military positions, poor coordination, and betrayal from within the ranks. The resignation came as western coast positions continued collapsing.

— Internal accusations inside the coalition

Reports described growing disputes between Saudi-backed and UAE-backed factions. Saudi-backed groups accused UAE-backed formations of abandoning positions, while UAE-backed groups accused Saudi-backed forces of collapsing the front. The accusations emerged after rapid territorial losses on the western coast.

— Hamdi Shukri intercepts retreating fighters

Reports stated that Hamdi Shukri, commander of the Second Brigade of Saudi-backed mercenary forces, began intercepting retreating fighters. According to the reports, he confiscated weapons, vehicles, and equipment allegedly taken during the withdrawal from frontline positions. The move reflected growing disorder inside the retreating forces.

— Statement from Hizam Al-Assad

AnsarAllah Political Bureau member Hizam Al-Assad issued a statement following the western coast advances. He declared, “Our western coasts are a Yemeni republic, not a Saudi monarchy.” The statement came as AnsarAllah consolidated control over newly captured coastal territory.

— Saudi-backed tribes isolate a commander in Al-Jawf

Reports from Al-Jawf said Saudi-backed tribes isolated one of their commanders after AnsarAllah advances in the province. Initial reports suggested he had been captured, although there was no definitive confirmation of his capture. An audio statement attributed to the commander denied the reports and described the tribes that isolated him as “terrorist gangs.”

— Saudi Arabia expands its air campaign

Saudi fighter jets intensified strikes throughout western Yemen during the offensive. Additional strikes targeted Jabal Al-Nar east of Al-Mokha and other western Taiz and Al-Hudaydah fronts. Saudi aircraft continued attempting to slow AnsarAllah’s advance toward the Red Sea coast, while strikes were also reported across six Yemeni provinces.

— Al-Mokha becomes the central battlefield

By the latest battlefield updates, Al-Mokha had become the main focus of the offensive. AnsarAllah forces were advancing toward the city from Yakhtal and Jabal Al-Nar, while the Yemeni Armed Forces continued launching missile, drone, artillery, and rocket attacks against Saudi-backed positions. Large explosions were reported inside Al-Mokha as the battle intensified.

— Latest battlefield situation

Saudi-backed PLC forces have withdrawn from a wide area stretching across the Hays axis toward Al-Mokha. The withdrawal includes multiple military camps, heights, towns, and positions that previously formed the main defensive belt protecting the approaches to the strategic coastal city.

The battle for Al-Mokha is now underway, with AnsarAllah advancing from both the Jabal Al-Nar and Yakhtal directions. Saudi-backed forces remain inside parts of the city, while other formations are reportedly withdrawing toward Aden. Yemeni ballistic missile, drone, artillery, and rocket attacks continue targeting Saudi-backed positions in Al-Mokha and its surrounding countryside, while Saudi aircraft continue striking the approaches to the city in an attempt to halt the advance.

The western coast offensive has therefore moved from the liberation of Hays and Al-Khawkhah to the direct battle for Al-Mokha, the strategic Red Sea port that remains the principal objective of the current Yemeni advance.
