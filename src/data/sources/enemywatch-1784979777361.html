🇮🇷| An Iranian F-5 pilot involved in the March 1 strike on Camp Buehring in Kuwait revealed that the mission was flown at an exceptionally low altitude of less than 50 feet, far below the standard training altitude of approximately 500 feet, to evade enemy air defense systems.

According to the pilot, the crew was fully aware of the Patriot missile batteries, layered air defense networks, AWACS surveillance aircraft, and Kuwaiti F-18 fighter patrols protecting the area. To avoid detection, the mission was conducted under complete radio silence.

The pilot stated that the aircraft flew so low over the Persian Gulf that it passed between two ships whose decks were higher than the jet's flight path.

After entering Kuwaiti airspace, the aircraft encountered dense infrastructure, including power lines, oil refineries, and military installations. However, the crew deliberately avoided all secondary targets and proceeded directly toward Camp Buehring.

Maintaining high speed and an ultra low altitude throughout the approach, the aircraft successfully reached the base and carried out its bombing mission before exiting the area.
