📣 | Regional updates | 24 August 2026

🇮🇶 Iraq

• The unrest currently unfolding in Erbil Province is reportedly linked to a dispute between the leader of the Herki tribe in the Khabat district and the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP). According to local reports, the tribal leader was previously affiliated with a prominent Barzani-led party before leaving it over internal disagreements and joining another political movement. He was later arrested, prompting members of the Herki tribe to issue an ultimatum demanding his release and threatening armed action if their demands were not met.

• The Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) announced that they destroyed an ISIS hideout along with a warehouse containing oil derivatives during an operation west of Ramadi.

• Iraq’s National Security Adviser also revealed that Baghdad has presented a proposal to Iran and Saudi Arabia to establish a unified coordinating security council aimed at strengthening regional security cooperation.

🇮🇷 Iran

• Secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei, warned that if the economic war against Iran continues, not a single drop of oil will be exported through the Strait of Hormuz or anywhere in the Persian Gulf. He added that Iran will regard any participation in or support for the American economic war against the Iranian people as an act of war.

• IRGC targeted a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz as maritime tensions continue to escalate in the Gulf.

• A report published by The Independent reported that Iran’s attacks on a major U.S. naval facility in Bahrain severely damaged the base and disrupted logistical support for the USS Abraham Lincoln. According to the report, damage to the U.S. Navy’s Fifth Fleet support facility forced supplies for the aircraft carrier to be transported from a location approximately 2,200 miles away, contributing to shortages and deteriorating conditions aboard the vessel.

🇾🇪 Yemen

• A Yemeni military source told Saba News Agency that Yemeni air defenses shot down a Saudi ScanEagle reconnaissance drone after it allegedly entered Yemeni airspace in Hajjah Governorate.

• Separately, an explosion was reported in the southern Yemeni city of Aden, although no official information has been released regarding casualties or the cause of the blast.

🇱🇧 Lebanon

Local Lebanese sources reported that Israeli forces carried out 44 strikes across southern Lebanon within the past 24 hours.

The reported attacks targeted Al-Mansouri, Ali al-Taher, Mayfadoun, Kafr Rumman, Baraachit, Mays al-Jabal, Hadatha, Qantara, and Srobbine, and included airstrikes, artillery shelling, and attacks on multiple villages across the border region.

🇮🇳/🇮🇷 | India has bought an average of 540,000 barrels of Iranian oil per day over the past month: Planning chief:

Head of Iran’s Plan and Budget Organization Davoud Manzoor said India has purchased an average of 540,000 barrels of Iranian oil per day over the past month, arguing that the situation is not as bleak as some portray it.

Manzoor said the fact that no new oil cargoes have been loaded in recent weeks does not mean that Iran’s oil exports have stopped. He added that Iran has enough oil available for sale for at least the next two months.

Responding to concerns that the government would be unable to collect taxes, Manzoor said, “When have we ever collected taxes during the first two or three months of the fiscal year? Tax collection always begins from the fourth or fifth month, in the second half of the year.”

🇸🇦 | Saudi oil tankers reroute around Africa amid disruptions:

At least nine Saudi Arabian very large crude carriers (VLCCs) are currently sailing around Africa to load crude at Sidi Kerir in northern Egypt or through ship-to-ship transfers in the eastern Mediterranean. Five of these oil tankers, which had returned from East and South Asia, decided 23 days ago to take the route around Africa due to Yemen’s blockade against Saudi Arabia. However, they have yet to reach Gibraltar. Four other oil tankers arrived in the Sea of Oman 10 days ago to receive oil from the Strait of Hormuz through ship-to-ship transfers. However, due to the slow and limited flow of this process, they have now changed course and opted for the much longer route around Africa to reach their cargo.
