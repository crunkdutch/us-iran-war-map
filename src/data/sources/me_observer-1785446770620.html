⚡️ ⚠️The War for Knowledge: Why "Seeing" Is Not Equivalent to "Having a Target." Attention, reconnaissance personnel!
⏺In current US doctrine, knowledge is not a "ready-made entity"; it must be fought for. The enemy also has drones, satellites, eavesdropping, deception, and bait, and can target sensors, communication nodes, or analysis teams. Therefore, reconnaissance without counter-reconnaissance is incomplete. The US unit tries to make first contact with a sensor, an unmanned system, or the smallest possible element so that the main body of the force is revealed later; simultaneously, through camouflage, emission control, deception, and movement, it tries to make enemy knowledge late, incomplete, or wrong.
⏺The main problem begins after detection. A drone image or raw coordinates are not yet a "target." The staff must examine its identity, importance, validity period, possibility of deception, legal consequences, and its relation to the commander's decision. Then it must select the appropriate effector; and the best effector is not necessarily the longest-range or most expensive weapon. Jamming, deception, artillery, loitering munitions, air attack, or even ground maneuver might produce a better result. The criterion is the required effect at the necessary time with acceptable consumption and risk.
⏺The D3A method—Decide, Detect, Deliver, and Assess—is used to prevent targeting from becoming a "firing list." In the decision phase, the commander clarifies which enemy function must be disrupted, by when, and by what criteria. In the detect phase, the sensor is connected to that need. Delivery is not just firing; any action that changes enemy behavior or capacity is part of the effect. Assessment should also not be limited to munitions impact; the question is whether the enemy system is still capable of performing its intended mission.
⏺This distinction is vital. The destruction of a few artillery pieces may be of little value if their command network, sensors, and supply remain intact and quickly introduce replacements. Conversely, a short disruption in a command link or deception of reserves can create a temporal gap for maneuver without widespread destruction. Consequently, successful targeting is not about the number of destroyed targets; it is about the reduction of enemy options and the increase of the maneuver commander's options.
An analytical point for our dear commanders to whom we report: The real cycle is this: clear decision need ← multi-source detection and confirmation ← selection of appropriate effect ← assessment of enemy function ← exploitation of maneuver. Wherever this link is broken, abundant data and munitions can be expended without operational results...

⚡️ 🚫From Isolation to Breakthrough: Combined Arms Maneuver in the Transparent Battlefield❗️
⏺In the American understanding, deep fire can disrupt the enemy system and wear down its forces, but it does not seize ground alone or impose political imperatives. Lasting results are achieved when fire, disruption, and deception are linked to close maneuver. For this reason, the attack pattern still revolves around identification, isolation, gap creation, penetration, gap expansion, and exploitation; with the difference that drones, electronic warfare, and loitering munitions have now entered this sequence from lower echelons.
⏺The cycle that has been highlighted in the new experience of armored brigades is "isolation-exploitation-reconstitution." In the isolation phase, unmanned sensors, long-range fires, disruption, and deception separate the target area from reserves, command, and support. In exploitation, armor, infantry, engineers, and direct fire enter for a short time to turn the gap into physical defeat. In reconstitution, sensors, communications, ammunition, casualty evacuation, air defense, and command are brought forward again to prepare the unit for the next round.
⏺Dispersion and the transparent battlefield have not diminished the role of combined arms; they have made their coordination more critical. Engineering without fire cover is established in the breach; armor without infantry is vulnerable in confined terrain and against small teams; infantry without fire and mobility is worn down against enemy mass; and artillery that does not displace after firing becomes a target in the counter-fire cycle. The main issue is creating several simultaneous dilemmas so that the enemy cannot fully respond to all of them.
🚫The Transformation in Contact program and the NTC rotations in 2025 and 2026 show that the army wants to test small drones, loitering munitions, robotics, and organizational changes in contact with opposing forces. The value of this program lies in "learning under pressure," not merely the presence of new equipment. The correct criterion is whether technology has shortened decision time, reduced human exposure, increased survivability, and sustained the tempo of maneuver. If it only increases the volume of video, operators, and electronic signatures, the advantage will be merely demonstrative.
In any case, we must understand that in today's model, the main force makes contact as much as possible with sensors and small elements; isolates the target area; creates combat mass for a short time; exploits with close maneuver; then disperses and reconstitutes. Victory comes from repeating this cycle, not from a single breakthrough.

⚡️ 🚫Command under disruption; the network is a tool, naturally not an absolute prerequisite! Attention, IT personnel!
⏺The American model relies on the network, but the new doctrine accepts that full and permanent connectivity is not realistic in peer-to-peer warfare. Large command nodes, antenna farms, data traffic, and long stops create a signature that can be detected by interception, drones, and precision fire. Therefore, command posts must become smaller, more dispersed, and more mobile, and be able to switch between states of full connection, limited connection, and disconnection.
Here, mission-command transforms from a cultural slogan (a 50-kilometer prayer station) into a survival mechanism. The higher echelon must clarify "the mission's why, priority, limits of authority, and desired state"; the lower echelon must be able to adapt the how of execution to actual contact in the absence of complete data. If every objective or movement depends on multi-echelon approval, the network, instead of increasing speed, reproduces decision centralization. Decentralized command is also not built only by a crisis-day order; authority must be genuinely delegated and practiced in daily training.
🚫The CALL report from JRTC in 2026 provides important training evidence: fixed positions and undisciplined emissions were detected and targeted faster; in contrast, dispersion, movement, cover and concealment, emission control, and deception caused the opposing force's sensors and fire to expend time and ammunition. The important point is that survival is not just a product of the defensive system; it is the result of behavioral rhythm. A unit that only moves after detection has effectively decided too late.
⏺You should know that "Next-Generation Command and Control" or the fancy name NGC2 wants to distribute sensor and echelon data on a common baseline, but its real test is not the day of connection. The professional criterion is how much functionality the system maintains with incomplete data, information contamination, bandwidth limitations, loss of a node, and time pressure. Paper maps, short reports, pre-agreed timing, and different communication paths are still important. True redundancy means the alternative path is technically and organizationally different and has been used before; multiple radios with common dependency do not create true resilience.
Our combat IT must understand that the central paradox is this: the network makes decisions distributable, but the organization can use the same network for more detailed control. Command superiority is sustained when the unit understands the intent with less data, makes local decisions, moves, and continues the essential mission.
