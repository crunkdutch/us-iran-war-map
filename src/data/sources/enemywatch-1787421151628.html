🇮🇷 | In a recently released film by the Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting (IRIB) on behalf of Iran's Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics, members of the Shahed family of drones were displayed alongside other products from the ministry's defense industries in a segment highlighting Iran's drone industry achievements.

This presentation could give the impression that the Shahed-136 is a product directly designed and manufactured by the Ministry of Defense. However, at least regarding the drone's origin, design, and development, that impression is not consistent with the available evidence.

According to specialized sources, the Shahed-136 is primarily attributed to Shahed Aviation Industries, which is recognized as the designer and developer of the Shahed drone family. The Royal United Services Institute (RUSI) also explicitly notes in its technical assessment that the common designation "HESA Shahed-136" should not automatically be interpreted as identifying its designer. RUSI distinguishes between the organization responsible for developing the system and the company involved in its industrial manufacturing.

Iranian sources similarly describe the Shahed-136 as a design of Shahed Aviation Industries under the IRGC Aerospace Force, while also referring to it as a product manufactured within Iran's aerospace industry.

Therefore, when discussing project ownership, design origin, and operational development, attributing the Shahed-136 to the IRGC Aerospace Force's Shahed Aviation Industries is the more accurate characterization. Simply displaying the drone in a Ministry of Defense promotional film does not establish that ministry as its primary designer.

The key distinction is between design and mass production. Shahed Aviation Industries, operating within the drone structure of the IRGC Aerospace Force, is widely recognized as the entity responsible for the design and development of the Shahed drone family. HESA (Iran Aircraft Manufacturing Industries Company), on the other hand, is understood to play a role in the industrial production chain and large-scale manufacturing of drones and other aerospace systems.

This distinction also appears in foreign assessments. The RUSI report identifies Shahed Aviation Industries as the organization linked to the drone's design and development, while more recent research likewise describes the Shahed-136 as a system developed by Shahed Aviation Industries.

A more precise description, therefore, is that the Shahed-136 is a product of Iran's broader drone ecosystem: its design and development are attributed to Shahed Aviation Industries under the IRGC Aerospace Force, while industrial production is carried out through a broader manufacturing network in which HESA has also played a role.

Ukrainian sources have repeatedly described the Shahed-136 as an Iranian-made drone and have stated that its design, production, and procurement of some components are linked to the IRGC Aerospace Force.

Our intention is simply to present the distinction as it is understood from the available evidence: separating the roles of design, development, and industrial production, rather than treating them as the same thing.
