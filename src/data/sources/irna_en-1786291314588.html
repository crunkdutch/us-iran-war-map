🔊 Pezeshkian stresses resilience and regional cooperation

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has said his administration is expanding regional cooperation, strengthening domestic infrastructure, and accelerating railway development to counter the impact of sanctions while maintaining economic stability and public services.


Speaking about Iran's regional policy, President Pezeshkian said closer ties with neighboring countries have helped expand trade despite financial restrictions. He noted that while banking limitations have made money transfers difficult, barter trade has become one of the alternatives for maintaining commercial exchanges.
​
In an interview with the IRIB at the beginning of the third year of his tenure, President Pezeshkian also praised neighboring countries for their security cooperation, saying they prevented attempts by hostile groups to enter Iran from border regions and create unrest during recent periods of conflict and tension. He described this cooperation as an important achievement made possible through regional coordination and national unity.
​
President Pezeshkian said building unity and cooperation both with neighboring countries and within Iran was neither easy nor accidental, but had played a crucial role in frustrating plans aimed at destabilizing the country. ​
Whenever officials faced difficulties, they turned to the martyred Leader, and he supported them with all his strength, Pezeshkian said. He added that Iran's resilience was the result of those policies, strategic direction, and guidance, and said the current Leader continues to encourage national unity and cohesion, helping the country address its challenges.
​
Addressing the impact of sanctions, President Pezeshkian said that restrictions on oil exports have reduced Iran's foreign currency earnings but stressed that the government is working with economists, academics, business professionals, chambers of commerce, and industry representatives to minimize economic challenges.
​
The officials have not solved every problem, but they are working together to reduce the impact of challenges, he said, adding that many foreign observers had expected Iran's economy to collapse under mounting pressure but had been surprised by its resilience.
​
The president said public cooperation has also played a key role in managing energy demand. He noted that nearly 7,000 megawatts of solar power capacity has already been added to the national grid, with further expansion underway. Despite damage to power plants and disruptions affecting approximately 230 million cubic meters of daily gas production, Iran has largely avoided widespread electricity outages, he said, adding that the government aims to prevent power cuts for industries and ensure stable energy supplies during the coming winter.

🔊 More than 1 million Arbaeen pilgrims return to Iran via Khuzestan borders

📌 Ahvaz, IRNA – More than 1 million Arbaeen pilgrims have re-entered Iran through the southwestern province of Khuzestan during this year’s pilgrimage period, the head of the province's Migration and Passport Police said on Friday.


Colonel Asghar Kolivand said 1,004,186 pilgrims had entered the country through Khuzestan’s two main border crossings with Iraq. Of those, 805,236 crossed via Shalamcheh and 198,950 through Chazzabeh.
​

​
He said 953,427 pilgrims exited through Shalamcheh, while 249,563 crossed via Chazzabeh.
​
Shalamcheh and Chazzabeh, both located in Khuzestan province, are among the busiest official border crossings between Iran and Iraq and serve as key transit points for millions of pilgrims travelling to the Arbaeen commemorations each year.
