💬 Iran on path of active deterrence: A new defense doctrine with new tools

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The unveiling of the Qasem Basir ballistic missile marks a turning point in Iran's defense strategy; it is a missile that not only boasts greater range and precision but also symbolizes a paradigm shift: the transition from reactive to active deterrence.


Iran now declares that it will take action against any threat—even before it materializes. The solid-fueled Qasem Basir—featuring electro-optical guidance and a half-ton warhead—is the instrument of this new doctrine; a tool that sends a clear message to adversaries: Iran no longer merely responds, but acts preemptively.
​
Developments in recent months have revealed signs of a shift in how Iran addresses security and military threats—a shift that, when viewed alongside recent statements by the Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council Mohsen Rezaei and the ongoing expansion of the country’s missile, naval, and operational capabilities, can be interpreted as a gradual transition from a reactive response model toward active deterrence and preemptive action against adversary threats.
​
Under this strategy, the issue is not merely responding to an attack that has already occurred. If signs of a serious and imminent threat or attack are detected, the objective is to raise the cost of that threat before it escalates into a full-scale attack. Put simply, Iran aims to shift from a dynamic of "they strike and we respond" to a situation where, should a threat reach a critical stage, we ourselves determine the timing and location of the response.
