📄 Baqaei dismisses ‘fabricated’ reports of backchannel talks between US and IRGC

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei has dismissed media reports of backchannel talks between the United States and Iran’s Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) leadership, describing the reports as fabricated and part of psychological warfare aimed at creating division among the Islamic Republic’s decision-making bodies.


Baqaei was speaking on Monday during his weekly press briefing in Tehran, responding to questions about US media reports that Washington had reached out to the IRGC leadership through officials in Iraq’s Kurdistan Region in May, while Iranian negotiators were engaged in talks with American representatives to seek an end to the war the US and Israel launched against Iran in February.
​
The US media reports claimed that Washington had sought to find out whether the IRGC had the same position as the Iranian negotiators attending the talks.
​
“News of a secret meeting between Iran and the US in Erbil is totally fabricated. The other news report is a media trick and psychological warfare aimed at creating division among decision-making bodies in the Islamic Republic of Iran,” the spokesperson said, emphasizing that such behavior is out of “desperation.”
​
He said that different sections of the Islamic Republic have demonstrated “unprecedented” cohesion and solidarity over the issue of war and peace in the past six months.
​
“The negotiating team is trusted by all institutions of the system, including defense sectors,” Baqaei said, adding that any decisions regarding talks and negotiations will be made considering the viewpoints of all sections of the Islamic System.
​
He also said that decision-making in the Islamic Republic is an institution-based process, and the institutions make the best decisions while taking into account all the considerations related to foreign policy issues.
​
Baqaei said the Foreign Ministry had not sent any letter to Parliament, asking it to put on hold a bill on strategic action to secure the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf.
​
“The Foreign Ministry has not sent any such letter. We respect the decisions and procedures initiated, pursued, and discussed in the Islamic Consultative Assembly,” the spokesperson said.
​
Baqaei added that Iran already has laws concerning its sovereign rights in the Persian Gulf, the Strait of Hormuz and the Sea of Oman, and that the Foreign Ministry has taken both existing legislation and current developments into account in its views on the bill. He said the ministry would continue to cooperate with parliament and provide its views whenever requested.
​
Baqaei said the complexity of the issue and the involvement of multiple actors have made talks between Iran and Oman on the Strait of Hormuz time-consuming.
​
He said the two sides have reached an understanding on a navigation map and are working to finalize a package comprising the map and a joint statement.
​
Baqaei cited the issue’s complexity, the number of actors seeking to influence the process, and efforts by certain parties opposed to regional peace and security as the main reasons for the delay. He said talks remain serious and ongoing.
​
The spokesperson said there was no 60-day deadline in the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, stressing that Iran does not formulate its policies under pressure, ultimatums, or deadlines.
​
He explained that the memorandum envisaged 60 days of talks on sanctions removal and the nuclear issue, with the possibility of an extension if no agreement was reached. However, the talks never began because the US breached the memorandum weeks after its signing.

🌍 Iran dismisses ‘special message’ claims over Pakistan visit, vows to pursue war-related damages

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei says Pakistani Interior Minister Mohsin Raza Naqvi’s recent visit to Tehran did not involve any special message from Pakistani leaders, while stressing that Islamabad continues to seek a mediating role in Iran–US issues.


Addressing reporters in a weekly press briefing on Monday, Baqaei said that Iran–Pakistan exchanges are routine between two neighboring countries. Naqvi traveled to Tehran at the invitation of Iranian Interior Minister Eskandar Momeni, with discussions focusing on bilateral relations and regional developments.
​
The spokesperson said Pakistan is seeking to use its capacities to facilitate dialogue, adding that the issue of negotiations between Iran and the United States is important for Islamabad because Pakistan played a role in shaping the Islamabad memorandum. He criticized repeated US breaches of commitments, saying they had undermined mediation efforts, as had previously happened with Oman.
​
Baqaei also said Iran would pursue environmental damages resulting from US and Israeli acts of aggression and decades of military activity in the Persian Gulf and Sea of Oman.
​
He argued that environmental damage in the region extends beyond recent conflicts and has accumulated over decades of foreign military presence and wars. According to Baqaei, the consequences affect all countries bordering the Persian Gulf and the Sea of Oman and require collective responsibility.
​
He said Iran would pursue the issue while calling for accountability from the United States and other parties involved in military interventions.
​
Addressing Iran’s position on recognizing the Taliban's caretaker government in Afghanistan, Baqaei described recognition as a legal and political issue.
​
He said Iran and Afghanistan maintain good relations in various fields due to their shared border, religion, history and culture. Regardless of the recognition issue, he added, Tehran will continue and seek to strengthen relations with Kabul in economic, commercial, and other areas.
​
Responding to recent European statements concerning human rights in Iran, Baqaei said European countries lacked the moral standing to lecture Iran on human rights.
​
He criticized European inaction regarding the situation in Palestine and their positions toward US and Israeli aggressions against Iran, arguing that such statements reflected double standards.
​
Baqaei also addressed a recent dispute involving two French embassy staff members in Iran. He said their reported involvement in activities described as supporting Iranian civil society constituted a violation of diplomatic norms and the Vienna Convention.
​
According to the spokesperson, the French ambassador was summoned, and Iran formally protested the activities. The two individuals subsequently left Iran and, he said, would not be allowed to return.
​
Baqaei added that Iran’s Foreign Ministry had fulfilled its responsibilities and that other Iranian institutions would express their positions when deemed appropriate.

🔊 Iran maintains food security despite war, blockade, drought: Agriculture minister

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s Minister of Agriculture Gholamreza Nouri-Ghezeljeh says the country’s food security remained intact throughout recent conflicts imposed by the US and the Israeli regime, despite maritime restrictions, sanctions, and severe drought.


Speaking at a press conference in Tehran on Monday, Nouri-Ghezeljeh said food supplies were not disrupted for even an hour during the recent wars, with domestic production and diversified trade routes helping maintain market stability.
​
He said around 85 percent of Iran’s food production is supplied domestically, while measures have been taken to secure the remaining imports. The minister also highlighted growth in agricultural output, including a 19 percent rise in poultry production and increased egg and red meat production.
​
According to Nouri-Ghezeljeh, crop production in greenhouses increased 18 percent, greenhouse acreage grew 16 percent, and seedling production rose 62 percent. Wheat deliveries also reached 7.7 million tons during the past two difficult agricultural years, despite severe drought.
​
During the latest conflict, three million metric tons of agricultural inputs were unloaded through northern ports, while Iran strengthened trade links with neighboring countries, the minister said, adding that the government also activated a crisis-management headquarters shortly after acts of aggression began to prevent disruptions to food supplies.
​
He added that Iran did not need to draw on its strategic food reserves during the war, while production and stockpiles continued to increase.
​
Nouri-Ghezeljeh stressed that Iran’s geographic diversity, multiple land borders, access to different waterways, and diversified trade channels provide alternatives to maritime restrictions. He attributed the country’s food resilience primarily to domestic farmers, producers and livestock breeders.
