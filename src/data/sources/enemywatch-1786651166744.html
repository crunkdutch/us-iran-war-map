— The decolonization of your mind, the term of "Middle East" and its consequences

In today's vocabulary, to describe the region between the Maghreb and Iran, we imply the term "Middle East". However, when we think about this word, something comes to mind, East to who ? and why Middle ? Its origin comes from the English language in the 20th century when the Brits wished to describe the region between their colony India and Egypt.

The term "East" is not a mere hazard and was used on purpose by the West. At first, Western civilizations needed to describe everything eastward of their Continents (America and Europe). Therefore, from the Ottoman Capital Constantinople to Tokyo in Japan, this region would be called the "East". East to who ? to Europe and America, East of the 'civilized' West.

To make it easier for them to recognize the East, Westerners divided the "East" into three parts : the Near East, the Middle East and the Far East. The Near East represents Egypt and the Levant but is often merged with the Middle East. The Far East represents the Iranic civilizations, the Steppes and everything eastward until Japan.

With the European colonization, these terms have entered our languages we nowadays use it like in Arabic with the term "الشرق الأوسط". People could tell us that it is solely a new way to describe what are our regions and it is simply a technical word of describing. However, the term "East" hides a hard colonization past.

These terms were used as well to separate the 'modern, civilized and advanced' West from the 'barbaric, archaic and undeveloped' East who only awaits to be civilized and fixed by the 'White Saviour'. Do not you see the deception behind it ? When we use this term of "Middle East", we indirectly affirm this inferiority between both of these world's parts.

However, we have nothing to envy the West : not in science, not in religion, not in civilization (the Cradle of Civilization was literally the Levant and Mesopotamia), in nothing ! These foreign terms were imposed on our minds and unfortunately, through the generation we have accepted it — maybe because we feel it is not that important. The truth is, it is this important to our societies. It is important to not feel the East of something, to not feel an inferiority in front of a foreign civilization. So how can we decolonize our minds ?

A better term would be "West Asia", for our region is located in the Asian Continent as we all know. Moreover geographically, we are at the edge of it, close to Africa and Europe. By using the term "West Asia", we reaffirm our true position on the map and remove this inferiority's mindset to truly express what we are. Arabs, Assyrians, Kurds, Persians, Turks and all other ethnicities of the "Middle East" are as Asian as Chineses, Koreans, Japaneses.

It is a hard work on our own mind to change such habits to use the term "Middle East", sometimes it may feel ridiculous to debate on such matters but in the end, we can all agree that it is quiet the opposite. Through education, we may reclaim the societies we are. And perhaps, we can finally extract ourselves from this position of second-zone region in the world.

Yemen

Ansarullah carried out another missile attack on the Saudi-backed forces and the port of Mocha in southwestern Yemen. Explosions were reported at Al-Mokha port after a new ballistic missile attack, with Saudi-backed forces claiming that damage and destruction at the port had increased and that the maritime pier was targeted. Reports said two missiles struck Mocha. Separately, Saudi-backed forces reportedly carried out an artillery attack on a school in the Al-Aabous area of Hayfan district, south of Taiz, targeting the vicinity of the 14 October School. Kan News claimed that Ansarullah was using Russian satellite support for operations against Saudi Arabia and Saudi-backed forces, while Oman was reportedly attempting to mediate a de-escalation between Ansarullah and Saudi Arabia.

Iran

Iranian developments included reports of two missiles being launched toward vessels violating Iranian procedures in the Strait of Hormuz. An Iranian political-security source told Al Mayadeen that the Strait of Hormuz has not been reopened and that Iran has made no change to its policy. The source warned ships against violating Iranian procedures and regulations, saying that large vessels currently cannot pass because of existing risks and that vessels failing to comply could face technical or safety-related problems. The source reiterated that the Strait remains under Iranian monitoring and management. Separately, some news sources claimed that a UAE government aircraft operated by Royal Jet flew to Iran on two consecutive days. According to the reports, the Boeing 737-700 BBJ flew from Abu Dhabi toward the Tehran area on August 11, remained there for about an hour, and departed, before returning to Iran on August 12 and landing at Payam Airport near Karaj for around 30 minutes.

Saudi Arabia

Saudi oil tankers have increasingly been switching off their tracking systems while loading crude at Yanbu on the Red Sea amid threats by Ansarullah against Saudi-linked shipping. The report said Ansarullah declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia on July 20, after which tanker traffic through Bab el-Mandeb reportedly declined sharply, with vessels increasingly rerouting through the Suez Canal or around Africa. The disruption is particularly significant while the Strait of Hormuz remains severely restricted. Saudi shipments through Egypt’s Sidi Kerir terminal reportedly reached a record 2.17 million barrels per day in early August, around 90% of which was Saudi crude.

United States / Qatar

At least four U.S. aerial refueling aircraft reportedly returned to Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar after being withdrawn in late July. The reports indicated that the aircraft had returned to the base amid the continuing regional military tensions.

Lebanon

IOF continued across southern Lebanon, with extensive artillery shelling reported in Wadi al-Hujair, Talousa-Bani Hayyan, the Ali al-Taher Heights, Kafr Tibnit, al-Mansouri, Beit Yahoun, Mashaa al-Mansouri, Harash Ayta al-Jabal, Hadatha, and between Beit Yahoun and Barashit. Israeli warplanes reportedly targeted Mashaa al-Mansouri, while a drone was reported on the Kafra-Al-Assi road. Explosive activity was reported in Harash Ayta al-Jabal, Harash Beit Yahoun and the Ali al-Taher Heights, while a sound bomb was reported in al-Mansouri. Explosions were also reported on the outskirts of Beit Yahoun, around al-Qantara, in Zawtar al-Sharqiya, Bani Hayyan, Hadatha, between Hadatha and Rashaf, Mashaa al-Mansouri, Konin and al-Khiam. Machine-gun bursts and sweeping operations were reported in al-Mansouri, Talousa-Bani Hayyan, Beit Yahoun and Hadatha. Military movements were also reported, including military vehicles moving from Hadatha toward al-Tiri and a D9 bulldozer and Merkava tank moving toward the eastern outskirts of Kafr Shuba. Homes were reportedly burned in Hadatha and Mashaa al-Mansouri. Earlier reports also indicated Israeli airstrikes on Wadi al-Hujair, Marjayoun and Deir Siryan, alongside artillery shelling across several areas of southern Lebanon.
