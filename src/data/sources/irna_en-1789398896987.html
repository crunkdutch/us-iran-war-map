🖥 Muscat meeting to focus on new maritime arrangements in Strait of Hormuz: Araghchi

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says the upcoming regional meeting in Muscat will focus exclusively on establishing new maritime arrangements in the Strait of Hormuz.


In an interview with the London-based Arabic-language news outlet Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, Araghchi said the joint meeting between Iran, its six southern neighbors and Iraq in Muscat will focus exclusively on new maritime arrangements in the Strait of Hormuz.
​
He added that Iran and Oman have agreed to establish the new route and have prepared the relevant maps and a joint statement. The top Iranian diplomat said the details of the agreement and the maps will be presented to the participating countries and then submitted to the International Maritime Organization (IMO) for registration.
​
Araghchi stressed that the Iran-Oman agreement does not mean the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Rather, it sets out the framework and arrangements for maritime traffic should conditions for reopening the strait be met. The agreement also specifies the new entry and exit routes through the Strait of Hormuz, he added.
​
He said that the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is conditional on the United States returning to and fulfilling its commitments under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding (MoU).
​
Araghchi added that Iran is committed to ensuring the security of maritime traffic, but considers US actions, including the naval blockade and economic warfare, the main cause of insecurity. The Iran-Oman agreement only establishes the framework and new maritime routes, which could serve as the basis for restoring normal traffic through the strait if Iran’s conditions are met, Araghchi underlined.
​
The top diplomat said Iran believes that regional security and stability should be ensured by the countries of the region themselves, without interference by foreign actors, stressing dialogue, confidence-building and cooperation among neighbors.
​
However, he said the upcoming meeting is not aimed at establishing a new regional security mechanism, and such a mechanism is not on its agenda. He described the meeting as a confidence-building step that could, if sustained, pave the way for regional cooperation on other issues and reduce external interference.
​
Araghchi also said that the Muscat meeting is not intended to address the Yemen conflict or resolve disputes among regional countries, with its agenda limited to the new maritime arrangements in the Strait of Hormuz. Nevertheless, he said direct dialogue among regional countries could improve mutual understanding, reduce misunderstandings and help manage tensions.
​
Regarding Yemen, he reiterated Iran’s position that there is no military solution and that the country’s future should be determined through Yemeni dialogue and an inclusive political process.

⚡️ World must break silence on continued Israeli atrocities in Lebanon: Iran’s rights body

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s High Council for Human Rights (HCHR) has strongly condemned the Israeli regime's continued airstrikes and shelling of residential areas and crimes against the people of Lebanon.


In a statement on Sunday, the right body described the attacks on Lebanon, which have resulted in the death and injury of dozens of civilians, including women and children, as a blatant violation of fundamental principles of human rights and international humanitarian law.
​
According to it, the continued attacks on civilians constitute an explicit violation of the right to life, the right to security, and human dignity, and contravene fundamental principles of international humanitarian law, including the principle of distinction between military and civilian targets and the principle of proportionality in armed conflict.
​
Underlining that the recurrence of these crimes, coupled with a lack of effective accountability, signals the emergence of a dangerous cycle of impunity, the Council also warned against double standards in addressing human rights violations.
​
"Human rights are universal, and the value of a Lebanese civilian's life should not be deemed less than that of victims elsewhere in the world based on nationality, geography, or political considerations," the statement stressed, adding that "remaining silent in the face of the killing of women and children—while simultaneously claiming to uphold the universality of human rights—seriously undermines the credibility of international human rights mechanisms."
​
Highlighting the inaction of international human rights bodies, including the UN Security Council, the HCHR said that their "failure to take effective measures to halt these atrocities effectively emboldens the Zionist Regime to persist in its gross human rights violations and undermine the concept of international accountability."
​
It further called upon "the Human Rights Council, the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights, and other international mechanisms to abandon their selective and political approaches," urging them to act for the immediate cessation of attacks on civilians, the protection of the right to life of the people of Lebanon, and the documentation and legal pursuit of the crimes committed.
​
"The right to life is a universal right, not a privilege contingent upon geography or politics," the HCHR statement concluded.
