⚡️ 🔴 Analysis of the spiral motion of Kheibar Shekan and Fattah missiles in hypersonic flights:
To understand the spiral motion of missiles in flight, a distinction must be made between two main origins: first, unintentional instabilities resulting from mechanical limitations, changes in the center of mass, and complex aerodynamic phenomena; and second, deliberate maneuvers designed to deceive interceptor systems, make trajectory prediction difficult, and increase accuracy in the final flight phase.
This phenomenon is a combination of flight dynamics, automatic control, and unstable airflows. In the stability analysis of a projectile, two vital points, the center of gravity (CG) and the center of pressure (CP), are of utmost importance. The center of gravity is the point around which the missile's total mass is balanced and serves as a reference for all weight moments, while the center of pressure concentrates the net effect of aerodynamic forces. The basic design principle states that as long as the center of gravity is ahead of the center of pressure, the missile has static stability.
The distance between these two, known as the static margin, determines the missile's ability to return to equilibrium and correct its angle of attack after environmental disturbances. If this margin is too small, any minor disturbance, such as wind gusts or atmospheric turbulence, can turn into angular oscillations. During flight, another factor contributing to instability is the gradual change in the center of gravity due to propellant burning. In solid-propellant missiles, the way the fuel burns can shift the center of mass backward.
When this shift causes the CG to be behind the CP, the missile is on the verge of complete instability: the flight path deviates unpredictably, the body rotates around its longitudinal axis, and its motion appears to the observer as irregular and sometimes erratic spirals. In contrast, some modern systems deliberately use controlled spiral movements to create a nonlinear flight pattern and cause radars or interceptor missiles to miscalculate the trajectory. In general, the spiral motion of missiles indicates the high precision of guidance systems and technical optimization in the design of new-generation missiles, not a malfunction.
