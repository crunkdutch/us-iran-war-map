⚡️ Iran working to overcome obstacles to BRICS New Development Bank membership: FM Araghchi

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said the Islamic Republic is working to remove obstacles to Iran’s membership in the BRICS New Development Bank, while highlighting the growing global focus on using national currencies.


Speaking with national television on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in New Delhi on Saturday, the foreign minister said existing obstacles to joining the BRICS New Development Bank would be addressed during the visit to India.
​
He added that Iran views BRICS as an important platform for expanding economic and financial cooperation and deepening ties among participating countries.
​
Araghchi also underscored the importance of strengthening economic cooperation among BRICS members and promoting the use of national currencies in trade.
​
The minister described the BRICS summit as important, noting that discussions on national currencies at the BRICS Business Council had attracted significant international attention.
​
He said the main BRICS summit was beginning, alongside a series of bilateral meetings involving President Masoud Pezeshkian. Araghchi said the president had held productive talks with Indian, Malaysian and Ethiopian heads of state.
​
According to Araghchi, bilateral and regional issues would feature prominently in these meetings. He emphasized Iran’s expanding relations with Muslim and regional countries, saying this trend was evident throughout the president’s diplomatic engagements.

📡 Pezeshkian calls for stronger economic, financial ties among BRICS members

📌 Tehran, IRNA – President Masoud Pezeshkian has called for stronger economic and financial relations among BRICS member states, saying expanded cooperation can contribute to their development.


Pezeshkian made the remarks in a meeting with Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed Ali on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in New Delhi on Saturday.
​
“Expanding economic and financial relations among BRICS member states can lead to the development of these countries, and everyone should take steps toward increasing interactions,” the president said.
​
Pezeshkian also referred to the “unfair” US-Israeli war of aggression on Iran, saying attempts to create divisions among the Iranian people has instead made them more united.
​
“By resisting, the Iranian people will not allow the United States and Israel to achieve their sinister objectives,” he said, adding that Iran has strengthened ties with Muslim and neighboring countries.
​
He described Iran-Ethiopia relations as having a history of more than 70 years, saying the two countries have always stood by each other and that their relations would become more cohesive and systematic.
​
Abiy, for his part, expressed condolences over the martyrdom of the Leader of the Islamic Revolution, Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, and praised the Iranian people’s resistance, resilience, and patriotism, saying Ethiopians wanted to learn from them.
​
He also called for expanded economic and cultural ties with Iran.

🎙 Malaysian envoy welcomes Iran Air’s return to East Asia

📌 Tehran, IRNA — The Malaysian ambassador to Iran has welcomed the resumption of Iran Air’s flights to Malaysia.


Referring to Iran Air’s history as the flag carrier of the Islamic Republic of Iran, in a meeting with the CEO of Iran Air, Taher Abdollahi, Dato’ Wan Yusri emphasized the importance of developing relations between the two countries.
​
He also expressed his readiness to support and facilitate the necessary coordination in this regard.
​
Meanwhile, stressing the importance of developing Iran Air flight network and its approach to re-expanding international destinations, Abdollahi said re-establishing the Tehran–Kuala Lumpur route can, in the first step, while facilitating the travel of passengers and tourists, pave the way for the development of economic and cultural interactions and greater cooperation between the two countries.

​
They also reviewed the development of bilateral cooperation, strengthening connections between the two countries, and the role of air transport in expanding economic, commercial, tourism, and cultural interactions.

🔊 Civil aviation must not be used as tool of political pressure: Iranian official

📌 Tehran, IRNA — The head of Iran’s Civil Aviation Organization has condemned the United States for imposing broad and unilateral sanctions on the country’s civil aviation sector, warning that the continuation of such measures could harm regional and international aviation.


Captain Abouzar Shiroudi said in a Saturday statement to the ICAO Council President that Iran expects urgent attention to the serious and ongoing consequences of US extraterritorial restrictions on its civil aviation.
​
He noted that after the failure of the US and Israel to achieve their goals during their imposed wars in 2025 and 2026, Washington expanded its pressure to civil aviation, sanctioning all Iranian airlines on September 8, 2026.
​
Shiroudi stressed that these actions contradict the principles of the 1944 Chicago Convention, which views the development of international aviation as a means of preserving friendship among nations.
​
He also added that the sanctions undermine ICAO’s strategic goal for 2026–2050 — No Country Left Behind — highlighting the gap between this objective and the reality faced by sanctioned states.
​
Despite years of restrictions, he noted, ICAO has taken no effective steps to support Iran’s aviation sector, which has continued safe operations mainly through domestic capabilities.
​
Civil aviation must never be used as a tool for political or economic pressure, and no state should be prevented from maintaining adequate aviation safety standards under such conditions, he added.

📡 Iran reaches Men's Volleyball Asian Championship final after beating Australia

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran reached the final of the AVC Men's Volleyball Asian Championship after defeating Australia 3-1 in the semifinal in Fukuoka, Japan, on Saturday.


Iran, coached by Roberto Piazza, won the first, third and fourth sets 25-21, 25-17 and 25-22 respectively, while Australia took the second set 25-17. The victory secured Iran’s place in the final and earned the team a ninth qualification for the 2027 World Championship in Poland.
​
Iran entered the match with a starting lineup of Morteza Sharifi, Issa Naseri, Ali Hajipour, Pouria Hossein Khanzadeh, Mohammad Valizadeh, Arshia Behnejad, and libero Mohammad Reza Hazratpour.
​
The match lasted 117 minutes, with Hossein Khanzadeh recording powerful serves reaching speeds of 129, 117 and 115 kilometers per hour.
​
The win was Iran’s 22nd victory over Australia in their 29 meetings across competitions.
​
Iran will play the AVC Men's Volleyball Asian Championship final on Sunday at 2 p.m. Fukuoka time against the winner of Japan and South Korea. The team aims to add another continental title to its record while continuing its run in Asian volleyball.

🔊 Why Iran seeks permanent end to war, not temporary ceasefire?

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Islamic Republic of Iran has emphasized that the key objective in the ongoing war is to eliminate the threat and secure a decisive and permanent end to the war, stressing that a temporary ceasefire without guarantees against renewed aggression would not provide lasting stability.


From the early days of the war, Iranian officials have repeatedly said that any arrangement to restore stability in the region must ensure that hostilities will not resume. The issue was also identified as the first and key provision of the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding that was signed by Tehran and Washington in mid-June.
​
Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on March 15 that the war must end in a way that prevents its recurrence.
​
“This war must end in such a way that it is not repeated again. When we say we do not want a ceasefire, it is not because, for example, we are seeking war, but because this time the war must end in a way that our enemies never again think of repeating these attacks and aggression,” he said.
​
Kazem Gharibabadi, deputy foreign minister for legal and international affairs, also stressed in the early weeks of the war that any ceasefire must include guarantees against renewed aggression.
​
“If a ceasefire is to be established or the war is to stop, there must be a guarantee that acts of aggression against Iran will not be repeated; otherwise, if another attack takes place after a few months, such a ceasefire will be meaningless,” he said.
​
In the latest position, Islamic Revolution Guards Corps spokesperson Brigadier General Hossein Mohebbi outlined six conditions for ending the current situation, with the first two calling for a complete halt to the war and an end to renewed threats.
​
The conditions reflect principles also incorporated into the first provision of the Islamabad MoU, signed by the presidents of Iran and the United States on June 17.
​
Analysts argue that Iran’s fundamental logic is to maintain pressure until it secures firm, upfront guarantees, rather than relying on promises for the future, making another round of confrontation highly unlikely.
​
Iran’s official positions indicate that Tehran considers merely managing the crisis insufficient and is instead seeking to address what it regards as the roots of the conflict. From Tehran’s perspective, a return to temporary ceasefires without concrete legal, military and international guarantees could leave the country exposed to a cycle of renewed attacks and instability.
​
Accordingly, Iran has called for credible guarantees ensuring a permanent end to the war, respect for its territorial integrity and measures preventing a recurrence of aggression. Tehran maintains that without such guarantees, a ceasefire alone cannot constitute a durable basis for regional stability.
