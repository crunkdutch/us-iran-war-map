The current draft agreement reportedly includes the following general framework:

• The nuclear issue remains untouched
No agreement on Iran's nuclear file is included in the current memorandum, and Iran is not undertaking any new commitments. Nuclear talks would take place during a 60-day period following the signing of the agreement.

• Strait of Hormuz: No transfer of control, no US role
Iran is not committing to hand over management of the Strait of Hormuz. The future administration of the waterway would be resolved as a regional matter through dialogue and joint decision-making between Tehran and Oman.

• A definitive end to the war on all fronts, including Lebanon
The primary objective of the memorandum is to end the war across all regional fronts. The United States would commit to compelling Israel to end the war in Lebanon, and the phrase "extension of the ceasefire" does not appear anywhere in the text.

• Release of frozen assets through a defined mechanism
Part of Iran's frozen assets would be released immediately after signing, with the remainder released gradually during the negotiations. Tehran has reportedly received clear guarantees based on mechanisms it proposed.

• War reparations on the agenda
Compensation for damages inflicted on Iran during the US and Israeli aggression is included in the memorandum. The mechanism for obtaining reparations would be agreed upon during the 60-day negotiations following the signing.

• Details of lifting primary and secondary sanctions to be addressed in a final agreement
The removal of all US sanctions and international resolutions would be examined during the 60-day nuclear negotiations.

• Three issues and 60 days for a final agreement
Only three issues would be discussed during the 60-day negotiations: continuation of Iran's peaceful nuclear program, lifting US unilateral sanctions, and mechanisms for compensation. No other issues, including Iran's missile capabilities, would be on the agenda.
