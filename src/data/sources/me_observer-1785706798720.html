- Considering the naval blockade imposed on Iran an act of aggression contrary to international law.
- Emphasizing that Iranian strikes are based on the legitimate right of defense stipulated in the United Nations Charter.
- Holding the Security Council and the United Nations responsible for their failure to stop what Iran describes as American aggression.
- Rejecting American justifications for the war, such as the nuclear file or defending, and considering them political pretexts.
- Emphasizing that the real reason for the conflict is Iran's refusal to submit to American hegemony and its adherence to its independence.
- Linking the current confrontation to a long history of American hostility towards Iran, starting with the 1953 coup and support for the Shah's regime.
- Washington orchestrating the incident of the ships in the Strait of Hormuz and using it as a pretext to resume military escalation.
- The United States used commercial ships for military purposes and transported personnel and military equipment through them.
- Condemning countries that assisted the United States or Israel by providing territory, bases, or logistical facilities.
- Emphasizing that Iran is not hostile to neighboring countries and seeks relations based on mutual respect and good neighborliness.
- Calling on the countries of the region not to allow their territories, airspace, or facilities to be used in any operations targeting Iran.
- Emphasizing that any Iranian response to sources of attack should not be considered an aggression against the countries from which the operations originated, but rather an exercise of the right to self-defense.
- Praising the unity of the Iranian people, armed forces, and political leadership, and emphasizing continued steadfastness.
- The confrontation is not only a defense of Iran but also a defense of international law, the sovereignty of nations, and the rejection of American hegemony.
- Sending a clear deterrent message that Iran will continue the confrontation and will not back down under military, economic, or political pressures.
