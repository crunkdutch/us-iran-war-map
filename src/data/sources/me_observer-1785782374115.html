⚡️ 🔹 According to statistics from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), the country's Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPR) have reached their lowest level since 1982 (44 years ago), approaching 300 million barrels.
🔹 These reserves, which were close to 650 million barrels before the Ukraine war, were reduced to 350 million barrels by mid-2023 to control prices. By 2026, the U.S. increased these reserves to 400 million barrels, which it has begun to release to control global oil prices due to the war with Iran.
🔹 According to estimates, with the current release trajectory (if the UAE and Saudi pipelines are active), it will approach the critical level of 150 million barrels by October of the current year. (This is one of the reasons for the U.S.'s insistence on trying to end the war.)
🔹 This critical threshold is significant for two reasons. On the one hand, 150 million barrels will only be sufficient for a few months, and on the other hand, replenishing these reserves will take a longer time, which limits the U.S.'s ability to wage war in the short term due to a higher risk margin.
🔹 In the event of an escalation of the war and a move towards targeting oil infrastructure and the elimination of the two UAE and Saudi pipelines, the rate of depletion of strategic reserves worldwide will increase and reach its critical limit faster.
