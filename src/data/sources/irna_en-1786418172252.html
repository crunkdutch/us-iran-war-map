📄 Journalists play valuable role in understanding global developments: Araghchi

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has congratulated journalists on Journalists’ Day, stressing their important role in helping society understand global developments and Iran’s position in the world.


In a message marking Journalists’ Day on August 8, Araghchi said understanding global developments and Iran’s position requires an accurate perspective and deep knowledge of the complexities of diplomacy, adding that journalists covering foreign policy play a valuable role in shaping such understanding.
​
He said the occasion was an opportunity to appreciate those who remain committed to the truth amid the flow of news, observe carefully, ask courageously and write responsibly.
​
Araghchi also praised Iranian journalists for their courage and sense of responsibility during the recent imposed war, saying they remained alongside the people during difficult days, accurately documented what happened in the country and conveyed it to the world.
​
Iran marks Journalist’s Day annually on August 8 to honor journalists and commemorate the anniversary of the 1998 killing of Iranian journalist Mahmoud Saremi in Mazar-i-Sharif, Afghanistan.

📄 Gharibabadi: Caspian Sea convention does not affect delimitation of seabed, subsoil

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Deputy Foreign Minister for Legal and International Affairs Kazem Gharibabadi says the ratification and entry into force of the Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea will have no impact on the delimitation of the seabed and subsoil of the body of water.


Speaking at IRNA’s Diplomacy Desk , Gharibabadi said in an interview published on Saturday that the delimitation of the Caspian seabed and subsoil must be determined through separate agreements between countries with adjacent and opposite coasts.
​
The interview with Gharibabadi was conducted this week as the Iranian government has decided to urgently submit the Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea to parliament. Iran is the only Caspian littoral state that has not yet ratified the convention. The government’s decision comes amid public debate over the convention’s provisions, Iran’s rights and interests in the Caspian, and questions surrounding the country’s share of the sea.
​
According to the deputy foreign minister, the decision was based on three main considerations: changes in the security environment around the Caspian Sea, the need to strengthen economic and commercial cooperation among littoral states, and the resolution of certain legal issues concerning the sea.
​
Gharibabadi noted that the security situation around the Caspian has changed significantly over the past two years, particularly as the presence of non-littoral actors in the region has increased as a result of decisions by some coastal states.
​
He also said the convention could facilitate greater economic and commercial cooperation among the five Caspian littoral states, including in fisheries, transportation and transit. He attributed delays in concluding several cooperation agreements to the fact that the convention has yet to enter into force.
​
Addressing concerns raised in Iran over the convention, Gharibabadi said issues such as Iran’s share of the Caspian Sea, its energy resources, water level, baseline and seabed delimitation should not be conflated with the convention itself.
​
He said the administration respects the concerns of critics and plans to hold meetings with academics and researchers holding different views on the Caspian issue, adding that their concerns should be addressed through documented and reasoned responses.
​
On security provisions, Gharibabadi said Article 3 of the convention prohibits the presence and even transit of armed forces belonging to non-littoral states in the Caspian Sea. The littoral states have also undertaken not to allow their territories to be used by other states for acts of aggression or other military actions against another member state.
​
Regarding seabed and subsoil rights, he said Article 8 stipulates that their delimitation must be determined through agreements between states with adjacent and opposite coasts, in accordance with generally recognized principles of international law.
​
Gharibabadi rejected claims that ratifying the convention would undermine Iran’s interests or future negotiations over the Caspian seabed, stressing that Iran’s sovereignty, sovereign rights and jurisdiction over resources within its maritime area would remain intact.
​
He added that Iran has so far protected the area it considers its seabed and subsoil territory and has not allowed other parties to conduct drilling there.

🖥 Judiciary: Enemy crimes will not go unanswered

📌 Tehran, IRNA — The Judiciary spokesperson has warned that crimes committed against nations and Islamic movements will not go unanswered, saying those responsible will eventually be held accountable.


Asghar Jahangir made the remarks on Saturday during his press conference, stressing that those behind such actions would have to answer for them.
​
“Certainly, [the enemy] will be held accountable for these actions. We hope that the criminals and perpetrators of these actions will soon receive the punishment for their deeds at the hands of Muslims and freedom-seekers,” he said.
​
The judiciary spokesperson also stressed the need for media outlets to remain vigilant against enemy efforts to manipulate public priorities.
​
“Journalists and media outlets must be careful that the enemy seeks to make the main issues and real concerns of society appear secondary, while presenting issues of secondary importance as the main issues and priorities of society and highlighting them,” Jahangir said.
​
He urged the media to avoid focusing on peripheral issues and narratives that have no significant impact on people’s lives or security, warning that doing so could unintentionally serve an agenda designed by Iran’s foes.
​
Regarding the case involving the victims of the Minab tragedy and the attack on Shajareh Tayebeh School, Jahangir offered condolences to the victims’ families and said the case was being investigated by the Tehran prosecutor’s office.
​
He added that a final decision would be made within two weeks and identified the Israeli regime and the US government as defendants in the case.
​
Jahangir also rebuked some Western media outlets for censoring the Arbaeen pilgrimage, saying they sought to ignore its scale and its religious and humanitarian message in Iran, Iraq, and other parts of the world.
​
“If human society awakens and properly recognizes the religious and human values ​​inherent in this great movement, it will certainly stand against the currents of arrogance, oppression, and injustice,” he added.

📄 Journalists key in building empathy, unity and social cohesion: President

📌 Tehran, IRNA – President Masoud Pezeshkian has reiterated his call for preserving national unity and cohesion, emphasizing that journalists and media personnel can play a vital role in creating an atmosphere conducive to social harmony.


Pezeshkian made the remarks during a press conference at the Interior Ministry in Tehran on Saturday, held to mark National Journalist Day. The event was attended by presidential aides, several cabinet ministers, as well as journalists and media professionals.
​
Stating that his only motivation is to serve the country based on justice and fairness, the president emphasized that journalists also have a duty to foster an atmosphere that strengthens national unity and cohesion.
​
He further elaborated on the importance and effectiveness of the media, noting that journalists, through honest reporting, can render futile the enemy's plots to sow division and discord within Iranian society.
​
Pezeshkian also urged media professionals and those working in the field to avoid covering contentious issues or disseminating incorrect and conflicting information through their platforms. He added that journalists should help prevent confrontation over ambiguous matters.
​
Referring to the high status that journalists hold in conveying truth to society, the president continued by saying that many historical facts would be forgotten and many crimes would remain hidden and unseen if journalists fail in their mission.
​
During the press conference, Pezeshkian also addressed other issues, including the economic problems resulting from the US-Israeli imposed war on Iran, and reiterated that the issue of people's livelihoods remains one of the most important concerns of his administration.
​
He said that American and Zionist enemies had speculated that they would overthrow Iran through a military offensive, but the Iranian Armed Forces, on the one hand, and the exceptional presence of the people in the streets, on the other, prevented them from carrying out their evil intentions.
