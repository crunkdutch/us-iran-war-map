📄 WWII-era Allied occupation remains etched in Iranian memory: Foreign Ministry spokesperson

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei has mentioned the 85th anniversary of the Allied invasion and occupation of Iran, saying the painful experience remains deeply rooted in the collective memory of the Iranian people.


In a post on X marking the anniversary of the invasion of Iran on August 24, 1941, Baqaei recalled on Tuesday that Iran was occupied despite its official declaration of neutrality during World War II. Soviet forces entered from the north and east, while British forces attacked from the south and west by land and air.
​
He said the Iranian army collapsed within days, major cities were occupied and the entire country came under foreign military control. Reza Shah Pahlavi abdicated 22 days after the invasion and was subsequently exiled to Mauritius, then a British-controlled island.
​
Despite the Shah’s government’s formal declaration of neutrality—which Mohammad Reza Pahlavi later, in a speech on November 5, 1974, apologetically described as the illusion of a “golden cage of neutrality” in “a world governed by the logic of force,” he said.
​
Baqaei said the occupation, which lasted six years, inflicted extensive damage and suffering on Iran. Beyond the destruction, loss of life and looting of Iranian assets, he said, the deepest wound was the humiliation inflicted on a nation that had no role in the wars imposed on the world by European powers.
​
The painful experience will never be erased from the memory of Iranians, Baqaei said, stressing that the historical episode continues to shape Iran’s understanding of national independence and security.
​
He argued that Iran, having overcome major crises with resilience and national resolve, is once again facing a decisive test.
​
According to Baghaei, protecting Iran’s national dignity and independence requires self-confidence, determination, and firm commitment to resistance.
​
He further maintained that national strength, in all its dimensions, remains the only effective deterrent against foreign demands and aggression.

📌 Moscow, IRNA – The 30th edition of MIMS Automobility Saint Petersburg 2026, Russia’s international exhibition for auto parts, aftermarket products and automotive services, has opened in St. Petersburg with the participation of Iranian companies in a unified national pavilion.


Iranian Ambassador to Russia Kazem Jalali addressed the opening ceremony, attended by Russian officials and representatives from participating countries, including China and South Korea. He said Iran’s presence at the exhibition for the fifth consecutive year demonstrates the two countries’ determination to expand industrial cooperation.
​
Jalali said trade between Iranian auto-parts companies and Russia has increased eightfold over the past five years, highlighting the growing potential of both markets.
​
He added that Iran has around 1,850 companies active in auto-parts manufacturing and the automotive supply chain, with a number of knowledge-based and industrial capabilities showcased at the event.
​
The fifth Iranian national pavilion was established with authorization from the Trade Promotion Organization of Iran and cooperation from the Association of Homogeneous Propulsion and Auto Parts Industries. Seven Iranian parts and supply-chain companies, along with two trading firms, are participating across 162 square meters.
​
More than 30 Iranian industry executives and business representatives are also attending meetings with Russian manufacturers, distributors and automotive companies.
​
Held for the first time in St. Petersburg, MIMS Automobility 2026 brings together more than 1,500 companies and is expected to attract over 45,000 professional visitors. The exhibition runs through August 28 at Expoforum.

🎙 Iran, Oman agree on temporary maritime corridor in Strait of Hormuz

📌 Tehran, IRNA – During a visit by Omani foreign minister to Iran on Tuesday, the two sides issued a joint statement declaring an agreement on establishing a temporary joint maritime corridor and implementing a joint project to clear mines from the Strait of Hormuz.


According to the joint statement, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and his Omani counterpart, Sayyid Badr Hamad Al-Busaidi, held ‘constructive consultations’ focused on the resumption of safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, while preserving the sovereignty and sovereign rights of both nations.
​
The consultations focused on the importance both countries attach to the resumption of safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz while preserving their sovereignty and sovereign rights.
​
The two Ministers discussed a phased framework that could provide a practical and implementable basis for moving forward, in light of the current situation in the Strait resulting from the recent war and its tragic repercussions.
​
The proposed framework includes the establishment of a joint temporary navigational corridor through the Strait of Hormuz and an agreement to implement a joint project to clear the Strait of mines. Technical negotiations between the two sides would continue with a view to agreeing on a permanent navigational corridor and future administration of the Strait, as well as a mechanism for information-sharing, traffic management, and the provision of relevant navigational and security services.
​
In this context, both sides underscored the importance of convening joint discussions with regional states bordering the waters of the Persian Gulf. They further stressed the need to uphold applicable international law and respect the sovereign rights of the coastal states.
