🇮🇷 | IRGC:

The IRGC has released videos and images of the unmanned submarine.

According to information released by domestic news agencies, the unmanned submarine in question is the Dive-LD, manufactured by Anduril.

The unmanned submarine is 5.8 meters long and 1.2 meters in diameter, with an endurance of up to 10 days and a maximum operating depth of 6,000 meters.

Its modular design allows it to be rapidly reconfigured for a range of missions, including underwater intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR), mine countermeasures, anti-submarine warfare (ASW), and high-resolution seabed mapping. Given its deployment in the Strait of Hormuz, reconnaissance and mine countermeasures are likely among its primary missions.

The Dive-LD is classified as a Large Displacement Unmanned Undersea Vehicle (LDUUV). In 2024, the Pentagon selected the system under the Replicator program, which aims to mass-produce low-cost autonomous and unmanned systems. The U.S. Navy’s Unmanned Undersea Vehicle Squadron 1 (UUVRON-1) received the system in 2025.

🇮🇷 | In retaliation, Iran launched a large-scale military operation codenamed “Ya Ali Karrar.” Ballistic missile launches were reported from multiple missile cities across Iran, including Kermanshah, Isfahan, Yazd, Zanjan, Khorramabad, Khomeyn, and Tabriz. Initial reports estimated that more than 30 ballistic missiles were launched, while later reports put the total at 35 to 40 missiles in the first wave alone, followed by a second wave of launches from central Iran, including additional launches from Isfahan, Yazd, and Tabriz. Iranian media described it as the largest missile wave since the April ceasefire and one of Iran’s biggest missile attacks in the last five months.

Iranian missiles targeted American military installations in Jordan, with Arab and Iranian media reporting strikes on the Muwaffaq Salti (Al-Azraq) and Prince Hassan air bases. Local sources in Jordan reported large numbers of Iranian missiles heading toward U.S. bases, while American and Jordanian air defense systems launched dozens of Patriot interceptors to stop the attack. Reports said that more than 60 Patriot missiles were fired, while Iranian media reported that many Iranian missiles passed through the defensive shield and struck their intended targets. Videos circulated online showing Iranian missiles passing through American interceptors before impacting military facilities. At least five missile impacts were confirmed inside Jordan, while explosions were heard across several locations in the country.

Several reports also said that Iran used cluster-warhead ballistic missiles for the first time since the Ramadan War. Iranian media later reported the use of Kheibar Shekan ballistic missiles during the operation, while military observers reported that Qader-H missiles were also among those launched. Iranian military sources stated that simultaneous launches from multiple missile bases were intended to make it more difficult for the enemy to suppress the launchers and intercept incoming missiles.

According to enemy media reports, missile launches were also detected toward Bahrain, while reports of explosions inside Bahrain remained unconfirmed. Air defenses in southern Jordan were activated again during the second wave of launches, and additional explosions were reported near American military positions.

Israeli and American media also reported direct hits on American bases in Jordan. Israeli Channel 14 reported direct impacts on an American base, while Hebrew media reported casualties among U.S. soldiers following the missile strikes. The New York Times later reported that American soldiers were wounded during the Iranian attack on Jordan. American sources also acknowledged that U.S. bases in Jordan had come under Iranian missile fire.

At the same time, reports said that the IRGC launched an attack on an American Arleigh Burke-class destroyer operating in the Sea of Oman. Later reports confirmed that Iran launched a second wave of attacks against U.S. Navy ships in the region. According to The Wall Street Journal, the second strike missed its targets but raised concerns in Washington over Iran’s increasingly sophisticated anti-ship missile capabilities.

In Announcement No. 13, the IRGC’s Public Relations Department confirmed that the Aerospace Force carried out heavy missile strikes against the American Al-Azraq Air Base in Jordan in retaliation for the American attack on Iranian oil tankers. The IRGC stated that the operation targeted maintenance and repair facilities, aircraft preparation and deployment areas, shelters for F-35, F-16, and F-15 fighter jets, and other military infrastructure, inflicting heavy damage on the base.

In Announcement No. 14, the IRGC confirmed that it also targeted the American destroyers DDG-119 and DDG-53, describing them as warships equipped with cruise missiles and the Aegis combat system. The IRGC stated that the two destroyers were struck with ballistic missiles and that significant damage was inflicted on the vessels.
