⚡️ #Enemy_Recognition | Explanation of Exercise Southern Jackaroo 2026
⏺From multinational ground combat to preparing the US coalition in northern Australia
🔹Exercise Southern Jackaroo 2026 was held from May 29 to July 3, 2026, at the Townsville Training Area in Queensland, Australia. This exercise, with the main participation of the US, Australia, and Japan, was one of the most important trilateral ground exercises in the South Pacific and culminated in a large joint fire operation on July 2.
🔹The title Jackaroo in Australian culture refers to a young ranch worker; however, the content of the exercise was entirely military: offensive and defensive operations, force-on-force engagement, armored mobility, combat engineering, mortar and artillery fire, anti-tank missiles, heavy machine guns, and finally, the execution of a joint attack with live ammunition.

⚡️ 1. Nature and Dimensions of the Exercise
🔹It should be understood that Southern Jackaroo 2026 was not a classic exercise at the division or corps level; rather, it was a multinational exercise at the tactical unit level, whose main objective was defined as connecting the infantry, armored, artillery, engineering, and support forces of the three countries into a common combat structure.
🔹According to published information, approximately 300 US Marines, 1,500 Australian forces, and 400 Japanese military personnel participated in the core of the exercise; therefore, the strength of the forces of the three countries in the main section is estimated to be approximately 2,200 personnel. Some Australian reports, including support units and simultaneous activities, have cited figures close to three thousand personnel. Therefore, the number 2,200 for the main force and the larger number for the support complex and related activities is more logical. This iteration of Southern Jackaroo was described as the largest phase of this series executed to date, and the Australian Army also introduced it as its largest ground exercise in 2026.

⚡️ 2. Participating Units
United States
The main element of the U.S. Marine Corps was provided by Marine Rotational Force – Darwin (MRF-D 26):
1st Battalion, 5th Marines;
Alpha Company, 1/5 Battalion, as one of the main maneuver elements;
Weapons Company, 1/5 Battalion;
Combat Logistics Battalion 5;
Marines and Sailors supporting the Darwin Rotational Force.
The U.S. Army also participated with elements from the following unit:
1st Battalion, 24th Infantry Regiment;
1st Infantry Brigade Combat Team;
11th Airborne Division, stationed in Alaska.
The simultaneous entry of the Marine Corps and the 11th Airborne Division indicates that the U.S. did not consider the exercise solely a Navy-Marine activity; rather, it viewed it as an opportunity to coordinate deployed naval forces with light ground forces suitable for cold, jungle, and remote areas.
Australia
The main host force was from the 3rd Brigade, 1st Division of the Australian Army. Elements from the following units are seen in official images and reports:
3rd Brigade, Australian Army;
2nd Cavalry Regiment and its armored vehicles;
4th Field Artillery Regiment;
Combat engineering elements;
Infantry, fire support, logistics, and reconnaissance units.
Japan
The Japan Ground Self-Defense Force mainly participated in the exercise with forces from the 7th Infantry Regiment, 3rd Division. In addition to infantry operations, Japanese units participated in obstacle crossing, armored movement, and artillery fire with M777A2 howitzers.
