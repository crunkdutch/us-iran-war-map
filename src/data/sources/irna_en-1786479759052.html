📄 Littoral states responsible for security, protection of Caspian Sea: Iran deputy FM

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Deputy Foreign Minister for Legal and International Affairs, Kazem Gharibabadi, has reminded the five coastal countries of the Caspian Sea of their responsibility for the security and protection of the world's largest inland body of water, warning that neglect could cause irreparable damage.


At the ceremony on Monday to commemorate the Caspian Sea Day that falls on August 12, Gharibabadi pointed to the importance of this strategic body of water and the shared responsibility of the coastal countries – namely Iran, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Russia and Turkmenistan – towards it.
​
Warning that the decline in the Caspian Sea's water level, pollution, and the weakening of the ecosystem can directly affect the lives of coastal communities and economic activities, he called for collective and coordinated efforts to confront these challenges.
​
He referred to "the Framework Convention for the Protection of the Caspian Sea Environment," also known as the Tehran Convention, saying that its implementation 20 years ago is a stark reminder of the importance of the sea for Iran and its coastal countries.
​
Stating that the Caspian Sea has been part of the geography, identity, and relations of nations surrounding it, Gharibabadi noted that the lake also played a role in shaping bonds between the societies of this region and became a bridge for human, cultural, and civilizational movements.
​
According to him, the geopolitical and strategic importance of the Caspian Sea has grown further in the face of the current regional turmoil, especially the joint US-Israeli-imposed war on Iran and its wider consequences.
​
"The Caspian littoral countries are more affected than any other country by the recent developments," he referred to the US-Israeli aggression, emphasizing that security is a regional and long-term responsibility through mechanisms agreed upon by the five countries.
​
Gharibabadi further warned against turning the Caspian Sea into an arena for competition or the presence of powers outside the region, stressing the need for decisions about the security, stability, and future of the Caspian Sea entirely on the littoral states.
​
The Caspian Sea has the potential to become one of the areas of trust-building and regional cooperation; he continued that differences in views based on national priorities are a bit natural and could be resolved through dialogue and cooperation between the five countries.
​
According to him, no coastal country can provide a comprehensive solution to a phenomenon alone and therefore, collective efforts are needed, under the framework of the Tehran Convention, to preserve and continue the Caspian Sea's capacities for future generations.
​
Gharibabadi underscored the need for holding the Tehran Convention Conference of the Parties regularly and effectively, the next round of which is set in Iran on October 1, 2026, in order to turn this framework into a more effective platform for scientific cooperation, exchange of knowledge and information, and formation of joint initiatives.
​
He concluded by thanking the diplomatic community and all other guests attending the Caspian Day ceremony, saying the celebration is aimed at striving a healthy, stable Caspian Sea that enjoys its rightful place in regional cooperation.

📡 Lincoln quagmire: From US show of force against Iran to cry for help

📌 London, IRNA – The prolonged deployment of the US aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln has raised concerns over the mental and physical well-being of its crew, with families of sailors and Marines pressing US Navy officials for answers about their living conditions and the timing of the carrier’s return home.


Around 200 family members met with acting Secretary of the Navy Hung Cao last Thursday at Naval Air Station North Island in San Diego, raising concerns about the crew’s mental health, shortages of food and essential supplies, water quality and living conditions aboard the carrier, according to US media reports.
​
The Daily Beast reported that Navy commanders acknowledged the strain caused by the prolonged deployment on service members, their families and the Navy’s ability to maintain the long-term well-being of its personnel. Officials did not provide a specific date for the carrier’s return.
​
Concerns about the crew’s condition had surfaced earlier. Stars and Stripes reported that crew members had raised concerns about their physical and mental health and described severe fatigue after months at sea.
​
The Lincoln left San Diego on November 21, 2025, with more than 5,000 sailors and Marines. Its deployment, initially expected to last around seven months, was extended following the US attack on Iran, and its crew has now entered its ninth month away from home.
​
The carrier reached 207 consecutive days at sea on July 6, surpassing the previous record set by the USS Dwight D. Eisenhower during the COVID-19 pandemic. It later spent a brief period in Oman after 208 uninterrupted days at sea.
​
Reports have also highlighted problems aboard the USS Gerald R. Ford, including a laundry-room fire that reportedly affected nearly 200 sailors and damaged around 100 sleeping areas. The carrier eventually returned to the United States after a 295-day deployment.
​
The newly released reports have given a different meaning to the term “help.” US President Donald Trump dispatched warships to West Asia after claiming that “help was on its way” to Iran, where protests over economic conditions in January were hijacked by foreign-backed terrorists.
​
Now, more than six months after Trump’s propaganda campaign, the word “help” is being heard in a very different context—not as a claim by the US president that assistance was on its way to exert pressure on Iran, but as a plea coming from the crew of the Abraham Lincoln.
​
The Lincoln, which was supposed to symbolize Washington’s show of force and military pressure against Iran, is now itself caught in the quagmire of a protracted and exhausting mission, with calls for help coming not from Tehran, but from the carrier’s deck and from the families of US service members in San Diego.
