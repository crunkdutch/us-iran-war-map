💬 Charoq, a traditional footwear in Iran’s Zanjan Province

📌 Zanjan, IRNA — Charoq is a traditional type of footwear that has been crafted in various styles for centuries in the northern Iranian province of Zanjan.


One of Zanjan’s most authentic handicrafts, Charoq, flourished during the Safavid and Qajar eras and evolved with shifting lifestyles, blending leatherwork, silk embroidery, and delicate traditional designs to attract people with different tastes.
​
But Charoq Duzi, like other handicrafts, has been facing obstacles in the age of machine-based modern technology, with artisans in Zanjan emphasizing that the survival of this craft requires transitioning from traditional methods to modern economic models.
​
Ali Heidari, an experienced Charoq-maker, says artisans and apprentices must be supported to prevent further stagnation of this art form and to preserve its authenticity.
​
The 70-year-old also stressed the need for suitable avenues for displaying and selling the products, adding that an artisan spends months crafting a single product with passion and dedication, but in vain without its commercialization.
​
Emphasizing that artisans need support to produce work to keep this art form alive, as they cannot afford to rent their shops and galleries in upscale commercial zones. “Art requires passion and dedication; however, alongside that passion, an artist must also be able to earn an income from their work,” he underscored.
​
Ali Rezaei, another Charoq-maker, insisted that, to ensure the art survives and retains its authenticity, it was essential to facilitate training, production, promotion, and—above all—sales.
​
Stating that he had dedicated fifty years to this craft because of his love for Charogh-making, but said he had reached a point where he no longer had the same motivation because of dwindling income. “If there is real support, I can continue this work for as long as I live.”
​
Rezaei emphasized that Charogh-making was part of Zanjan’s identity, and that the people and authorities must not allow it to die out, urging them to simply acknowledge the reality of the artists’ lives and take action to preserve these art forms.
​
Deputy Director of handicrafts at the Zanjan Province Department of Cultural Heritage, Tourism and Handicrafts, Dariush Naderi, has also acknowledged the challenges facing the art of Charoq-making (traditional leather footwear). He cited a lack of new-generation artisans, shifts in lifestyle and clothing habits, and competition from industrial products as among the most significant problems.
​
Naderi stressed the need for new applications that align with the needs and tastes of today’s society, pointing to the necessity of expanding the market in order to allow artisans to showcase their products via the internet and online sales platforms to customers in other cities and even other countries.

“There is absolutely no truth to the claim made by US officials regarding Iran’s involvement in these developments, and it is categorically denied,” Baqaei said on Monday during his weekly press conference in Tehran.
​
He added that the US continues to make false allegations to create divisions between countries in West Asia and complicate the situation amid tensions.
​
“Americans are constantly producing false news reports and making false claims at no cost to themselves; there is no doubt about that. In these circumstances, the fact that the United States keeps fabricating lies to deepen divisions among regional countries is one of the consequences of the issues facing our region, which the countries themselves must resolve among them. Otherwise, those who have no goodwill toward our region will continue to resort to such lies to further complicate the situation.”
​
Responding to a question about the latest military advance by Yemen’s Ansarullah movement along the Red Sea coast, and whether Tehran plays a role in the movement’s decisions, the spokesperson said that Iran has no role in Yemen-related decisions, stressing that Ansarullah is an independent party that acts based on its own interests.
​
Baqaei said some parties are exploiting regional developments to deepen divisions among Muslim countries. He added that Yemen’s actions should be viewed in the context of years of attacks and blockade against the country, as well as repeated US and Israeli strikes over the past two years.
​
He stressed that maritime security and freedom of navigation must be ensured for all, urging regional countries to avoid unfounded accusations and focus on addressing the root causes of tensions.
​
Baqaei said incomplete or distorted accounts of facts by the head of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) provide grounds for their misuse.
​
The spokesperson further said that responsibility for participation in or aiding an internationally wrongful act does not disappear over time, adding that Iran will continue documenting and explaining the issue.
​
He criticized the IAEA and its director general for allowing reports to be misused in support of last week’s resolution against Iran, saying the resolution was not based on the report itself but on its misuse.
​
Baqaei urged the IAEA chief to condemn US and Israeli attacks on Iran’s nuclear facilities, which were carried out in 2025 and 2026 during the two wars of aggression conducted by Washington and Tel Aviv.
​
The Iranian diplomat dismissed claims of nuclear activity at Kolang Gaz La Mountain as media hype, saying they have no basis.
​
Baqaei said Iran had provided the IAEA with all information required under its safeguards obligations concerning the site. He noted that the location could not even be described as a nuclear facility in the safeguards sense.
​
He said the United States is using such claims as part of a media and psychological campaign against Iran, adding that despite decades of allegations about Iran’s nuclear program, the IAEA has never stated that Iran has been in non-compliance.

🔊 IAEA General Conference kicks off in Vienna with US sabotage

📌 London, IRNA – The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has begun its 70th General Conference, with the United States obstructing Iran’s participation.


Leaders, scientists and national representatives have gathered in Vienna for the IAEA conference on Monday, under a broad agenda covering the Agency’s annual report, advancing the peaceful uses of nuclear science and technology, technical cooperation, safeguards implementation, and illegal attacks on nuclear facilities.
​
Behrouz Kamalvandi— the spokesperson for the Atomic Energy Organization of Iran (AEOI) —is set to deliver an address at the conference, after Austria, under pressure from the United States, blocked Iran's nuclear chief, Mohammad Eslami, from addressing the gathering.
​
Iran had submitted a formal request to the IAEA Secretariat to include, in its agenda, the topic entitled "prohibiting all forms of attacks and threats of attack against nuclear sites and facilities that are under Agency safeguards and dedicated to peaceful purposes."
​
In an explanatory memorandum, Tehran stated that an attack or threat against safeguarded nuclear facilities poses serious risks to nuclear safety and security, undermines the credibility of the Agency’s safeguards system, and disrupts the peaceful use of nuclear energy.
​
Explaining that such actions contravene the objectives and functions set forth in the IAEA Statute, the Islamic Republic also proposed the General Conference to facilitate a review of the legal, institutional, and technical aspects of attacks on peaceful nuclear facilities.
​
The General Conference is one of the principal policy-making bodies of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). It comprises representatives from all member states and convenes annually in September at the Agency's headquarters in Vienna.

🔻 Blocking Iran’s nuclear chief from IAEA conference marks clear violation of rights: Envoy

📌 London, IRNA — Iran’s permanent representative to international organizations in Vienna, Reza Najafi, has described preventing the Atomic Energy Organization of Iran (AEOI) chief, Mohammad Eslami, from attending the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) General Conference as a clear violation of Iran’s rights.


Addressing the IAEA General Conference, Najafi protested the prevention of the Iranian vice president and AEOI head from attending the meeting, saying the move was not a minor administrative issue or a human error.
​
“This is certainly not a minor administrative issue or a human error, but a serious violation of our country’s rights as a member of this agency,” he said.
​
It came after Eslami was blocked from addressing the IAEA’s general conference in Vienna after pressure from the US government.
​
Najafi said preventing the head of Iran’s delegation from attending the conference violated the principle of sovereign equality stipulated in Article IV, paragraph C of the IAEA Statute, which guarantees equal rights for all members and their enjoyment of membership benefits.
​
He added that the move undermined Iran’s right to participate in the General Conference through a representative of its choice and weakened the privileges necessary for representatives to perform their duties under the Agency’s statute and the relevant privileges and immunities agreement.
​
Najafi also said the measure violated the host country’s obligations under the headquarters agreement between the IAEA and Austria, which requires the host country to facilitate the entry of representatives of member states into Austria and issue their visas as quickly as possible.
​
“We call on the host country to immediately remedy this breach of its obligation. We expect immediate corrective action and seek assurances that such incidents will not be repeated,” he added.
