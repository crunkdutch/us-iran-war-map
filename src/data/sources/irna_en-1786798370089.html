⚡️ Exclusive | Iran’s control of Strait of Hormuz has disrupted US calculations: Official

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s authority in managing the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted US strategic calculations, while Washington’s claims about controlling the strategic waterway reflect a reaction to Iran’s initiatives, an Iranian Foreign Ministry official says.


In an exclusive interview with IRNA on Saturday, Hossein Noushabadi, director general for parliamentary affairs and laws at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Islamic Republic of Iran, described recent statements by US President Donald Trump about the Strait of Hormuz as a reaction to Iran’s strategic plan for ensuring the waterway’s security and sustainable development.
​
Noushabadi said claims that the United States could declare the Strait of Hormuz part of its territory were inconsistent with international maritime law. He referred to the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, which establishes the rights and responsibilities of coastal states while recognizing a special legal regime for straits used for international navigation.
​
The Iranian official also highlighted the Strait of Hormuz’s longstanding historical and economic importance, noting that it connects the Persian Gulf with the Sea of Oman and international waters and serves as a major route for global energy trade.
​
He said Iran and Oman’s efforts to develop a mechanism for managing the strait during peacetime could strengthen Iran’s position.
​
Noushabadi further argued that Iran’s strategic position around the Strait, combined with the global dependence on maritime trade through the waterway, has made Hormuz a significant strategic leverage point for Tehran.

💬 Intelligence Ministry warns France over diplomats’ meddling

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s Intelligence Ministry has warned the French government that it will not allow its diplomatic personnel to engage in illegal and interventionist activities, stressing that those responsible will face an appropriate response if such conduct is repeated.


In a statement issued on Saturday, the ministry said its forces, while investigating a major foreign infiltration and interference case and carrying out a judicial order to arrest two suspects, discovered the illegal presence of two French diplomats at a secret meeting point.
​
The ministry said the two diplomats had an extensive record of violations and conduct contrary to Iranian laws and their diplomatic obligations. After their identities were confirmed, the matter was reported to Iran’s Foreign Ministry, and the diplomats were handed over to Iran’s ambassador to France in the presence of diplomatic police.
​
The ministry said that while it was investigating the suspects and examining documents and evidence seized from the meeting point, the French side launched a media campaign aimed at deflecting attention from its confirmed violations.
​
It said a preliminary examination of the documents seized during the inspection of the site revealed details of a broader project involving foreign infiltration and interference, including efforts to identify individuals, establish covert contacts inside and outside Iran, and build networks with individuals of interest to foreign parties while observing secrecy.
​
The ministry added that contracts uncovered as part of the project bore the signature of France’s former ambassador to Iran, saying the French government must answer for its illegal and interventionist activities and explain the plan.
​
It said the project constituted clear interference in Iran’s internal affairs, particularly during wartime, and a blatant violation of Iranian laws and diplomatic norms.
​
The Intelligence Ministry warned that it would not allow foreign diplomats to engage in illegal and interventionist activities in Iran, stressing that if such conduct is repeated, those responsible would face an appropriate response.

📡 Tehran, Dushanbe to ink long‑term energy cooperation agreement

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Iran and Tajikistan have reached an agreement to sign a long‑term contract for the export of petroleum products and the supply of crude oil to Tajikistan’s refineries.


Iran’s Oil Minister, Mohsen Paknejad, in his Saturday meeting with Tajikistan’s Minister of Energy and Water Resources, Daler Juma, and Minister of Transport, Azim Ibrohim, announced the finalization of the framework for a long‑term agreement between the two countries.
​
The negotiations focused on the possibility of exporting Iran’s surplus petroleum products to address some of the energy challenges faced by Tajikistan, he said.
​
The two sides, the minister said, agreed on setting up a working group to examine the technical and logistical details of the cooperation.
​
The agreement can resolve Tajikistan’s fuel challenges amid the global energy crisis and increase the volume of bilateral trade to over 800,000 metric tons, he stated.
​
Meanwhile, Juma lauded Iran’s goodwill, and said that Dushanbe’s primary objective is to increase imports of fuel products from Tehran.
​
In the meantime, Ibrohim said that the current volume of trade between the two countries is approximately 800,000 tons (less than one million tons).
​
After the working groups begin their mission, this volume will increase significantly, and the necessary logistical infrastructure for the transfer of fuel and crude oil will be provided, he noted.

🖥 Iran proposes Caspian Sea consortium to monitor falling water levels

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Head of the Ports and Maritime Organization of the Islamic Republic, Mohammad Shakibi-Nasab, says Iran has proposed forming a consortium of Caspian Sea littoral states to jointly monitor the significant decline in the sea’s water level.


Shakibi-Nasab said the issue is being pursued through economic diplomacy in cooperation with the Iranian Foreign Ministry, while the Ports and Maritime Organization is providing technical support and maintaining separate contacts with Caspian littoral states.
​
He said the falling water level has become a major concern for maritime transport and could develop into a serious new challenge for the Caspian Sea.
​
In this regard, Shakibi-Nasab said Iran has proposed that countries with ports on the Caspian Sea and those using its maritime transport capacity establish a joint specialized working group to coordinate monitoring of the trend.
​
He said the proposal has been submitted to the Foreign Ministry, adding that the organization is also pursuing the issue directly with the littoral states.
​
Meanwhile, Shakibi-Nasab said the decline in the Caspian Sea’s water level is not limited to Iran and affects all countries along the sea.
​
He said coordination with Caspian countries is underway and that the United Nations is also independently following the issue.
​
On operational measures, Shakibi-Nasab identified dredging at ports as one of the necessary steps amid the falling water level and said alternative plans and joint measures may also be needed among countries using the maritime route.
​
Russia, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan are among the Caspian countries with ports that use the sea’s maritime capacity, he said.
​
However, Shakibi-Nasab said regional cooperation has not yet fully met expectations in practical and operational terms, despite countries expressing willingness to cooperate during negotiations.
​
He said efforts are continuing because a lack of cooperation would also seriously affect the maritime businesses and transport systems of the other Caspian states.
​
Shakibi-Nasab also highlighted the importance of cooperation with Russia, saying more than 80% of Iran’s maritime exchanges through its northern ports are currently conducted via Russian ports.
​
He said the scale of these exchanges requires Iran and Russia to pursue joint plans and cooperation, warning that Russian ports would also suffer significant damage if the issue is not addressed.
