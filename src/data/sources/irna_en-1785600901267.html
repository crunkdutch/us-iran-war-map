📄 Over 2.3 million Iranians completed mandatory online registration for Arbaeen: Official

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Head of Iran’s Central Headquarters for Arbaeen Pilgrimage Ali Akbar Pourjamshidian has said that pilgrims are required to register on the Samah website, adding that more than 2.3 million people have already completed the mandatory registration.


Samah is the online platform of the Arbaeen Central Headquarters used to manage pilgrims’ travel to Iraq, where they participate in a march to the holy shrine of Imam Hossein (PBUH) in Karbala as part of rituals commemorating Arbaeen, the 40th day after the martyrdom of Imam Hossein, the third Shia Imam.
​
Speaking on national television on Tuesday evening, Pourjamshidian said that every pilgrim, whether an Iranian national or a foreigner legally residing in Iran, should register on Samah to receive insurance coverage during their trip to Iraq.
​
“So far, more than 2,350,000 people have registered on the Samah website,” he said, reiterating that registration is mandatory.
​
He also said that six official border crossings are currently operating through which pilgrims from Iran can enter neighboring Iraq for Arbaeen. They are Shalamcheh and Chazzabeh in Khuzestan Province, Mehran in Ilam Province, Khosravi in Kermanshah Province, Bashmaq in Kordestan Province, and Tamarchin in West Azarbaijan Province.
​
Arbaeen falls on August 4 this year. Every year, millions of people from different countries travel to Iraq to commemorate the religious occasion.

🎙 Lut Desert’s scientific, tourism potential highlighted on UNESCO anniversary

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Lut Desert, considered one of the hottest places on Earth, still holds many unexplored and pristine treasures. According to Iranian experts, it offers a valuable and unique potential for scientific research, education, and the development of nature-based tourism.


The four experts addressed a specialized meeting held virtually on the tenth anniversary of the inscription of the Lut Desert on the UNESCO World Heritage List on July 18. The meeting was organized by Tehran University’s International Desert Research Center and the Lut Desert World Heritage Site.
​
Nasser Mashhadi, a University of Tehran professor and geomorphology researcher, described the Lut Desert as a natural museum of wind and water processes, highlighting its unique geological features, including yardangs, known locally as kalouts, sand dunes, alluvial plains and rare armoured mud balls. He said the desert’s exceptional geomorphological diversity provides valuable opportunities for scientific research, education and the development of geotourism.
​
Hessam Ahmadi Birgani, a faculty member at the University of Urmia’s Faculty of Natural Resources, highlighted the economic and tourism potential of Iran’s sandy areas, noting that they cover about five million hectares of the country’s territory. He called for a shift in approach from viewing sand dunes as a “threat” to recognizing them as an “opportunity,” pointing to new industrial applications of sand as well as the potential of dark skies, desert silence and local community participation in developing sustainable tourism.
​
Hamidreza Naseri, a University of Tehran professor and head of the International Desert Research Center, presented the results of the first ground-based measurement of the Lut Desert’s surface temperature conducted July 18, 2022, saying that the findings confirmed satellite data and reinforced the Lut’s status as one of the hottest places on Earth and a natural laboratory for climate, geography and climate-change studies.
​
Mehran Maqsoudi, a University of Tehran professor and director of the Lut Desert World Heritage Site, highlighted the desert’s unique ecosystem, including heat- and drought-resistant plant species and animals adapted to its harsh climate, and described the Lut’s kalouts as the world’s tallest, longest and most continuous known yardangs.
​
Participants stressed the need for scientific management of tourism, empowerment of local communities, continuous monitoring of climate change and national and international cooperation to protect the world heritage site.
