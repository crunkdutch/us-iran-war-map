🖥 Iran proposes Caspian Sea consortium to monitor falling water levels

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Head of the Ports and Maritime Organization of the Islamic Republic, Mohammad Shakibi-Nasab, says Iran has proposed forming a consortium of Caspian Sea littoral states to jointly monitor the significant decline in the sea’s water level.


Shakibi-Nasab said the issue is being pursued through economic diplomacy in cooperation with the Iranian Foreign Ministry, while the Ports and Maritime Organization is providing technical support and maintaining separate contacts with Caspian littoral states.
​
He said the falling water level has become a major concern for maritime transport and could develop into a serious new challenge for the Caspian Sea.
​
In this regard, Shakibi-Nasab said Iran has proposed that countries with ports on the Caspian Sea and those using its maritime transport capacity establish a joint specialized working group to coordinate monitoring of the trend.
​
He said the proposal has been submitted to the Foreign Ministry, adding that the organization is also pursuing the issue directly with the littoral states.
​
Meanwhile, Shakibi-Nasab said the decline in the Caspian Sea’s water level is not limited to Iran and affects all countries along the sea.
​
He said coordination with Caspian countries is underway and that the United Nations is also independently following the issue.
​
On operational measures, Shakibi-Nasab identified dredging at ports as one of the necessary steps amid the falling water level and said alternative plans and joint measures may also be needed among countries using the maritime route.
​
Russia, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan are among the Caspian countries with ports that use the sea’s maritime capacity, he said.
​
However, Shakibi-Nasab said regional cooperation has not yet fully met expectations in practical and operational terms, despite countries expressing willingness to cooperate during negotiations.
​
He said efforts are continuing because a lack of cooperation would also seriously affect the maritime businesses and transport systems of the other Caspian states.
​
Shakibi-Nasab also highlighted the importance of cooperation with Russia, saying more than 80% of Iran’s maritime exchanges through its northern ports are currently conducted via Russian ports.
​
He said the scale of these exchanges requires Iran and Russia to pursue joint plans and cooperation, warning that Russian ports would also suffer significant damage if the issue is not addressed.

💬 Iran urges ICRC to pursue case of three fighter pilots captured by Qatar

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Brigadier General Mohammad Bagherzadeh, commander of the Iranian Armed Forces General Staff’s committee for missing personnel, has urged the president of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) to follow up on the case of three Iranian pilots held in Qatar.


General Bagherzadeh, in a letter addressed to Mirjana Spoljaric Egger, stated that credible information indicates three Iranian pilots—who went missing during an operation in response to US-Israeli attacks on the Islamic Republic—were captured alive by Qatari forces.
​
The letter identified the pilots as Javad Salehi, Abdolmajid Dashtian, and Omran Behraveshian, saying that they were on a combat mission following "the cowardly attacks by the terrorist army of the United States and the illegitimate and usurping Zionist regime" on Iran on February 28, 2026, launched from bases in Arab countries located on the southern shores of the Persian Gulf.
​
According to the text, one of the four pilots on a combat mission targeting an enemy military base in Qatar was martyred, while the others remained in Qatar after their fighter jets were hit and they were forced to eject.
​
Stating that the heroic Air Force of the Islamic Republic of Iran responded to the enemy based on the principle of defending Iranian territorial integrity, the letter accused the Qatari government of ignoring and violating the inalienable rights of Iranian prisoners over the past six months, contrary to all international laws and regulations, including the Geneva Conventions.
​
Despite all diplomatic and follow-up efforts, Doha has not allowed the prisoners to meet with, interview, or contact their families and authorities, the Iranian commander complained, and urged the ICRC to take the matter into account.
​
Recalling Articles 70 and 78 of the Third Geneva Convention, the letter stated that the families of prisoners of war and the ICRC headquarters must be informed about their captivity, and that PoWs have the right to submit complaints and requests to representatives—including those of the Red Cross—who visit their camps, or to hold discussions and negotiations.
​
The Iranian Armed Forces General Staff's committee for missing personnel, while expressing grave concern about the situation of the captured pilots in Qatar, has requested that high-ranking ICRC officials conduct interviews with the Iranian pilots at the earliest opportunity, in line with their humanitarian missions and bilateral cooperation, and inform the Islamic Republic of their health status.

📡 Araghchi urges provinces to expand economic ties with neighboring states amid sanctions

📌 TEHRAN, IRNA — Foreign Minister Seyyed Abbas Araghchi highlights the economic potential of Iran’s neighboring countries, including Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, and Pakistan, urging greater use of regional trade and cooperation to support economic development amid the United States’ cruel sanctions.


“Our neighbors are the biggest opportunity now in our foreign policy and economic cooperation,” Araghchi said at a specialized meeting with tourism and handicrafts investors in Razavi Khorasan Province.
​
The meeting was attended by Minister of Cultural Heritage, Tourism and Handicrafts Seyed Reza Salehi-Amiri, Minister of Economic Affairs and Finance Seyed Ali Madani, Razavi Khorasan Governor Gholamhossein Mozaffari, Ali-Asghar Shalbafian, head of the Investment and Economic Affairs Center of the Ministry of Cultural Heritage, Tourism and Handicrafts, the head of the Razavi Khorasan Chamber of Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture, and a number of investors and senior officials.
​
Iran’s top diplomat stressed the need to remove domestic obstacles, expand provincial authority, facilitate private-sector activity, and strengthen economic engagement with neighboring countries.
​
On provincial decision-making, Araghchi said that the president has emphasized increasing the authorities of the provinces and has a serious motivation for this issue so that problems can be resolved at the provincial level, with greater speed and more precise understanding.
​
Turning to Razavi Khorasan’s regional position, Araghchi highlighted the province’s potential for engagement with Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, and Pakistan, adding, “Razavi Khorasan Province has the necessary authorities to develop trade relations and travel with neighboring countries, and coordination with the Foreign Ministry’s representation in the province can help facilitate affairs.”
​
He added that the duty of the Foreign Ministry’s representation in Razavi Khorasan is to facilitate the province’s relations with neighboring countries, and this capacity should be used more than before to promote the province’s economic, commercial, and tourism development.
​
In another part of his remarks, Araghchi addressed health tourism between Iran and Afghanistan and added that the Ministry of Foreign Affairs has no obstacle to issuing visas for Afghan nationals and is ready to facilitate the visa issuance process for genuine applicants, particularly health tourists.
