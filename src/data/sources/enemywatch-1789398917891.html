🇮🇳 | Indian regime demolishes a 150-year-old mosque in Sarahanpur city, Uttar Pradesh, one of India’s most densely populated states, amid controversy over the legality and timing of the action:

The mosque, located inside the Saharanpur Collectorate complex, was demolished on September 5, days after a district court dismissed an appeal against an earlier eviction order. The September 2 court ruling upheld the eviction order but did not expressly direct the demolition of the structure. Mosque authorities have therefore questioned the legal basis and timing of the demolition, while the administration maintains that it was acting in accordance with the court’s ruling.

Opponents of the demolition argue that the operation was an illegal, discriminatory, and politically motivated act designed to communalize the state ahead of the 2027 Assembly elections. The mosque was a 115-to-150-year-old pre-independence structure registered with the Waqf Board, making it protected under the Places of Worship Act, 1991, and long-standing "waqf by user" principles. Legal representatives point out serious procedural flaws: the Waqf Board was never made a party to the proceedings, revenue records still list the land under private zamindari names rather than government ownership, and the magistrate's order directed eviction rather than explicit demolition. By carrying out the pre-dawn blitz under cover of darkness just three days after the district court verdict, the administration bypassed standard appeal windows, effectively denying the mosque committee their constitutional right to seek relief from higher courts. Furthermore, heavy-handed tactics such as restricting access to the site and placing Muslim leaders like MP Iqra Hasan under house arrest were condemned as an autocratic suppression of democratic oversight and public representation.
