Speaking in a televised interview on Sunday night, Baqaei said that discussions between Tehran and Muscat over the future of the Strait of Hormuz had been underway for some time, with the aim of reaching a specific framework to ensure safe maritime navigation in the area.
​
“Under no circumstances will the Strait of Hormuz return to the situation before the war (February 28),” Baqaei said.
​
He explained that Iran and Oman, as the two coastal states of the Strait of Hormuz, have a shared responsibility to cooperate on maritime security and safe navigation. According to him, talks between the two countries began immediately after the ceasefire on April 8, with several rounds of exchanges taking place.
​
Baqaei noted that the first foreign trip by Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi after the war was to Oman, where discussions with the Sultan of Oman and the country’s foreign minister included the issue of the Strait of Hormuz and the establishment of a secure maritime route.
​
The Iranian diplomat also referred to the provisions of the memorandum of understanding on ending the war, stating that under its fifth clause, the future management of the Strait of Hormuz should be carried out by Iran in consultation with Oman and through dialogue with regional countries.
​
“During the first 22 or 23 days of implementing the agreement, the northern route of the Strait was completely secure and ships were passing through it. ​
Baqaei added that the future agreed route in the Strait of Hormuz would be neither the current northern route nor the current southern route, but rather a new route to be determined through an agreement between Iran and Oman.
​
He emphasized that Tehran’s talks with Muscat are bilateral in nature and do not concern any third party.
​
“We must ensure that the Strait of Hormuz cannot be misused by parties that have committed aggression against Iran. This requires a mechanism in which Iran’s sovereign rights are fully respected,” Baqaei said.
​
In another part of his remarks, the Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson addressed threats by US officials against Iran, saying such statements were not new and that Tehran had already faced military actions.

Baqaei stressed that Iran’s decisions are based on national interests and security considerations and would not be influenced by threats, intimidation, or media pressure.

🌍 Ashura-inspired symphony returns to Iran’s stage after 23 years

📌 Tehran, IRNA – “Khosouf” (Eclipse), a symphonic musical narration of the martyrdom of Imam Hossein (PBUH) at Ashura, is set to return to the stage after a 23-year absence, coinciding with the commemoration of Arbaeen, one of the largest annual religious observances in the Islamic world.


The Tehran Symphony Orchestra will perform the acclaimed work on August 6 and 7 at Tehran’s Vahdat Hall under guest conductor Arash Amini, accompanied by a choir led by Behrang Shegarfkar and vocalist Hadi Feyzabadi.
​
Composed by Mohammad-Saeed Sharifian, “Khosouf” is regarded as one of the landmark works of contemporary Iranian symphonic music. Inspired by the events of Ashura and the epic of Karbala, the composition blends orchestral and choral music to convey themes of sacrifice, grief, faith, freedom, and the pursuit of truth. The work incorporates nine traditional Ashura lamentation melodies selected after months of research into classical literary texts and mourning chants.
​
The symphony was first staged in 2003 to mark Arbaeen, with performances by the Tehran Symphony Orchestra alongside Romanian choir members. It was later invited to Romania, where it was performed by the Oltenia Philharmonic Orchestra and broadcast live on national radio. A studio recording was released several years later.
​
Amini described “Khosouf” as “one of the greatest and most significant musical works produced after the Islamic Revolution,” noting that repeated attempts to revive it over the years had failed because of its technical complexity and the extensive orchestral, choral, and rehearsal requirements.
​
He said the Rudaki Foundation’s support this year made the production possible after months of preparation and more than 20 rehearsal sessions.
​
Recalling the symphony’s original performances, Amini said today’s orchestra members are “far more accomplished” than two decades ago, citing the younger generation’s technical proficiency and broader exposure to international musical developments.
​
He added that the composer has personally supervised rehearsals to ensure the performance remains faithful to the original score.

📄 Iran, Oman negotiating temporary Hormuz shipping corridor as talks continue

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran is negotiating with Oman to establish a temporary shipping corridor in the Strait of Hormuz that would serve as a single two-way route rather than multiple separate lanes, Iranian Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei says.


Speaking at a press briefing on Monday, Baqaei said Iran accepted responsibility under the June 18 memorandum ending the recent conflict to help manage future navigation in the strategic waterway. He said Tehran immediately began efforts to restore maritime traffic to pre-war levels within a 30-day timeframe.
​
According to Baqaei, those efforts were disrupted after the United States violated the agreement by launching military attacks against Iran before the deadline expired and reinstating an unlawful maritime blockade and restrictions on Iranian oil exports.
​
Baqaei said negotiations have focused on replacing separate northern and southern routes with a single intermediate corridor designed to accommodate the interests and security concerns of both coastal states.
​
He argued that the southern route through Omani waters, promoted by Washington after the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, contradicted the memorandum and created environmental and national security concerns. He also noted that some vessels using the route transported military equipment, contrary to the understanding that the corridor should facilitate only commercial shipping.
​
The proposed interim corridor would remain in use until Iran and Oman agree on a permanent replacement for the existing international Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS).
​
Responding to questions about possible transit fees and whether the negotiations could evolve into broader diplomatic engagement, Baqaei said the current talks are strictly focused on maritime navigation. He added that it remains too early to determine whether they could contribute to wider regional de-escalation.
​
He stressed that issues related to sovereignty, national security, sovereign rights and maritime services would be fully considered in any eventual agreement.
​
Baqaei also welcomed positive reactions from some regional countries, saying Iran and Oman had exchanged maps and conducted technical reviews with relevant specialists over the past week. He urged regional and extra-regional actors to support Iran's responsible approach and allow the negotiations to proceed constructively.
​
He also expressed concern over Iraqi reports alleging that individuals linked to Ukraine had been arrested for sabotage activities targeting Iraqi oil facilities, warning that any attempt to conduct "false flag" operations in the region would threaten collective security.
​
Reaffirming Iran's close consultations with neighboring states, Baqaei said regional security is indivisible and argued that instability affecting one country inevitably impacts the entire region.
​
The spokesperson further said that Iran remains deeply skeptical of developments involving the Israeli regime, arguing that past experience has shown the regime has consistently failed to honor its commitments and agreements.

📡 Industry minister travels to Pakistan for trade, free trade talks

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Minister of Industry, Mine and Trade, Mohammad Atabak, has traveled to Islamabad, leading a high-level delegation of government officials and private sector representatives for talks aimed at expanding economic, trade and industrial cooperation.


Starting on Monday, the two-day official visit includes participation in the 10th meeting of the Iran–Pakistan Joint Trade Committee and a meeting with Pakistan’s prime minister.
​
The agenda covers 19 key issues, including finalizing the text of a free trade agreement between the two neighboring countries, establishing a barter mechanism, and reviewing bilateral trade agreements.
​
The two sides are also expected to discuss easing transit barriers and facilitating the movement of Iranian containers through Pakistani ports.
​
The talks will address plans to develop border infrastructure at the Gabd–Rimdan crossing, create a new road to the Pakistani border, and improve cross-border road transport.
​
The discussions will also focus on maritime and rail connectivity, including linking the Gwadar maritime border with Rimdan and expanding the rail corridor between the ports of Chabahar and Gwadar.
​
Other issues include promoting joint industrial production, particularly in textiles, resolving banking and financial obstacles facing traders, facilitating business visas and activating joint border markets as part of efforts to advance a bilateral trade roadmap targeting $10 billion in annual trade.
