📡 Iran’s Rimdan Free Zone targets $8 billion in annual trade: Official

📌 Chabahar, IRNA – Iran’s Rimdan Free Zone is emerging as a major trade and transit hub in the country’s southeast, with plans to attract major investment in 88 projects and raise annual trade through the border crossing to $7.5-8 billion.


Located in Sistan and Baluchestan Province near Pakistan, Rimdan has strategic access to the markets of Pakistan and India, and is relatively close to Pakistan’s Gwadar port. Its location also offers potential links to the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC).
​
Mohammad-Saeed Arbabi, head of the Chabahar Free Zone Organization, told IRNA that Rimdan had handled nearly 320,000 tons of goods and nearly 13,000 truck movements during the first four months of the current Iranian year, which began on March 21.
​
The trade included 144,296 tons of exports to Pakistan, 163,064 tons of imports from Pakistan and 12,638 tons of transit goods destined for other countries, he added.
​
Arbabi said the ultimate goal was to increase traffic to 1,000 trucks a day, potentially raising annual trade to as much as $8 billion based on 300 working days a year.
​
He said Rimdan was being developed beyond a traditional border crossing into an integrated commercial and logistics center, with plans for warehouses, cold-storage facilities, an international terminal, a logistics park, processing and manufacturing units, and customs infrastructure.
​
The official said 201.01 trillion rials in investment had been approved for 88 projects in areas including food processing, logistics, storage, petroleum products, LPG, energy and infrastructure. Investment contracts worth 25.4 trillion rials had also been signed during the first four months of the year.
​
Rimdan officially began operating as a detached part of the Chabahar Free Zone in March 2026. Development plans include expanding trade, transit, storage, processing, energy and logistics services.
​
Arbabi said the zone could provide Iran with a shorter and more economical land route to Pakistan and help expand access to the country’s market. It could also facilitate access to India’s 1.4-billion population and strengthen Iran’s links with CPEC.
​
Among the key measures sought to accelerate development are establishing a modern joint customs facility, allowing Iranian trucks to travel to Gwadar, upgrading Rimdan customs to 24-hour operations, developing a dedicated truck route to Pakistan, and accelerating construction of the Chabahar-Rimdan railway, he noted.
​
The railway, if completed, could provide a direct link between Chabahar, Rimdan and the CPEC, strengthening Iran’s role in regional trade and transit, the official said.

It called on people from all walks of life to attend ceremonies marking the 40th day since the burial of the martyred Leader.

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iranian Ambassador to Mexico Abolfazl Pasandideh has described Fidel Castro as a symbol of a nation’s determination to freely and independently shape its own destiny.


Pasandideh made the remarks during a ceremony marking the 100th anniversary of Castro’s birth in Puebla.
​
The event was attended by Aquiles Cordova, leader of the Torchbearers Movement, members of the group and about 2,000 Mexican social and political activists and residents.
​
Pasandideh paid tribute to Castro’s historical legacy, describing him as a symbol of the will of a people that chose to determine its own destiny independently and freely.
​
Referring to the history of struggles for independence and national sovereignty in Latin America and Mexico, the Iranian ambassador said Castro was not merely a political leader in Cuba’s history but had become a symbol of a nation’s determination to shape its destiny independently and freely.
​
Pasandideh also expressed respect for the Cuban people and criticized sanctions and economic pressure imposed on the country, stressing that ordinary people should not become victims of political disputes and economic pressure policies.
​
Turning to Iran’s experience with sanctions and external pressure, Pasandideh said: “The Iranian nation has resisted the pressures and sanctions of the system of domination over the past 47 years and has also stood firm against foreign aggression in recent months.”
​
His remarks were met with applause and support from the audience.
​
Addressing the Cuban people, he said: “You are not alone and the free and freedom-loving nations of the world stand alongside the Cuban people and in defense of peace, justice, human dignity, the independence of nations and a world free of sanctions and coercion.”
​
Pasandideh’s remarks were repeatedly met with applause and chants of “Viva Irán!” from the audience, turning the ceremony into an expression of solidarity with the Iranian people.
​
The Torchbearers Movement has previously expressed solidarity with Iran.
​
On April 11, 2026, amid the US and Israeli regime aggression against Iran, members of the movement joined other Mexican social and political groups in a rally of about 1,000 people outside the Iranian Embassy in Mexico City.
​
Participants carried flags and placards reading “Mexico is with Iran” and “Yes to peace, no to war,” while condemning the attacks against Iran and commemorating the martyred children of the Shajareh Tayyebeh School in Minab.
​
Abel Perez Zamorano, one of the movement’s leaders, also stressed the group’s “complete and fraternal” solidarity with the Iranian people at the rally.
​
Mexico, world should learn from Iran’s resistance
​
At the Puebla ceremony, Aquiles Cordova, leader of the Torchbearers Movement, referred to Iran’s resistance to external pressure and aggression and said Mexico and the world should learn a lesson in resistance from the Iranian people.
​
Cordova referred to the Iranian people’s 168-day resistance against the United States, calling it part of a struggle to defend humanity.
​
He said the Iranian people had shown that it was possible to withstand pressure from major powers.
​
Cordova also paid tribute to Fidel Castro, saying history would never forget him because he had always stood alongside his people.
​
He described Castro as a leader who took up arms alongside his people and fought until victory, and who after the revolution relied on the Cuban people to take the revolution into a new phase.
