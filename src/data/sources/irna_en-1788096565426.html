⚡️ Iran moves to establish joint free trade zone with Turkey in Khoy

📌 Khoy, IRNA – Iran has completed the necessary legal and administrative steps to establish a joint free trade zone with Turkey in Khoy, northwestern Iran, and is now awaiting further action from the Turkish side to advance and finalize the project, an Iranian official has said.


Hossein Forouzan, secretary of Iran’s Supreme Council of Free Trade-Industrial and Special Economic Zones, made the remarks during a visit to Khoy on Saturday, highlighting the city’s significant border, economic and transportation potential.
​
Forouzan said the proposed joint free zone has entered the legal process following negotiations between the two countries, adding that the Iranian government has also approved the project. He said implementation will proceed under a bilateral memorandum of understanding.
​
He emphasized the strategic importance of the Razi border crossing, describing it as a major route for trade between Iran and Turkey. He said developing the area’s supporting infrastructure and establishing a logistics hinterland are essential for expanding commercial activity.
​
During his visit, Forouzan reviewed plans for a 50-hectare site in Ivughli designated for customs facilities and a logistics hinterland. He also visited Khoy’s industrial and transportation facilities, including its airport.
​
The official said the proposed joint free zone would complement existing economic capacities in West Azarbaijan Province rather than weaken other free zones.
​
Khoy Governor Ali Alaei said the city’s road, rail, air and customs infrastructure provides strong potential for expanding economic exchanges with Turkey. He also highlighted the Razi border, Khoy airport and logistics facilities as key assets for regional development.

🔊 Iran’s diplomacy supports national interests alongside military front: Ex-CBI governor

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s Foreign Ministry, under the administration of President Masoud Pezeshkian, has kept the country’s diplomatic front active under exceptionally difficult conditions, while building an effective negotiation network and helping shape the Islamabad memorandum of understanding between Tehran and Washington, according to former Central Bank of Iran Governor Mohammad-Hossein Adeli.


In an exclusive Persian-language article for IRNA, published on Sunday that marked the last say of the National Government Week, Adeli said the Foreign Ministry had played a significant role alongside the military front in protecting Iran’s national interests and security during a period marked by intense military and political tensions.
​
He said maintaining diplomacy during wartime was particularly difficult, as negotiations faced pressure at home and abroad. Despite these challenges, Iran’s diplomatic apparatus continued consultations, meetings and negotiations with various parties before, during and after recent military confrontations, the expert noted.
​
Adeli also highlighted the establishment of an active negotiation network through Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi’s contacts with counterparts in countries including Oman, Pakistan, Turkey, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, and other regional states, as well as Iran’s allies and members of the Axis of Resistance.
​
He said maintaining these channels of communication had helped preserve opportunities for dialogue and could contribute to a path toward peace and Iran’s strategic objectives.
​
Adeli further described the Islamabad memorandum as a significant diplomatic achievement, arguing that its provisions reflected Iran’s position in negotiations with its adversaries.
​
He said the document could serve as a basis for future negotiations and praised the Foreign Ministry for helping create the conditions that led to the agreement.
​
Overall, Adeli said, Iran’s diplomacy had remained active despite severe political and military pressures.
