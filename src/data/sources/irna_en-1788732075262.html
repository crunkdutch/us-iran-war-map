🔊 IRGC denies reports of attack on nuclear facilities in Isfahan

📌 Isfahan, IRNA – The Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) in Isfahan province has denied reports of US-Israeli attacks against nuclear facilities in the central province.


The IRGC’s public relations office said in a statement on Saturday that reports circulating on social media about an attack on nuclear centers and facilities in Isfahan were “completely false” and that no such attack had taken place.
​
The statement said the reports were being circulated during a sensitive period in an effort to create tension, disturb public opinion and undermine people’s psychological calm.
​
It said the claims were being spread through social media by republishing reports without reliable sources.
​
The IRGC urged people to obtain information about security conditions and possible incidents only from official sources and credible media outlets.
​
It also called on the public to refrain from circulating unverified reports, old images, and information from unreliable sources.

🔊 Martyr Imam Khamenei a benchmark for distinguishing right from wrong: Ghalibaf

📌 Tehran, IRNA — Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says that “jihadi management,” a service-oriented approach in Iran, was a core mission of the martyred Leader of the Islamic Revolution Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, whose relationship with officials and forces transcended organizational hierarchy in the country.


In a note titled “A Soldier's Account of the Commander-in-Chief” published in the special edition of the monthly cultural and artistic magazine Sarv, while remembering the wisdom and ideals of the martyred Leader, Ghalibaf called Ayatollah Khamenei “a criterion” for distinguishing right from wrong on the most difficult paths.
​
Stating that a relationship beyond organizational hierarchy between a commander and his force is formed based on trust, faith, and experience, he said the late Leader had acquired such a position because he believed in his personnel, understood their pain and suffering, and stood with them.
​
He said the mourning for the martyred Leader was not only an emotional matter; rather, the legacy Ayatollah Khamenei left behind constantly reminds the Iranian people of the “loss of a unique gem.”
​
Calling himself a student of Ayatollah Khamenei, Ghalibaf said he had the opportunity—while serving the nation in different positions, from the field of Sacred Defense to executive and managerial responsibilities—to observe the Leader's vision and wisdom, which were firmly woven into his human emotion.
​
A good commander is not defined only by the concept of power, but he must also be “beloved” by his forces, he said, adding that Imam Khamenei's kindness, sharing, understanding of subordinates' pain, and standing by them made him unique.
​
The top parliamentarian also pointed to the late Leader's decision-making skills in difficult moments, saying that one should not back down from principles even a little, no matter how much pressure one is under. “He was a teacher of realistic idealism,” he said. “He never allowed idealism to turn into fantasy and never accepted that realism be reduced to conservatism.”
​
According to him, the concept of “jihadi management” had a special place in Ayatollah Khamenei's thought, as he believed that the popular legitimacy of the Islamic establishment could not be maintained by mere slogans. Rather, the people must feel the effects of the revolution—they must feel justice, see progress, and experience services in their daily lives.
​
Recounting his role as Mayor of Tehran, Ghalibaf said the Leader's emphasis on the jihadi spirit was a great spiritual support for him. When the Leader of the Revolution spoke about serving the people, he was actually drawing a management model—a model centered on the people, he added.
​
In his view, jihadi management was a “lifestyle,” he continued, saying that the Leader wanted the manager to consider service, not the desk, as the goal, and that there should be solutions to problems instead of justifications and excuses.
​
He explained that such belief was rooted in Ayatollah Khamenei's deep understanding of the Iranian nation, not in a political slogan, and that through this perspective he repeatedly led the country through crises at critical points in the aftermath of the Islamic Revolution.
​
“I must mention another characteristic of the Leader of the Revolution—that was emotion and kindness,” Ghalibaf said, adding that his speeches showed his powerful and steadfast face, but those who met and spoke with him closely knew that Ayatollah Khamenei had a heart full of love for the people, the families of martyrs, veterans, citizens, and those who served the country.

🔻 Iran condemns US attacks on oil tankers as violation of international law

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Iranian Foreign Ministry has issued a strong condemnation of the attacks by the US on its commercial shipping fleet, saying they represent a clear violation of international law.


The foreign ministry said in a statement on Saturday that the attacks overnight on oil tankers anchored in Iranian waters were a continuation of the US war of aggression on Iran and its blockade on the country's maritime trade.
​
It said the attacks "constitute a clear violation of Paragraph 4 of Article 2 of the United Nations Charter, constitute a war crime, and represent an overt threat to international peace and the security of commercial shipping."
​
The statement said Iran is determined to decisively defend its sovereignty and national interests against the US's state terrorism and its aggressive actions, including its economic war that has manifested itself in the continued naval blockade and harassment of commercial vessels.
​
It said the US regime and its allies and partners will be responsible for the consequences arising from Washington's continued hostile and interventionist actions in the region.
​
The US military claimed in a statement on Saturday that it had attacked one Iranian oil tanker near the island of Kharg in the Persian Gulf, and two others in the Sea of Oman.

💬 Statue honoring iconic Iranian artist Akbar Abdi unveiled

📌 Tehran, IRNA – A memorial ceremony marking the 40th day since the death of acclaimed Iranian actor Akbar Abdi was held in the presence of fellow artists, friends, colleagues and family members, culminating in the unveiling of a statue honoring him.


The ceremony, held Saturday evening, featured figures including Iraj Tahmasb, Hamid Jebeli, Farhad Aslani, Kianoush Ayari, Mohammadreza Sharifinia, Mahmoud Kalari, Alireza Khamseh and Salar Aghili. The program opened with Quranic recitation by Hamed Shakernejad and was hosted by Amir Yal Arjmand.
​
Actress Akram Mohammadi recalled Abdi’s distinctive humor and his enduring place in people’s memories, highlighting their collaboration in the acclaimed film “Mother,” produced about 37 years ago.
​
Director Behrouz Afkhami said Abdi should not be judged merely by his box-office record or roles, emphasizing his generosity and charitable nature.
​
Director Kianoush Ayari described Abdi as a figure who had become part of Iranians’ collective memory through his diverse performances and deep understanding of Iranian society.
​
Abdi’s wife said she still could not believe he was gone, recalling four decades of marriage and his kindness toward family and others.
​
Friend Shahram Zamani proposed establishing a comedy festival in Abdi’s name to discover and support emerging talents, with his statue presented to winners.
