🇾🇪 💡 | Diversion front south of Hodeidah: Sana’a forces approach Mokha:

Over the past two days, Sana’a forces have made further advances on the western fronts of Taiz Governorate in southwestern Yemen, bringing them close to the junction of the strategic city of Mokha, overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

Despite attempts by Saudi-backed factions to retake some areas on the fronts of Al-Kadaha, Maqbanah, Jabal Habashi, and Al-Ma’afer, Ansar Allah has maintained its military gains achieved late last week and strengthened them with further advances after heavy fighting with those factions.

One of the results of these battles has been Sana’a forces gaining control over military supply routes between the western Taiz fronts and the western coast, while also establishing fire control over the main supply route linking Aden and Taiz. This followed their advance in the Al-Bireen area of Al-Ma’afer District, southwestern Taiz. The development has raised concerns that the various fronts in western and southern rural Taiz could collapse in the coming days.

The most significant battlefield development was recorded on the Al-Barh axis, southwest of Taiz, where Sana’a forces advanced westward and approached the Al-Rashah Al-A’la area. They also captured important positions around Al-Arish, located about 5 kilometers east of the Mokha junction and about 7 kilometers from Khalid bin Al-Walid Camp, one of the main defensive lines protecting Mokha, the principal stronghold of Tariq Saleh’s forces.

Ansar Allah-affiliated platforms released new footage yesterday evening showing further significant advances across the Maqbanah fronts, including the capture of Hamir Market and other areas east of Jabal Habashi District in western Taiz.

These developments prompted Saudi-backed factions to attempt opening other fronts south of Hodeidah and around Marib in an effort to ease the military pressure on the western Taiz fronts.

The attempts came amid mutual accusations among the factions over responsibility for the military setbacks that led to the loss of several areas overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait. Saudi Arabia has sought to control these areas at any cost because of their strategic importance for securing international shipping in the Red Sea and the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

While Saudi-backed factions said they had fully captured Hays District in southern Hodeidah, despite the fact that Hays city has been under the control of the Aden government for years, the announcement appeared intended to divert attention from the complete loss of the Al-Kadaha front and other western Taiz fronts to Sana’a forces the previous evening.

Al-Kadaha holds strategic importance due to its geographic position between Jabal Habashi, Maqbanah, and Al-Wazi’iyah districts in the governorate’s countryside, as well as its location on one of the fronts overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

According to local and military sources who spoke to Al-Akhbar, Saudi-backed factions issued several directives ordering their fighters not to use the Taiz-Mokha road, the main artery for their supply movements, whether coming from the Taiz axis or the western coast. This amounts to an implicit acknowledgment of Sana’a forces’ influence over the route.

Contrary to Saudi media reports about “victories” on the Jabal Habashi and Haifan fronts south of Taiz, informed sources confirmed to Al-Akhbar that the areas of Al-Mukazamah and Akad, both within Jabal Habashi District, had fallen under Ansar Allah’s control. All attempts by the factions to retake the strategic Jabal Nu’man and nearby positions also failed.

Meanwhile, Sana’a forces continued advancing toward Al-Bireen, which overlooks major supply routes linking Taiz and Aden. According to the sources, the advance enabled Sana’a forces yesterday to establish complete fire control over the most important main supply routes connecting Aden and Taiz, potentially reducing the military maneuvering options available to Saudi-backed factions on the western Taiz fronts.

According to the information released, Dive-LD is one of the newest autonomous underwater systems in the U.S. Navy’s fleet and is designed for long-endurance underwater missions without a crew onboard.
