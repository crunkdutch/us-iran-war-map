🇮🇷 | How does the Leader of the Revolution Imam Mujtaba Khamenei view the ceasefire negotiations?

1. The wisdom behind not publicly addressing the details of the negotiations

The first point is that the Leader has not publicly commented on the substance of the negotiations, their requirements, red lines, or conditions. He has not, in specific terms, instructed what should or should not be done during the negotiations, nor has he publicly evaluated the negotiating team’s performance.

His decision not to comment on the negotiators themselves, their conduct, or the technical and substantive aspects of the talks reflects a profound wisdom. According to the statement, this approach is intended to prevent controversial technical and political issues from being turned into public disputes in society or on social media.

2. The negotiating table is an extension of the battlefield

The second point concerns two statements previously made by the Leader regarding the negotiations.

First, he told the public: “Your voices in the public squares influence the outcome of the negotiations.”

Second, at the conclusion of the same message, he invoked Imam al-Mahdi (a.j.), asking for prayers for victory in both “the military battlefield” and “the battlefield of negotiations.”

According to the statement, this demonstrates that negotiations themselves are another arena of struggle, not something opposed to resistance.

3. The correct interpretation of “your voices influence the negotiations”

The statement argues that some have incorrectly interpreted the Leader’s remarks as permission to direct criticism and public pressure against domestic officials or the negotiating team under the pretext of strengthening the system.

It argues that this interpretation contradicts the Leader’s Qur’anic approach, citing the verse:

“Strong against the disbelievers, merciful among themselves.”

It also recalls the position of Imam Khomeini and the Martyred Leader, noting that before the twelve-day war the latter stated:

“We are pessimistic toward the enemy, but optimistic toward our own forces.”

According to the statement, this distrust is directed toward the enemy’s history of arrogance and breaking agreements, not toward Iran’s negotiating team.

—A constructive interpretation

The statement says the correct understanding is that public participation, support for the Islamic system, and backing the negotiating team strengthen Iran’s position.

Such public support is said to give negotiators greater political confidence and enable them to resist excessive demands from opposing parties.

4. Understanding the Leader’s statements as a whole

The statement emphasizes that the Leader’s remarks should not be viewed in isolation but understood as part of a broader framework.

It notes that one of his repeated themes has been preserving national unity and cohesion, quoting his statement that:

“Unity between the people, the government, and the institutions of the Islamic Republic is a blessing.”

It argues that even legitimate disagreements should not be allowed to undermine that unity.

5. Belief in the Leader’s authority

The statement asks whether people view the Leader as a capable, prudent, and fully informed authority over military and diplomatic affairs, or as someone influenced by others.

It argues that those who truly believe in his authority should not assume that individuals or political factions can impose decisions contrary to Iran’s national interests upon him.

6. The mission of the Guardian Jurist

According to the statement, the foremost responsibility of the Guardian Jurist (Wilayat al-Faqih) is to safeguard the country’s principles, strategic direction, and national interests.

It argues that if negotiations were to deviate from those principles or amount to compromise with what it describes as the “Great Satan,” the Leader would be religiously obligated to intervene and prevent such a course.

🇮🇷 | Mehdi Mohammadi, the strategic adviser to the head of Iran’s negotiating team, has revealed details of the proposed Iran-U.S. memorandum of understanding:

The first phase includes a complete halt to military operations against Iran and Lebanon, along with a commitment to prevent any new military action. The United States would also be required to provide guarantees to prevent a renewed escalation.

Under the discussed framework, a portion of Iran’s frozen assets would be released at the start of the agreement’s implementation. At the same time, the process of suspending certain economic restrictions and sanctions would begin, allowing for increased economic exchanges and oil exports.

Facilitating the movement of Iranian commercial vessels and easing maritime restrictions is one of the agreement’s principal provisions. This aims to restore Iran’s maritime trade to normal conditions and remove obstacles to international shipping.

The first phase of the agreement does not include nuclear issues. According to the proposed framework, the other party must first implement and verify its initial commitments, after which negotiations on nuclear matters would proceed in subsequent phases.

The final phase envisions lifting both primary and secondary U.S. sanctions, as well as establishing mechanisms to compensate for the damage caused by the war and economic pressure, including reconstruction. This provision is regarded as one of Iran’s most important demands in the negotiations.
