🇦🇱 | Protests grow in Albania over Kushner-linked Sazan Island project:

Protests have intensified in Albania over a major real estate development linked to Jared Kushner, son-in-law of former U.S. President Donald Trump, and Ivanka Trump. Demonstrators have once again demanded the suspension of the project near the city of Zvërnec on the Adriatic coast, while also accusing the Albanian government of corruption and calling for the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama. What began as a high-profile investment proposal has increasingly evolved into a political and legal controversy. In June, Albania’s Special Anti-Corruption Prosecution Office (SPAK) launched an investigation into laws and planning decisions related to the development of a luxury resort within the protected Karaburun–Sazan Marine National Park.

Interest in Sazan Island dates back several years. In 2024, Ivanka Trump publicly stated that she and Jared Kushner were acquiring a 1,400-acre island in the Mediterranean. Subsequent reporting revealed a deal reportedly worth $1.4 billion between Kushner’s Affinity Partners and Albanian authorities. Sazan Island is not merely a tourist destination. It is a former Cold War military fortress containing thousands of bunkers, military installations, and defensive structures. Due to its location at the entrance to the Adriatic Sea and near the Strait of Otranto, the island has long held strategic military significance. Visitors have also reported the presence of warning signs for landmines and unexploded ordnance dating from previous conflicts.

The development project is reportedly valued at approximately $4 billion and involves Gulf-backed investors alongside luxury hospitality groups. Plans include eco-resorts, hotels, private villas, restaurants, entertainment facilities, and a large-scale luxury retreat in the Zvërnec area. Affinity Partners, Kushner’s investment firm, manages substantial capital originating from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. Reports have also noted that Kushner first became interested in Sazan Island while sailing aboard the yacht of financier Nathaniel Rothschild, further drawing attention to the network of influential political, financial, and business figures associated with the project.

Critics argue that the combination of strategic geography, extensive bunker infrastructure, private ownership, and elite financial backing raises broader questions about the project’s long-term purpose and implications. Some commentators have speculated that the island could eventually function as a highly secure private refuge for wealthy elites, similar to the survivalist compounds and fortified retreats increasingly discussed in elite circles. Others have drawn parallels between the networks surrounding the project and broader transnational relationships linking Gulf investors, political dynasties, major financial families, and influential international actors. While many of these claims remain speculative, they have contributed to growing public suspicion and helped fuel the protests currently unfolding in Albania.
