⚡️ Enemy Media - Haaretz Analysis:
Netanyahu faces two main political paths. The first is to officially accept the Trump plan, then work to delay its implementation and add new conditions to it, thereby preserving his coalition and avoiding a direct confrontation with his far-right partners.
This scenario aligns with the position of National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, who rejected the draft agreement and demanded continued assassinations in Gaza and "encouraging emigration," which would make any withdrawal or cessation of attacks a point of conflict within Netanyahu's camp.
The second path is for Netanyahu to decide to proceed with the agreement, even at the temporary expense of his partners, if he sees in it an opportunity to achieve broader political progress, especially opening horizons for the normalization process with Saudi Arabia, and presenting himself before the elections as a statesman capable of transforming the war into regional arrangements.
In this scenario, Netanyahu might bet that the relationship with Trump is electorally more important than fully maintaining the positions of Ben-Gvir and his partners, especially if the plan grants him a diplomatic achievement he can market to the Israeli public.
However, this option also carries a risk; it could shake the stability of the coalition and open a confrontation with parties on which the government relies to survive, while the cost of the agreement within the right-wing camp has not yet become clear.
Therefore, the question is no longer limited to Netanyahu's position on the terms of the agreement, but on how he interprets his electoral interest: Does he see that obstructing the plan protects his base and coalition, or that implementing it under Trump's patronage gives him a stronger political image before the elections?
In both cases, Trump remains the decisive factor. The success of the plan depends on his willingness to use his influence to push Netanyahu to implement it, while Netanyahu's electoral future may largely depend on his ability to maintain his relationship with the American president without losing his government partners.
