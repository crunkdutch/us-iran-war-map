⏺ Over 400 Injuries in the First Phase of the Iran War
The investigative website "War Horse," citing new Department of Defense records, has reported that between February 28 and April 8, 2026, over 400 injuries were recorded among US forces; a statistic that represents the most detailed public image released of US human losses in the first phase of the Iran War.
These documents were obtained through an official Freedom of Information Act request and include the date of injury, service branch, military rank, and, in cases related to the ground army, the type and cause of injury. At least 17 of the injuries were classified as life-threatening.
The important point is that each row of these documents represents an "injury incident"; therefore, more than one entry may have been recorded for a service member who sustained multiple types of injuries.

⚡️ 🔺Declaration of National Crisis in U.S. Blood Reserves
🔹The American Red Cross has declared a national blood supply crisis for the second time and called for immediate blood donations.
🔹According to the Red Cross, blood donations have reached their lowest level in the past four summers, which has exacerbated the blood shortage in emergencies and threatens patient care.

🔹Extreme heat, poor air quality, and the spread of foodborne illnesses have contributed to the decrease in blood donations this summer.

⚡️ 🚫Post 3 | Head Injury; The Signature Injury of the Iran War?
⚠️ At least 170 cases of head injury among American forces
⏺The most common injury recorded in U.S. Department of Defense documents is not shrapnel wounds or bone fractures, but head injury. From the beginning of the war until early April, at least 170 cases of head injury have been reported; an issue that has increased the concern of military medical specialists about blast-induced traumatic brain injury becoming the "signature injury of the Iran War."
⏺A significant portion of these injuries is likely related to the blast waves from missiles and drones. Even so-called "mild" brain injury may not be visible in initial imaging but may manifest weeks or months later as persistent headaches, memory impairment, vision problems, imbalance, speech disorders, or reduced cognitive ability.
🚫The U.S. Department of Defense was previously forced to gradually increase the number of those suffering from brain injuries after Iran's 2020 missile attack on Ain al-Asad. Now, the widespread nature of the attacks and the recording of at least 170 head injuries indicate that physical protection alone has not prevented the effects of blast waves on forces stationed at bases.
