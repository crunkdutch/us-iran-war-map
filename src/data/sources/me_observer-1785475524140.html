⚡️ Follow-up || Economic Researcher Moayad Afaneh:
• There are 13 banks in Palestine that have financial relationships with Israeli banks, while the two Israeli banks "Hapoalim" and "Discount" threaten to sever this relationship within a month or two.
• Severing this relationship would affect supply chains and commercial operations, in addition to electricity and water purchases and telecommunications services.
• 90% of Palestinian exports go to or pass through Israel, while 100% of imports enter through Israel, which makes these threats a major concern.
• Successive Palestinian governments have been slow in addressing economic issues related to Israel, as the clearing and banking relationship crises are not new.
• What the Minister of Finance spoke about represents a realistic reading of the Palestinian economy and public finances. Although citizens need to hear what alleviates their concerns, there is a pressing financial crisis for which Israel is responsible.
• The Minister of Finance spoke about a number of solutions, including expanding the scope of electronic payment and reducing reliance on cash.
• Local revenues represent the minimum available resources, and financial payments are expected to arrive from the European Union and the World Bank, in addition to what is collected locally.
• These funds are sufficient to continue paying 50% of salaries until the end of the current year, as the government needs about 650 million shekels monthly to secure this percentage.
• Complete reliance on the shekel was a strategic mistake, as there are about 34 billion shekels in Palestine, of which 17 billion shekels are surplus in banks, and 17 billion shekels are held by citizens.
• We are currently going through the most difficult financial circumstances, yet Palestinian banks are strong and enjoy high levels of hedging, according to a scientific survey, so there is no need for citizens to worry about their deposited money.
