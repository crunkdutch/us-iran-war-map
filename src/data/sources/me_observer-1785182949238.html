⚡️ 6. Final Joint Fire Operation
The exercise concluded on July 2 with the largest and most complex joint fire operation in the history of Southern Jackaroo. In this phase, tanks, artillery, combat engineers, observation teams, infantry forces, and anti-tank weapons from the three countries were employed against a common target.
The logical sequence of such an attack included the following stages (very important; noteworthy for ground force operational echelons):
Target identification and positioning;
Information transfer to fire control centers;
Execution of artillery and mortar fire to suppress positions;
Route clearing or obstacle breaching by engineers;
Advance of armored and infantry elements;
Use of machine guns and anti-tank missiles against remaining targets;
Target capture and force reorganization.
As far as I have observed, official American sources have not published the exact sequence of all firings and tactical formations; therefore, this sequence is an analytical reconstruction based on the announced components of the exercise. The definite point is that the commanders of the three countries had to manage linguistic, ammunition, communication, and fire control method differences under conditions of using live ammunition.

⚡️ 7. Strategic Objective of the Exercise
🔹The appearance of this exercise is land-based, but its strategic context is maritime and oceanic. In a potential war in those areas, the capture or defense of islands, ports, airports, coastal choke points, and suitable missile deployment positions is not possible without ground forces.
🔹The United States combined three complementary components in this exercise:
Marine Corps for expeditionary operations and combat in coastal environments;
11th Airborne Division for light infantry and operations in difficult environments;
Australian Army and Japan Ground Self-Defense Force for armored support, artillery, engineering, and operational depth.
🔹This structure is consistent with the concept of "distributed operations": small forces are deployed at various points, use shared sensors and fire, and strive to achieve local superiority over a target without long-term concentration.
🔹The presence of Japan's 7th Infantry Regiment alongside Australian armored forces and American Marines is also part of transforming the political cooperation between Tokyo, Canberra, and Washington into real combat coordination. The reciprocal access agreement between Australia and Japan has provided the legal and executive framework for these types of deployments.

⚡️ 9. Limitations and Vulnerabilities
⚠️Despite extensive publicity, Southern Jackaroo 2026 should not be considered equivalent to the capability of executing a large-scale combat operation. The most significant limitations include:
⬅️The scale of the exercise was below the level of a divisional or corps operation;
⬅️Air superiority and relatively secure training ground conditions were pre-existing;
⬅️The real threat of drones, heavy electronic warfare, and long-range enemy fire on the scale of actual war was not replicated;
⬅️The command network operated in a controlled environment;
⬅️The logistics chain was not under attack;
⬅️Multinational units still face differences in language, doctrine, ammunition, and fire control methods;
⬅️Light forces and wheeled vehicles are vulnerable to drone detection and precision fire;
⬅️The TOW team must maintain line of sight and guidance until impact;
⬅️The M777 towed artillery relies on vehicles and crew for rapid movement.
❗️Therefore, rather than proving full readiness for war, the exercise was an important step in identifying and reducing friction among allied forces.
