🎙 Iran’s non-oil exports reach $15 billion by mid-2026: Official

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The head of the Islamic Republic of Iran Customs Administration (IRICA) says non-oil exports of the country have reached $15 billion from the beginning of this year.


Foroud Asgari made the revelation about the mid-year import–export statistics at the 43rd meeting of the Tehran Chamber of Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture (TCCIMA) on Tuesday.
​
According to him, both exports and imports of non-oil commercial goods were respectively at $15 billion and $17 billion, although there was an average decrease of 24 percent compared to the same period last year.
​
Pointing to the import sector, he said the policies had been implemented in a way that goods with foreign exchange priority remain at the top, resulting in a decrease in the import of some unnecessary or low-priority items to the country.
​
Asgari cited a decrease in mobile phone imports, saying about 3,160,000 units were brought into the country in the first five months of last year, which has reached one million units this year, about 70 percent less in terms of value. However, he reported clearing 25,000 vehicles so far in 2026, representing a 67 percent increase compared to the same period last year
​
The IRICA chief pointed to the situation of basic goods in the country’s ports, saying 9.5 million out of 39 million tons of the total imports in Iran are basic goods, of which 4.5 million tons are currently available in 13 ports. According to him, of this amount, about 3 million tons of basic goods are stored at Imam Khomeini Port and about one million tons in 12 other ports.
​
Emphasizing the need to facilitate port and customs operations to meet the country’s needs promptly, he noted that Iranian Customs is trying to ensure that the process of clearing essential goods and priority items continues without interruption.
​
Elsewhere in his speech, the Director General of Iranian Customs referred to issues related to borders and exports to neighboring countries and stressed the need for consultation to remove border restrictions with Turkiye, Azerbaijan, and Pakistan.
​
On some borders, including with Turkiye, there are still restrictions that require negotiation at higher levels, he said, adding that even at the crossings with Azerbaijan, there is a need to take operational problems at some points into consideration.
​
Asgari also stated about exports to Pakistan, saying it is possible to send a large number of trucks at some borders, but the capacity or conditions of the other side prevent this from being fully realized.

📡 Kurdish VP says Iran won’t allow conspiracies to drive wedge with Iraqi Kurdistan

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Vice President for Rural Development Affairs Abdolkarim Hosseinzadeh says that Iran will not allow any conspiracy to drive a wedge between the Islamic Republic and Iraq’s Kurdistan Region, stressing that Iran and the northern Iraqi region are united.


Hosseinzadeh made the remarks late Monday on X, reacting to a drone attack on the office of Kurdistan Region Prime Minister Masrour Barzani earlier in the day.
​
The vice president strongly condemned the attack and called for those responsible to be held accountable.
​
“As an Iranian Kurd and a member of the Iranian government, I strongly condemn the attack on the office of my brother, Masrour Barzani, Prime Minister of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq,” he said, emphasizing the unity between Iran and the Iraqi region.
​
He said that Iran and Iraq are “more than just neighbors”. “We are one. We will not allow any attack or conspiracy to drive a wedge between Iran and the Kurdistan Region. The truth must come to light, and those responsible must be held accountable.”
​
Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei described Monday’s attack on Barzani’s office as “highly suspicious” and urged regional vigilance to thwart attempts to destabilize ties between Iran and Iraq.

⚡️ Iran provides AI training to 4 million students

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Education Minister Alireza Kazemi says special artificial intelligence (AI) training courses have been held for about 4 million students during difficult wartime conditions.


Speaking on Tuesday at the third day of a conference of provincial education chiefs, Kazemi said the ministry’s key programs also include the Iran Digital initiative, coding, programming, and promoting AI education.
​
He said empowering teachers was another major priority, noting that 250,000 teachers have received AI-related training.
​
Kazemi said Iran’s education system had pursued two major policies over the past year: “justice” and “quality.” He added that major achievements had been made under both policies with the government’s support.
​
He also announced the construction of nearly 2,400 educational facilities containing 24,000 classrooms.
​
Other measures included standardizing 50,000 classrooms, renovating more than 150,000 classrooms, and equipping schools with modern technologies.
