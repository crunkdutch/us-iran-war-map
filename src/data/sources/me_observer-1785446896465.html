⚡️ ⏺Dispersal for survival, convergence for effect!
⏺In a battlespace saturated with satellites, drones, SIGINT, radar, and precision fire, the U.S. Army no longer assumes complete and permanent superiority. The operational objective is to create a "window of superiority" in a limited sector and for a limited time: a few minutes to cross a belt of obstacles, a few hours to reposition reserves, or a few days to bring more forces into the theater. In this logic, time is part of superiority itself; superiority that is built late practically does not exist.
⏺The organizational response is to separate "force concentration" from "effect concentration." Units remain dispersed for survival, reduce their physical and electromagnetic signatures, and use multiple routes and nodes; but at the moment of decision, the effect of space sensors, electronic warfare, deception, artillery fire, air attack, and ground maneuver converges on a few decisive enemy nodes. This is the difference between convergence and classic massing: forces do not need to stand together for a long time, but the effect must arrive at the same time and place.
⏺Convergence is a "designed event," not an automatic result of the network. If cyber disruption ends before fires are ready, if a drone discovers a target but fire approval arrives late, or if a brigade is not ready to move after suppressing air defenses, all capabilities are present but the operational effect does not materialize. Therefore, the staff's criterion is not the number of data pages or the count of targeting meetings; it is the quality of the sequence: who recognized the opportunity, which echelon authorized it, what effect was applied, how the maneuver unit exploited it, and how it then re-prepared.
⏺The 2026 exercises indicate the direction of this effort. Exercise Sword 26 tested force movement, border crossing, coalition building, and reinforcement of the European theater in a multinational chain. PC-C6, more than a software demonstration, was a test for linking sensors, data, command, and decision under adversary pressure and disruption. But the limits of evidence must be maintained: an exercise proves what problem the army takes seriously and how it tests it; it does not prove that the same architecture will work flawlessly in a long war, under real attack, and with high casualties.
🚫Analytical point and summary for combat intelligence professionals: Let's summarize the broad American formula as: "Dispersal for survival, convergence for effect, reconstitution for repetition." The difficult part is not dispersal or individual firing, but the rapid change of status from dispersal to concentrated effect and back again before the enemy's response.
