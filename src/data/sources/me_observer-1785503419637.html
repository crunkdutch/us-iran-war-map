⚡️ Sustainability and Agility of Iran's Missile Industries:

The combination of the Haj Qasem ballistic missile booster with the warheads of the Kheibar Shekan and Fattah missiles, according to aerospace standards, is a medium-term project (between one and two years) and has many complexities, including the redistribution of the center of pressure and the center of mass. Due to the cylindrical and single-conical geometry of the Haj Qasem missile warhead, the mass distribution of this warhead is very different from the Kheibar Shekan and Fattah warheads, which are elongated, bi-conical, and single-conical. The adaptation of these two sections means redesigning and rewriting the guidance and control algorithms of the missile's flight computer.

Structural Adaptation: The solid-fuel engine of the Haj Qasem missile generates a very high initial velocity, and the warhead of the Kheibar Shekan 2 missile is designed to withstand heavy temperatures and pressures at speeds above Mach 5 (hypersonic speeds). The adaptation of these two sections required very complex thermal and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations in software such as Ansys Fluent and CFX.

The design and testing of this combined platform within this short timeframe and under conditions of heavy enemy attacks on the country's missile facilities indicate the defense industries' access to a digital design platform. This platform allows specialists to perform flight simulations in engineering software without numerous operational tests and directly introduce the new platform into the operational phase. For example, a missile or new combination would normally have to be tested operationally several times to ensure temperature resistance and the integrity of the warhead and structure. However, in this type of design, only the simulations performed in the software are relied upon, and the missile quickly and immediately enters the operational phase.

All these points demonstrate the agility, sustainability, and progress of the missile industries.
