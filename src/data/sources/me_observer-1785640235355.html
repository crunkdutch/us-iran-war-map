⚡️ The Harding Project is a strategic initiative by the Chief of Staff of the Army, launched in 2023 to revive the culture of professional writing, military knowledge production, and the modernization of the Army's specialized publications. The main idea of this project is that operational experiences, lessons learned, and the ideas of commanders and military personnel should not remain only within units, but should be transformed into articles, research, and publications so that the entire Army can benefit from them.

The project is named after Major General Edwin "Forrest" Harding, an officer who, in the 1930s, as editor of the Infantry Journal, played a significant role in reviving the professional literature of the U.S. Army. Today, the Harding Project, with the same philosophy, strives to transform military publications into a platform for experience transfer, critique, innovation, and doctrine development.

⚡️ 🚫First Post: Sole Reliance on Drones, a Weakness in Divisional Reconnaissance
🔹The US Army speaks of a significant weakness in its targeting cycle!
⏺A specialized article published in the US Army's Military Review journal indicates that divisional intelligence units in large-scale combat operations face a fundamental problem in discovering high-value targets deep within enemy positions: over-reliance on the division's organic sensors and reconnaissance equipment!
⏺This habit is largely a product of two decades of war against non-state actors in the "Global War on Terror"; an environment where the US usually had air superiority and extensive freedom of action for reconnaissance drones. However, in battle with a peer adversary, drones like the MQ-1C Gray Eagle may not be able to freely enter deep into the battle zone. Air defense, electronic warfare, long-range fire, and communication disruption can create a large gap in the division's intelligence picture.
🔹The conclusion seems clear; deep enemy reconnaissance should not depend on a single drone or a single type of sensor; rather, data from tactical sensors, the operational theater, and national technical capabilities must be integrated simultaneously.
