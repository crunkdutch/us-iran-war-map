📡 Exclusive | Iran-Russia relations gain momentum under President Pezeshkian: Ambassador Jalali

📌 Moscow, IRNA – Iranian Ambassador to Russia Kazem Jalali says relations between the two countries have gained significant momentum under President Masoud Pezeshkian, with the two sides pursuing a strategic partnership based on long-term interests.


Speaking to IRNA in Moscow on Wednesday in an exclusive interview, the ambassador said Iran’s approach toward Russia is strategic and independent of its relations with Western countries, rejecting perceptions that closer ties with Moscow are primarily linked to Tehran’s relations with the West.
​
According to the ambassador, Iran has always sought constructive relations with Western countries, but deception of the Western side undermined those relations.
​
Jalali emphasized that Iran considers Russia an important neighbor and major global power. Bilateral ties have grown under the strategic vision of the martyred Leader of the Islamic Revolution Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, whose deep and insightful approach to relations with neighbors, particularly Russia, has helped strengthen ties over the past two years, the ambassador noted.
​
He stressed that Iran’s foreign policy toward Russia, China, and Western countries is based on maintaining national independence and observing the principles of dignity, wisdom and prudence.
​
Sixth Pezeshkian-Putin meeting
​
The ambassador also said Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and his Russian counterpart Vladimir Putin would hold their sixth face-to-face meeting next week on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. The two presidents have also held six phone calls since Pezeshkian took office in August 2024.
​
Foreign ministers maintain close contact
​
Jalali said Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and his Russian counterpart Sergey Lavrov have held 12 in-person meetings and 17 phone conversations during the same period, with their latest meeting taking place two weeks ago in Bishkek.
​
Five pillars of economic cooperation
​
Jalali said five key pillars have driven the expansion of ties between Iran and Russia over the past two years: the signing and implementation of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty between the two sides, free trade with the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU), the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), financial and banking mechanisms, and energy cooperation.
​
He said the free trade agreement, effective since May 15, 2026, has eliminated tariffs on 87 percent of goods traded between Iran and the Eurasian Economic Union, creating new opportunities for Iranian exporters.
​
Jalali also highlighted the North-South Corridor, energy cooperation, gas projects, and nuclear power development as major areas for expanding economic relations.
​
The envoy said Iran and Russia are pursuing direct banking links and independent payment mechanisms to boost bilateral trade, adding that many financial and banking challenges are being resolved through ongoing talks.

💬 Pezeshkian stresses full coordination to preserve Iran’s economic stability

📌 Tehran, IRNA – President Masoud Pezeshkian has stressed the need for full coordination among Iran’s economic institutions to maintain economic stability and contain inflationary expectations amid mounting external pressures.


Pezeshkian made the remarks during a Wednesday visit to the Central Bank of Iran, where he attended a meeting with Economy Minister Ali Madanizadeh, Central Bank Governor Abdolnaser Hemmati, and a group of prominent economists.
​
The meeting reviewed the latest monetary, foreign exchange, and financial indicators and the Central Bank’s decision-making processes.
​
Economists also presented their views on addressing trade barriers, managing the country’s new economic conditions, fiscal and budgetary policies, monetary and foreign exchange policies, trade and economic relations, and management of enterprises.
​
Referring to the special circumstances arising from the US-Israeli war of aggression, economic blockade, and foreign pressures, Pezeshkian underscored the importance of maintaining economic stability and containing inflationary expectations.
​
“Management of key markets, particularly the foreign exchange and monetary markets, in such circumstances requires complete cohesion and synergy among the monetary policymaking institution, the government and other economic pillars of the country,” he said.
​
The meeting was held as part of the second periodic session between the Central Bank governor and economists, following the announcement of the US economic pressure campaign against Iran.

🌍 Parliament to pursue US war crimes in Lamerd through legal channels: MP

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Iran’s Parliament, in cooperation with the Judiciary, will pursue the legal and international cases arising from US war crimes in Lamerd, alongside the US attack in Minab, Iranian Parliament Deputy Speaker Ali Nikzad says.


Nikzad made the remarks Wednesday during a visit to Lamerd County, where he inspected Shahid Naeimi Hall and residential homes in the Isargaran Township that were extensively damaged in missile attacks by the United States and the Israeli regime.
​
Paying tribute to those killed in the attacks, Nikzad said martyrdom was the highest blessing God could bestow upon a person.
​
Nikzad offered condolences to the families of the martyrs and the Iranian people, saying the victims were “pure, faithful, devout, honorable and dedicated” people who sacrificed their lives for the dignity and security of the country.
​
He said their martyrdom should be congratulated, while their loss should also be mourned.
​
Nikzad went on to say that the sports facility was a place where young people and children had gathered for recreation and sports.
​
He questioned why such people had been targeted by a bomb containing more than 760,000 pellets.
​
The deputy speaker said Lamerd has a population of 40,000, yet the American-Zionist enemy used bombs against the population containing more than 760,000 fragments.
​
“This is evidence of America’s war crime,” he said.
​
Nikzad said that the American crimes in Lamerd had occurred alongside their crimes in Minab, adding that the Parliament members, particularly the Parliamentary Judiciary and Legal Commission, will pursue the necessary measures in cooperation with the Judiciary through legal authorities as well as international forums.
​
Lamerd, a town in southern Iran, came under US military attack on February 28. CCTV footage published by Iranian state media showed a projectile moments before it exploded above a residential area. International experts identified it as being a US missile, based on its appearance, the size of the blast, and the distance from potential US launch sites in the Middle East.
​
Multiple analysts said the missile seen in the footage was likely a PrSM - a brand new missile manufactured by Lockheed Martin for the US military.
​
Official reports indicated that 21 people, including four children, were killed and a hundred more were injured. On the same day, nearly 400km east, strikes hit an Iranian school in Minab, killing 168 schoolchildren along with their teachers.

⚡️ Negotiations not a sign of retreat, but resistance if national interests are served: Ghalibaf

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says negotiations with adversaries become meaningful if they help advance national interests, realize the rights of the nation, eliminate oppression, and consolidate the achievements of the resistance.


In response to a letter from the editor-in-chief of Kayhan, a leading Iranian daily, Ghalibaf clarified his stance on negotiations with the enemies, stating that the responsibility to engage in dialogue with those whose hands are stained with the blood of the martyred Leader, military commanders, scientific elites, and citizens was not a privilege, but a heavy test and a serious trust.
​
Expressing gratitude to Hossein Shariatmadari for reminding him of the ideals of the Islamic Revolution, the guidance of the late leaders, and the sacrifices made by the faithful people, Ghalibaf described the opinions as a sign of brotherhood from one who is a commander of the soft war and at the forefront of defending national dignity.
​
“And prepare for them what you can of strength,” he quoted from the Holy Qur'an to justify his notion, adding that, with such logic, dialogue—when necessary—is the very field of struggle and resistance, not a sign of retreat. He also recalled the martyred Leader's resistance to arrogance to protect the interests of Islam and Muslims through rationality and prudence.
​
Ghalibaf said the Leader's emphasis was on interaction with the world, and that negotiation, whenever necessary, must be pursued from a position of power while preserving dignity, wisdom, and expediency. “I must say that any rational action, any authoritative dialogue, or any attempt to take the nation’s rights from the enemy should not be incorrectly defined as compromise and passivity.”
​
From the first days of the Islamic Revolution, the top parliamentarian continued, he had been committed to the path of the founder of the Islamic Republic, away from Westernization, sedition, deviation, and distortion of the Imam’s line.
​
He said he had a fundamental difference with those who, instead of relying on the power of the people, look to the enemy for a solution, adding that the enemy always tries to create misunderstandings and differences, but it should be countered with benevolent criticism and revolutionary vigilance, rather than hasty interpretations.
​
Ghalibaf also warned against political atmosphere-building, saying it should not be allowed to weaken the country or undermine the unity, trust, and belief in those who remained loyal to the Leaders of the Islamic Revolution.
​
“We must shoulder the responsibility of safeguarding and protecting the instruments of power so that we can consolidate the achievements of the war and safely lead the country through the serious disasters that the enemies have planned,” he said, adding that it would be wrong to evade the responsibility of negotiating with the enemy and to call every achievement of the negotiating team a betrayal.
​
Referring to the current challenges, Ghalibaf called for united efforts to solve the people’s problems, increase Iran’s authority, safeguard the achievements of the resistance, and defeat the enemy—rather than responding to inaccurate perceptions and judgments.
​
He emphasized that the set of guidelines from the Leader of the Islamic Revolution also points to the same: resisting the enemy, maintaining national cohesion, strengthening domestic power, and avoiding harmful polarization.

🔻 IRGC spokesperson says US faces shortfall in strategic weapons after war

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The United States has suffered shortages in strategic weapons following its recent war against Iran, and replenishing some of its depleted stocks could take years, a spokesman for the Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) says.


Brigadier General Hossein Mohebbi said on Wednesday that Washington, which had traditionally managed wars in other countries while shifting the costs onto others, had this time faced direct strategic vulnerabilities.
​
Speaking at a meeting with media representatives in the central city of Isfahan, Mohebbi said the United States was forced to retreat “not only in the hard war but also in the infrastructure war.”
​

​
On military supplies, Mohebbi said Washington had expected Iran’s missile stocks to run out over time, but Iranian missile operations continued.
​
He said a reduction in US strategic weapons stocks was one of the reasons Washington retreated, adding that the US military doctrine was based on the ability to fight simultaneously on several fronts.
​
Any new threat in the western Pacific, including from China or Russia, would further limit the weapons available to Washington for a prolonged confrontation with Iran, he said.
​
Mohebbi said the United States was forced to retreat in three phases of confrontation with Iran, including the 12-, 40- and 17-day wars, ultimately turning to economic warfare.
​
Despite the detection and targeting of numerous sites, he said, the enemy’s technology had failed to break the cohesion of the Iranian people and the mutual support between the public and armed forces.

⚡️ Pezeshkian inaugurates 158 transport projects in northern Iran

📌 NOSHAHR, Iran — President Masoud Pezeshkian inaugurates 158 projects of the Ministry of Roads and Urban Development in Mazandaran province worth about 23 trillion tomans, during an online ceremony marking Government Week.


The projects were inaugurated simultaneously through a video conference with Pezeshkian, while the main ceremony was held at Noshahr Port.
​
According to Mohammad Shakibaei-Nasab, the managing director of the Ports and Maritime Organization, the projects were designed to expand transportation infrastructure, improve public welfare, and strengthen Mazandaran’s economic capacity, while two domestically built multipurpose maritime search-and-rescue vessels were launched in the maritime sector at a cost of more than 4.3 trillion rials.
​
Shakibaei-Nasab said the vessels will strengthen maritime search-and-rescue capabilities and improve the safety of maritime operations.
​
Meanwhile, four advanced marine loading arms were inaugurated at Amirabad and Noshahr ports.
​
Shakibaei‑Nasab said the equipment was designed and manufactured for loading and unloading petroleum products, with more than 2.4 trillion rials spent on the project.
​
Separately, the approximately 3.5‑kilometer road connecting Kolahduz‑Kala to Kohneh‑Kala in the Amirabad Port area was opened at a cost of more than 2 trillion rials.
​
Shakibaei‑Nasab said the road will improve access to Amirabad Port and strengthen transportation infrastructure in the surrounding area.
​
In the housing sector, 4,810 residential units were also inaugurated, including 1,915 urban and 2,895 rural units, with an investment of about 18 trillion tomans. A total of 150 plots of land were also allocated to applicants under a government program supporting families and population growth.
​
Other projects included 11 kilometers of transportation routes, a 40‑kilometer drainage, surface‑water collection, and sewage network at Sari Airport, and a weather station and meteorological buoy at Noshahr Port. The projects were completed with investments of more than 2 trillion, 1.137 trillion, and 18 billion tomans, respectively.
​
Meanwhile, 146 projects to eliminate accident‑prone areas and improve roads across the province were completed with more than 1.68 trillion tomans in investment. Shakibaei‑Nasab said maritime and port infrastructure remained a key priority in Mazandaran, while reliance on domestic manufacturers would improve safety and services, strengthen the maritime economy, and increase the operational capacity of the country’s ports.
