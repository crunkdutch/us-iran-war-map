🎙 Iran urges Qatar to allow Air Force fact-finding team over missing pilots

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Commander of the Missing in Action Search Committee of Iran’s Armed Forces General Staff has urged Qatar to allow an Iranian Air Force fact-finding team to enter the country and investigate the status of Iranian pilots held by the country.


Brigadier General Seyyed Mohammad Baqerzadeh made the remarks on Sunday after Qatar’s Foreign Ministry denied reports that Iranian pilots were being held captive in the country.
​
“Instead of denying the matter and creating restrictions on the path to finding the truth, the Qatari government should allow the fact-finding and expert team of the Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force to travel to Qatar,” he said.
​
According to the commander, a team of highly experienced Iranian Air Force experts has been waiting for several months to enter Qatar and conduct a field investigation.
​
He said repeated obstruction and delays by Qatari officials have so far prevented the return of the Iranian pilots to the country.
​
Baqerzadeh also stressed the need for the International Committee of the Red Cross to urgently follow up on the issue.
​
He further warned that Kuwait had similarly avoided providing information about the fate of four Iranians held by the country.
​
The remarks came after Qatar denied holding Iranian pilots in secret detention following the downing of their aircraft during hostilities earlier this year.

💬 East Azarbaijan prepares for post-war tourism market with major investment: Official

📌 Tabriz, IRNA – Iran’s East Azarbaijan Province is preparing for a recovery in international tourism following the recent war of aggression by the US and Israel, with billions of dollars invested in tourism projects and 36 hotels currently under construction, according to the provincial tourism chief.


Ahmad Hamzehzadeh, head of the Cultural Heritage, Tourism, and Handicrafts Department of East Azarbaijan Province, told IRNA on Saturday that the recent conflict had created a new opportunity to introduce Iran’s cultural identity, history and civilization to international audiences and potentially encourage foreign visitors to experience the country firsthand.
​
He said foreign tourist arrivals at official accommodation centers in the province fell in the first three months of the current Iranian year (March 21-June 21), as regional tensions led travelers to postpone trips and tour operators to plan more cautiously.
​
Despite the downturn, tourism infrastructure development has continued, Hamzehzadeh said, adding that authorities were easing investment procedures, including waiving construction fees for hotels and reducing administrative barriers.
​
He stressed that accommodation and tourism infrastructure must be developed before demand returns, so the northwestern province is ready when international travel gradually recovers.

📄 Over 200,000 tourists visit Meshginshahr during recent Iranian holiday

📌 Meshginshahr, IRNA – More than 200,000 tourists visited Meshginshahr County, Ardabil Province, in northwestern Iran during last week’s holiday break, according to the local head of the Cultural Heritage, Tourism and Handicrafts Department.


Jafar Faramarzi said on Saturday that more than 90,000 vehicle movements were recorded at the county’s entrances during the four-day period that started on August 11, highlighting Meshginshahr’s popularity as a tourist destination.
​
He added that tourism facilities and accommodation centers across the county were fully prepared to serve visitors. Official statistics and field estimates showed that 15,000 and 20,000 overnight stays were recorded at official accommodation facilities, government guesthouses and schools on Wednesday and Thursday respectively, he said.
​
Located on the slopes of Mount Sabalan, Meshginshahr is known for its natural landscapes and diverse tourist attractions, including mineral hot springs, historical sites and monuments, and a suspension bridge.
​
Faramarzi said tourism infrastructure in the county has expanded in recent years, helping improve services for visitors.
​
Among Meshginshahr’s notable attractions are Qahqaheh Castle, Qanli Bulagh Caravanserai, Kohneh Qaleh (old castle), the suspension bridge and the Shabil and Qinarcheh hot springs.
​
The Shahr Yeri archaeological site, featuring dozens of stone sculptures believed to date back around 8,000 years, is also located in the county.
​
With a population of about 155,000, Meshgin Shahr is a popular tourist destination, particularly during the warmer months.

⚡️ Tehran, Qom, Mashhad to host ceremonies marking 40th day after martyred Leader’s funeral

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Office of the Leader of the Islamic Revolution has announced that ceremonies marking the 40th day after the funeral of the martyred Leader of the Islamic Revolution, Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei, will be held in Tehran, Qom and Mashhad.


A statement issued by the office on Sunday said that commemorative ceremonies for Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei and his martyred family members will be held in the three cities, under the direction of the current Leader of the Islamic Revolution, Ayatollah Sayyid Mojtaba Hosseini Khamenei.
​
The ceremonies will be held as follows:
​
Tehran: Tuesday, August 18, from 5 p.m. to 7 p.m. local time at the prayer hall of Imam Khomeini Mosalla.
​
Qom: Wednesday, August 19, at the Shrine of Hazrat Masoumeh (peace be upon her) after evening prayers.
​
Mashhad: Thursday, August 20, at the Holy Shrine of Imam Reza (peace be upon him) after evening prayers.
​
Ayatollah Seyyed Ali Khamenei and several members of his family were martyred in an airstrike on his office and residence in Tehran on February 28, the first day of the recent US-Israeli war of aggression against Iran. They were laid to rest on July 9 following a week-long memorial service.

🎙 Iran managing continuous enemy cyberattacks: ICT minister

📌 Gorgan, IRNA – Iran is managing continuous cyberattacks by its enemies amid the country’s wartime conditions, says Minister of Information and Communications Technology Sattar Hashemi.


“While the country is facing wartime conditions, we are witnessing cyberattacks by the enemy, but our colleagues are working around the clock to counter the cyberattacks, and our young people and elites have managed these attacks well and securely,” Hashemi said Sunday.
​
He added that the complexity of the attacks varies over time, but Iran’s experience in repelling cyberattacks in recent decades has prevented disruptions to services and thwarted the enemy’s objectives.
​
The remarks come amid US escalations against Iran in the wake of a 40-day war that Washington and Tel Aviv waged against Iran on February 28, which prompted Iran to take control of the Strait of Hormuz.
