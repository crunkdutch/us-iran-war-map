🇵🇸 | Hamas: Hamas and the Palestinian factions have dealt responsibly and positively with the negotiation process and the mediators’ proposals aimed at completing the implementation of the second phase of the ceasefire agreement. This was achieved through…
— Will Hamas really hand over its weapons? When, and how?

What has been announced so far does not mean that Hamas has decided to hand over its weapons or has begun implementing such a decision. Rather, the Palestinian side has accepted a negotiating framework containing highly stringent conditions that make any process of restricting and storing heavy weapons conditional upon Israel fully implementing its commitments during the first phase.

These commitments include halting attacks, airstrikes, and assassination operations, withdrawing to the “Yellow Line” and evacuating areas occupied in recent months, enabling the Palestinian National Committee and an international stabilization force to operate, and fully implementing the humanitarian protocol, including the entry of humanitarian aid, the resettlement of displaced people, debris removal, and the reconstruction of critical infrastructure such as electricity and water systems.

The proposed Palestinian framework is based on three principles: gradualism, simultaneity, and verification. Under this approach, no stage begins until the previous one has been fully completed. Responsibility for restricting and storing the weapons would rest with a Palestinian body, while the weapons themselves would remain inside the Gaza Strip and would not be handed over to Israel or any external party.

From a political perspective, this degree of flexibility can be viewed as an attempt to protect the resistance project from another existential war while buying time until after the Israeli elections on 27 October and the US presidential election on 3 November 2026. In this equation, time itself becomes a strategic asset, giving Gaza an opportunity to recover while awaiting regional developments and shifts in the balance of power.

Conversely, the likelihood that Benjamin Netanyahu will fully honor these commitments during the middle of an election campaign appears limited. Any Israeli breach of its obligations would halt the phased implementation of the agreement, allowing the resistance to argue that the deal had been violated by the other side and was therefore no longer enforceable. Under such circumstances, some of the political and legal justifications used to resume the war would also be weakened.

The historical experience of the Palestinian resistance shows that ceasefires and phased agreements have repeatedly been used as tools to reorganize priorities, rebuild capabilities, and preserve key elements of strength. Likewise, the Lebanese experience following UN Security Council Resolution 1701 demonstrated that restrictions on military deployment south of the Litani River did not result in complete disarmament, and that Hezbollah was later able to rebuild and further develop its capabilities.

For this reason, the central question today is not, “When will Hamas hand over its weapons?” Rather, it is whether Israel will actually fulfill the commitments upon which the beginning of this process depends, or whether acceptance of this framework will come to a halt precisely at the point where Israel is expected to begin making meaningful withdrawals.
