The discussions are progressing positively at both technical and political levels between the two countries, Baqaei said on Tuesday.
​

🔊 Iran After the War: From Resilience to Political Renewal

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The recent war demonstrated that, contrary to the expectations of those who planned it, external military pressure does not necessarily lead to political collapse or social fragmentation in Iran. However, successfully weathering the war does not mean that the country’s domestic problems have been resolved.


The central question is whether Iran can transform its wartime resilience into better governance, greater public trust, and a renewed relationship between the state and society.
​
Discussions about Iran are often trapped between two extreme narratives. The first views Iran exclusively through the lens of a security threat and the perceived necessity of regime change, seeking solutions to the country’s problems through increased external pressure. The second attributes every criticism of domestic governance, economic difficulties, and social demands to foreign propaganda.
​
Neither narrative offers a complete or accurate picture of Iran. It is a country with a deep historical and civilisational identity, a diverse and resilient society, and a multilayered political system, while also facing serious economic, social, and administrative challenges. Understanding Iran’s future requires recognising both the reality of external aggression and the country’s right to defend its sovereignty, without overlooking the need for domestic reform.
​
A nation may require reform while retaining the right to defend itself against external attack. Criticism of Iran’s domestic problems cannot justify war or foreign military intervention. Equally, resistance to external aggression should not result in the denial of internal shortcomings.
​
The recent war was not merely a military confrontation; it also had political, psychological, social, and media dimensions. Militarily, Iran’s defensive capabilities, command structure, and capacity to respond under pressure were tested. Politically, the continuity and functionality of the state’s core institutions were put to the test. Psychologically and socially, the question was whether fear and insecurity would fragment society or strengthen national solidarity.
​
In the media sphere, an extensive battle emerged over how the war should be interpreted. Some foreign media outlets portrayed military pressure as a prelude to the collapse of Iran’s political structure and the beginning of fundamental change. The evidence, however, showed that external pressure did not produce immediate collapse. Instead, at least for a period, it strengthened national solidarity and heightened public sensitivity to the country’s sovereignty and territorial integrity.
​
This assessment does not ignore the costs of war. The conflict caused human, economic, and infrastructural damage and placed further pressure on people’s lives. Nevertheless, expectations that the state’s principal institutions would rapidly cease functioning and that political collapse would follow were not realised.
​
Many Iranians distinguished between criticising the state’s domestic performance and defending the country against external aggression. Support for Iran during wartime should not necessarily be interpreted as approval of every domestic policy. This distinction is one of the most important realities overlooked in many external analyses.
​

🎙 Arbaeen strengthens religious understanding, Iran–Iraq academic ties: Iranian minister

📌 Karbala, IRNA – Iran’s Minister of Science, Research and Technology Hossein Simaei Sarraf has described the Arbaeen pilgrimage as a continuation of the message of Ashura, saying the annual gathering provides a valuable opportunity to deepen religious understanding, strengthen cultural dialogue, and expand scientific cooperation between Iran and Iraq.


Speaking on Tuesday during Arbaeen ceremonies in Karbala, Iraq, Simaei Sarraf said the pilgrimage symbolizes the enduring legacy of Imam Hussein’s uprising and demonstrates that the ideals of Ashura – the 10th day of the Islamic month of Muharram, commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Hussein (PBUH) at the Battle of Karbala in 680 CE – continue to inspire millions of pilgrims each year.
​
He praised the generous hospitality of the Iraqi people toward visitors, particularly Iranian pilgrims, describing it as a significant social and spiritual asset that has reinforced ties between the two neighboring nations.
​
The minister stressed that Arbaeen should not be viewed solely as a religious ritual, urging students and young people to study the historical, ethical, and intellectual dimensions of Imam Hussein’s movement.
​
He noted that numerous scholarly works published in Persian since the 1979 Islamic Revolution have enriched research on Ashura.
​
Simaei Sarraf also highlighted the growing presence of Iraqi students at Iranian universities, saying academic exchanges promote international engagement, cultural dialogue, and closer relations between the two countries.

📄 Six Pakistani ministers meet with Iranian minister in Islamabad

📌 Tehran, IRNA – In a rare diplomatic gesture, six members of Pakistan’s federal cabinet met with Iran’s Minister of Industry, Mine and Trade, Mohammad Atabak, at the Iranian Embassy in Islamabad, reaffirming the country’s determination to expand bilateral cooperation to the highest possible level.


According to the Industry Ministry, the six senior Pakistani officials voiced their government’s firm commitment to strengthening comprehensive cooperation with Iran.
​
The Pakistani ministers included Federal Minister for Commerce Jam Kamal Khan, Minister for Communications Abdul Aleem Khan, Minister for National Food Security and Research Rana Tanveer Hussain, Minister for Maritime Affairs Muhammad Junaid Anwar Chaudhry, Minister for Petroleum and Natural Resources Ali Pervaiz Malik, and Minister for Investment Qaiser Ahmed Sheikh.
​
Atabak thanked the Pakistani ministers for their visit, saying, “The Islamic Republic of Iran, with a view to long-term horizons, welcomes all the proposals put forward by the esteemed Pakistani ministers and announces its readiness to draw up an executive roadmap in the shortest possible time.”
​
The Iranian minister arrived in Islamabad on August 3 at the head of a high-level economic delegation to attend the 10th meeting of the Iran-Pakistan Joint Trade Committee and hold talks with Pakistan’s prime minister.
​
The August 4-5 visit focuses on expanding economic, trade and industrial cooperation.
​
The agenda includes finalizing a free trade agreement, establishing a commodity barter mechanism, addressing transit obstacles, facilitating the movement of Iranian containers through Pakistani ports, improving border infrastructure, and advancing rail and maritime connectivity between Chabahar, Gwadar and the Rimdan-Gabd border crossing.

💬 Islamic Human Rights Day symbol of Muslim countries’ resistance against Western hegemony: Statement

📌 Tehran, IRNA — The High Council for Human Rights of the Islamic Republic of Iran has, in a statement, described the Islamic Human Rights and Human Dignity Day as a symbol of resistance by Islamic nations against Western hegemony and cultural and political domination.


The West has consistently sought to impose its self-constructed values of liberal democracy, masked as human rights, upon the world, while disregarding the rich culture of other nations, including Islamic countries, said the statement issued on Wednesday.
​
Freedom and human dignity are divine gifts that everyone, people and governments alike must honor. Respecting these values is key to safeguarding human rights, it added.
​
For nearly fifty years, the Islamic Republic of Iran has made it a priority to observe this day to highlight the foundational sources of human rights within Islam, foster academic and cultural dialogue to develop an Islamic framework for human rights, and offer a viable model of religious democracy and governance grounded in Islamic rationality, it noted.
​
Marking the anniversary of the Islamic Human Rights and Human Dignity Day, the High Council for Human Rights of the Islamic Republic of Iran affirms that Islamic nations are endowed with the potential to achieve progress rooted in Islamic principles and respect for human dignity.
​
It also holds that the recent hostile actions by global powers against Islam only further confirm the truth of the Islamic faith.

🎙 Iran, Pakistan reaffirm $10bn trade goal, advance free trade agreement

📌 Islamabad, IRNA – Iran and Pakistan have signed a memorandum of understanding to expand bilateral trade, reaffirming their commitment to raising annual trade to $10 billion and advancing negotiations toward a free trade agreement.


The memorandum was signed during the closing session of the 10th Joint Trade Committee in Islamabad on Wednesday, attended by Iran’s Minister of Industry, Mine and Trade, Mohammad Atabak, and Pakistan’s Commerce Minister, Jam Kamal Khan.
​
The two sides discussed expanding commercial cooperation, particularly in shared border regions, developing maritime and shipping activities, coordinating steps toward a free trade agreement, establishing joint free trade zones, and promoting barter trade.
​
Atabak said negotiations over the past two days had focused on increasing commercial exchanges, adding that some areas required facilitation and the removal of obstacles, and that the Pakistani side agreed to pursue further follow-up.
​
He said key decisions included activating border regions, expanding cross-border trade, strengthening private-sector participation, exchanging technical delegations, and immediately implementing bilateral agreements.
​
“The Islamic Republic has no limitation on increasing the volume of trade with Pakistan,” Atabak said, calling on business communities in both countries to play a leading role.
​
A separate cooperation memorandum was also signed between the Iran Chamber of Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture and the Federation of Pakistan Chambers of Commerce and Industry.
​
Atabak is also scheduled to meet Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif during his visit.
