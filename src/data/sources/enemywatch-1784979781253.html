- The Northern Governorate Police Directorate stated that three citizens were arrested for selling clothing through social media platforms printed with what they described as inflammatory images and slogans.

- Reports state that civilian forces affiliated with the Interior Ministry detained the young Sayed Hassan Ahmed after an ambush in Duraz on June 9.

- The issue of printed T-shirts caused concern in Bahraini society, especially due to the strict security response.

- The printed slogans reportedly included sayings of Imam Husayn (A), such as:

- “How can we accept humiliation?”

- “Whoever is like me does not pledge allegiance to someone like him”

- “I do not give you my hand in humiliation”

- On June 2, the young Jaafar Mohammed was also arrested after being summoned for investigation, adding to a wave of reported arrests since February last year.

- Any flag displayed must be officially stamped by the mourning hall.

- The Bahraini authorities cancelled flag-raising ceremonies marking the beginning of Ashura in multiple regions, claiming they are foreign customs.

- Authorities also ordered mourning halls and Husayniyas not to display images of any religious scholar, whether living or deceased, during Ashura.

- Decisions issued by the General Directorate of Ja’fari Affairs under the Ministry of Interior sparked anger within the Husayni community.

- Restrictions were imposed on Ashura processions, including limiting their timing and forcing them to end at midnight instead of continuing until dawn.

- Authorities restricted installation of Ashura banners only to the vicinity of mourning halls, banning placement on main streets.

- Mourning hall leaders were warned they would be held responsible for any banners containing what authorities describe as “political slogans” or “politicization of processions.”

- Processions outside official mourning hall frameworks were restricted, including those originating from mosques.

- The decisions particularly affected the scholars’ procession held on the night of the 10th in Manama from the Mosque of the Believer.

- Mourning halls were instructed to display their names at the front of each procession.

- The restrictions were described as lacking clear organizational or security justification by critics.

- Reports say these measures are part of broader control over religious and Ashura activities by the Interior Ministry.

- The Ministry is accused in reports of restricting religious freedoms and controlling jurisprudential and endowment affairs.

- Allegations include wider repression affecting Shia religious institutions, scholars, seminary teachers, and religious representatives.

- Additional reported actions include:

- Closure of cultural institutions

- Revocation of citizenships

- Targeting of religious thought and practices

- Seizure of charitable funds
