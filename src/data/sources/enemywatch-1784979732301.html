The call was linked to the case of Samira al-Zubairi, who has been identified by some reports as Mira Hussein, a daughter of former Iraqi President Saddam Hussein. According to Sheikh Fadgham, nearly 8,000 tribal fighters have responded. Ansar Allah has answered with its own military deployments, transforming al-Jawf into one of Yemen’s most heavily militarized frontlines.

Ansar Allah’s leadership is making no attempt to hide its expectations. Deputy Information Minister Ahmed Al-Shami openly declared that “the battle is coming,” while attacking Saudi-backed tribal leaders and praising Ansar Allah’s military campaign in support of Gaza. Such statements are not ordinary political speeches. They are preparation for war, directed both at domestic audiences and at regional adversaries.

Saudi Arabia’s role cannot be separated from these developments. Although Riyadh reduced direct military operations in recent years, Saudi-backed tribal forces are once again organizing along Yemen’s northern front. From Ansar Allah’s perspective, this is not simply a tribal dispute. It is viewed as the possible reopening of the Saudi front at a time when Yemen is already confronting israel in the Red Sea.

Even more significant is israel’s growing interest in the Horn of Africa. Reports indicate that military-related equipment, including advanced communications systems with intelligence applications, was recently sent to Somalia. At the same time, israel and Somaliland have announced plans to deepen diplomatic engagement, including the opening of a Somaliland diplomatic mission in occupied Jerusalem.

For israel, Somaliland offers enormous strategic value. Positioned directly across the Gulf of Aden from Yemen, it overlooks the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, one of the world’s most critical maritime chokepoints. Establishing intelligence, surveillance, or logistical infrastructure there would allow israel to monitor Yemeni military activity, protect shipping routes, gather intelligence on Ansar Allah operations, and prepare for future military operations across the Red Sea.

The Bab al-Mandeb has become one of israel’s greatest strategic vulnerabilities since Ansar Allah entered the Gaza war. Yemeni missile and drone operations, together with attacks on israeli-linked shipping, have demonstrated that israel’s southern maritime lifeline can be threatened. By strengthening its presence in Somaliland, israel is attempting to create a forward observation and intelligence platform capable of countering Yemen’s growing regional influence and reducing the effectiveness of any future closure of the Bab al-Mandeb.

Ansar Allah has already drawn this conclusion. Abdul-Malik al-Houthi warned only days ago that Yemen would not remain passive in the face of Zionist expansion into Somaliland. From Sana’a’s perspective, israeli activity there is not diplomatic. It is military positioning. A Zionist presence directly across the Gulf of Aden represents an intelligence threat, a surveillance threat, and potentially a future operational threat against Yemen itself.

Taken together, these developments suggest that Yemen is preparing for more than another round of fighting.
