🇮🇱 | Unverified document allegedly lists Israeli demands in Syria talks:

An unverified document circulating online allegedly outlines 11 demands presented by Israel to representatives of the Syrian government. The document has not been independently authenticated, and its contents should therefore be treated with caution. If genuine, however, the demands would represent a highly consequential and dangerous framework for Syria’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and military independence. The alleged demands include opening an Israeli embassy in Damascus and a consulate in Aleppo; establishing a “David’s Path” corridor linking northern Israel with northeastern Syria; creating a 40 to 50-kilometre-deep security zone inside Syrian territory; limiting the Syrian Army to a maximum of 30,000 personnel; granting Suwayda self-governance; allowing Israeli forces to conduct operations inside Syrian territory whenever deemed necessary; granting Syrian citizenship to Palestinians while removing references to Palestinian identity from educational and official curricula; appointing Shimon Topashur, reportedly head of Damascus’s Jewish community, to the People’s Council and increasing Jewish parliamentary representation to five seats; transferring five military bases in northern Aleppo, Latakia and northern Syria for use as Israeli radar and operational centres; prohibiting Syria from developing an air force and air-defence capabilities; and restricting Syrian military aviation to helicopters used for agricultural spraying. The allegations come amid ongoing U.S.-mediated Syria-Israel negotiations, where security arrangements and Israel’s military presence in southern Syria are established areas of discussion. At this stage, however, the document itself remains unverified, and its authenticity must be established before its contents can be treated as confirmed.

🇮🇷🏴‍☠️ | Preliminary analysis says remnants match a JSOW bomb, not a SLAM-ER missile:

A closer examination of the remaining wreckage from the weapon used in the recent attack on a residential house indicates that the munition does not appear to be a SLAM-ER missile. Instead, the recovered fragments reportedly show a stronger resemblance to the AGM-154C JSOW.

According to the analysis, the red-marked section shows a control fin that matches one of the rear control fins of the AGM-154C JSOW. The yellow-marked section appears to be a burned portion of the weapon’s body, which also matches the JSOW’s fuselage. The blue-marked section is believed to be part of the internal launch lug, the component used to attach the weapon to a fighter aircraft’s pylon.

The extent of the destruction at the residential house is also described as being consistent with the effects of a 500-pound warhead, which is the warhead carried by the AGM-154C JSOW.

The analysis further argues that the strike was not carried out by a Lucas drone, stating that none of the recovered wreckage matches that platform. It adds that if a Lucas drone had been used, its engine should have been found near the impact site.

Important note: The residential house was reportedly located about 200 meters from a communications tower. The analysis argues that such a level of inaccuracy would be highly unlikely for a JSOW, and therefore claims the strike was deliberate rather than accidental.
