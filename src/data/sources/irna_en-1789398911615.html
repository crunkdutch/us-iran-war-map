📄 Iranian airlines warn sanctions must not put civil aviation safety at risk

📌 Tehran, IRNA – The Association of Iranian Airlines has warned that continued sanctions against Iran’s carriers could affect civil aviation services, stressing that air transport should not become a target of political and economic measures.


The association said civil aviation is vital infrastructure and an essential link between nations, serving millions of passengers traveling for tourism, medical treatment, education, business and family visits. It argued that restrictions on airlines and aviation supply chains can directly affect access to air transport and operational safety.
​
The association cited the principles of the Chicago Convention, which call for the safe, orderly and economic development of international civil aviation and fair opportunities for international air transport services.
​
It also highlighted difficulties in accessing aircraft parts, equipment and technical services, saying continuous access to such resources is directly linked to flight safety. Despite decades of restrictions, Iranian engineers, pilots and technicians have maintained and supported the country’s fleet by relying on domestic expertise and experience.
​
The association called on the international aviation community, the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), the International Air Transport Association (IATA) and member states to consider the humanitarian, technical and safety consequences of sanctions on Iranian airlines.
​
It also urged stronger cooperation among domestic aviation companies, airports, maintenance centers and government bodies to overcome current challenges.
​
The statement came after the US Treasury Department announced sanctions against 36 targets supporting Iran’s aviation sector, including 27 Iranian airlines.

🌍 Iran’s Hormozgan targets harvest 200,000 metric tons of dates; 20% for export

📌 Bandar Abbas, IRNA – Iran’s southern Hormozgan Province is expected to harvest around 200,000 tons of high-quality dates this year, with about 20 percent destined for international markets.


Some 50,000 tons have already been harvested since the season began in mid-June in Minab. Harvesting will continue until late November, ending with the premium Piarom variety in Hajiabad.
​
Hormozgan ranks third nationally in date production and cultivates more than 55 varieties, including 20 commercially valuable varieties for domestic consumption and export. About 65,000 tons are expected to be exported this year to Eurasian, European, and East Asian markets, as well as countries around the Persian Gulf.
​
Piarom, Kriteh, Mazafati, and Zahedi are expected to account for a major share of exports due to their quality.
​
Hajiabad has the largest area under date cultivation in the province, followed by Rudan, Minab, and Bandar Abbas.
​
Agricultural authorities have also prepared a five-year plan to modernize orchards, expand commercial varieties, preserve date germplasm and improve pest and disease control. The program aims to raise productivity, profitability, branding and value-added production.
​
Hormozgan has 38,000 hectares of date plantations and produces more than 200,000 tons of dates annually.
