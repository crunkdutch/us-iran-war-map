ℹ️ | Referring to the group as "Houthi" rather than their chosen name, Ansar Allah (Supporters of Allah), is considered reductive and by the movement itself, derogatory because it deliberately boils a broad national, religious, and political movement down to a single family surname, the al-Houthi family (originating from their founder, Hussein Badreddin al-Houthi). This framing was originally popularized by former Yemeni president Ali Abdullah Saleh during the Sa'dah wars in the 2000s to paint them as a localized tribal militia or rogue clan rather than a legitimate opposition government. By stripping away "Ansar Allah," the label erases the millions of non-Houthi-family supporters, delegitimizes their self-defined spiritual and anti-imperialist identity, and positions them strictly as a proxy or rebel faction rather than a major governing force, even though international media continues to use "Houthis" simply as a practical shorthand for global audiences.

When it comes to pronunciation, the standard English version is HOO-thee (/ˈhuː.θi/), where "HOO" rhymes with blue or who and "thee" uses a soft "th" sound as in think (or HOO-theez when pluralized), while in native Yemeni Arabic it is pronounced al-ḤOO-thee with a deep, raspy "H". However, many argue that it should be termed and pronounced as Hoosee instead, as this matches how millions of conversational Arabic speakers, regional dialects like Egyptian or Levantine, and French speakers naturally pronounce the term, making Hoosee a smoother, more accessible alternative in everyday speech.

🇾🇪 🇸🇦 | Saudi Arabia launches widespread strikes across Hodeidah after Ansarallah targets Saudi vessel in the Red Sea:

Yemeni forces targeted the Saudi vessel NCC MASA while it was navigating through the Red Sea. According to multiple reports, the vessel sustained direct hits, resulting in minor structural damage to its hull. Saudi state television confirmed that the ship had been targeted and suffered limited damage. An official source from the Saudi Public Transport Authority also acknowledged the attack, stating that the vessel, belonging to a Saudi company, sustained minor damage to its hull. The Saudi Press Agency later repeated the same assessment, while additional reports confirmed that the vessel was operating with its Automatic Identification System (AIS) switched on when it was struck. Explosions were also reported in the Bab al-Mandab Strait during the incident.

Shortly after the attack on the Saudi vessel, Saudi warplanes launched a widespread series of airstrikes across Hodeidah Governorate in western Yemen. Initial reports described explosions across Hodeidah as Saudi fighter aircraft flew overhead. Yemeni media reported Saudi aggression against eastern Hodeidah, while Al Mayadeen confirmed that Saudi airstrikes targeted the Port of Hodeidah. Several reports indicated that the strikes were carried out in response to the targeting of the Saudi vessel in the Red Sea. Updated reports stated that Saudi strikes targeted fuel storage tanks at Hodeidah Port as well as locations in eastern Hodeidah. At least four airstrikes reportedly struck objectives in and around the city, primarily focusing on Hodeidah Port. Additional strikes targeted facilities belonging to the Communications Corporation in Hodeidah. A worker was reportedly injured in a building affiliated with Yemen’s Ministry of Communications during the attacks. Separately, Saudi raids also targeted Kamaran Island in Hodeidah Governorate, where a woman was reportedly injured according to Yemeni and Al Mayadeen reports.

Amin Hayyan, Director of the Office of the Yemeni Armed Forces spokesperson, stated that Saudi aggression had begun targeting Hodeidah Port and warned that “they have opened the gates of divine hell upon themselves.” The Yemeni Ministry of Foreign Affairs declared that the Saudi regime bears full responsibility for all consequences resulting from its actions, adding that instead of responding to Yemen’s legitimate demand to lift the blockade, Saudi Arabia had committed a grave act of recklessness that would carry a heavy price. The ministry further stated that the Saudi attacks had transformed the principle of “tit-for-tat” into the defining characteristic of the coming phase. According to Yemen’s official Saba News Agency, citing a source in the Ministry of Defense of the Sana’a government, Yemeni air defenses forced several Saudi fighter jets to withdraw from Yemeni airspace during the operation.
