🔻 Air defense never lets its guard down: Army’s deputy chief

📌 Tehran, IRNA – Rear Admiral Habibollah Sayyari, chief of staff and coordinating deputy of the Islamic Republic of Iran Army, has described air defense as a key pillar of the country’s defensive power and a vigilant shield for national security.


In a message marking Air Defense Day on Tuesday, Sayyari congratulated the chief commander, commanders, personnel, and veterans of the country’s air defense forces, as well as their families, saying the occasion was an opportunity to honor those who protect Iran’s skies.
​
“Undoubtedly, air defense is one of the pillars of Iran’s defensive power and a vigilant shield of national security; a force that, through round-the-clock presence and continuous monitoring of the airspace, protects sensitive and vital centers of the country and, in carrying out this sacred mission, never for a moment neglects vigilance and readiness,” he said.
​
Sayyari said the achievements of Iran’s air defense forces were rooted in the sacrifices of personnel who stood at their posts during the Iran–Iraq War, recalling the “lasting epic” of the martyrs of the Soubashi air defense site and the tireless efforts of Martyr Major General Mansour Sattari, along with thousands of other servicemen who defended the country’s airspace.
​
He added that the same path continued during the second and third imposed wars, when servicemen once again put duty ahead of their own safety and sacrificed their lives in defense of Iran’s territorial integrity.
​
“[They] showed with their sacrifice that the soldiers of the homeland, at the moment of trial, fear no danger and, in the field of defending the homeland, put their lives on the line to honor their pledge,” Sayyari said.
​
He paid tribute to the fallen air defense personnel and wished the force continued success in carrying out its “critical and strategic” missions.

📌 Tehran, IRNA — During the summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) member states in Kyrgyzstan, a series of documents were reviewed, approved, and signed with the participation of the SCO heads of state, including President Masoud Pezeshkian.


Based on the list of documents presented at the summit, the “Bishkek Declaration on the 25th Anniversary of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization” was among the documents of this summit.
​
The draft protocol on amendments and additions to the Charter of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) and decisions regarding the approval of the regulations for the Universal Centre for Countering Security Challenges and Threats to SCO Member States, and the regulations of the executive committee of the Anti-Drug Centre were placed on the agenda.
​
Decisions concerning the provision of translation services for the SCO activities, the member states’ engagement program on combating drug addiction until 2030, the coordinated action plan to counter the spread of synthetic drugs and precursors until 2030, and the action plan for the development of ports and logistics centers for the period 2026–2030 were also cited as other documents and decisions of this summit.
​
Furthermore, decisions on the approval of the initiative for the conservation and sustainable management of biodiversity and the joint action program for 2026–2031, the concept of establishing regional data processing centers, computing capacities and artificial intelligence development, and the cooperation program on creating a “Green Technology Corridor” for 2026–2030 were among the documents presented.
​
Other issues on the summit's document list include declaring the city of Lahore, Pakistan, as the SCO Tourism and Cultural Capital for 2026–2027, a decision on signing a Memorandum of Understanding between the SCO Secretariat and the African Union Commission, the member states’ engagement program on ensuring international information security, and amendments and additions to the regulations on the honorary title of “Goodwill Ambassador.”
​
Additionally, decisions on approving the annual reports of the SCO Secretary-General and the Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS), as well as a decision on the termination of the validity of previous decisions of the Council of Heads of State, were included in the summit’s document list.
​
Alongside these documents, declarations were also presented in the areas of strengthening multilateral cooperation for nuclear security and nuclear physical security, developing engagement on energy conservation and efficiency, developing engagement in the field of energy systems, and supporting and strengthening the institution of the family.

​
Over the past 35 years, despite ups and downs in relations, Iran and Tajikistan have always strived to develop bilateral ties, and today a strategic partnership has been formed between the two countries, he added.
​
One of the most important components of Iran-Tajikistan relations is the shared historical, linguistic, and cultural values that have long connected the peoples of the two countries, he stated.
​
The sustained and uninterrupted high-level political dialogue over the past 35 years has set Iran-Tajikistan relations apart, he reiterated.
​
Over the past 30 years, President Emomali Rahmon has made 22 official and working visits to Iran, and the Iranian presidents have also visited Tajikistan 16 times, Zahidi said.
​
The volume of bilateral trade in 2020 was about $55 million; previously it was higher, but for a period it declined due to some misunderstandings, the diplomat said.
​
However, in 2025, the trade volume between the two countries reached $450 million, which shows that over five years, bilateral trade has increased more than fivefold, he added.
​
He termed cultural cooperation as the fourth factor in developing bilateral relations and said the two countries share a common culture, language, and history.
​
Highlighting countering common threats as the fifth effective factor in Tehran-Dushanbe relations, he said at the top of the common threats are the fight against terrorism, organized crime, and drug trafficking, and the two countries have extensive cooperation in this field.
​
The combination of these factors has brought Iran-Tajikistan relations into a phase of strategic partnership, he emphasized.
​
He also referred to the existing mechanisms for developing bilateral cooperation, saying that so far, 18 sessions of the Joint Commission on Iran-Tajikistan Cooperation have been held in Tehran and Dushanbe, and the latest session of this commission was held last month in Dushanbe.
​
In response to another IRNA question regarding joint Iran-Tajikistan cooperation on Afghanistan, Zahidi said: Afghanistan is a common neighbor of both countries. We do not refrain from economic and trade cooperation with Afghanistan.
​
We have trade and economic cooperation with Afghanistan, including Tajikistan exporting electricity to Afghanistan. We have border markets with Afghanistan, he said.

Tajikistan uses Afghan territory as a transit route for cooperation with Iran, he stated.

On certain political issues, we hold differing views from the current Afghan administration, and this is a matter of public knowledge, Tajik diplomat said.
