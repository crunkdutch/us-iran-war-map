🇮🇶: 🇮🇷 | Breaking — The sacred coffin of Imam Sayyid Ali Khamenei is expected to arrive in the holy city of Karbala within the coming hours, where the final Iraqi funeral procession will begin. According to the funeral committee, the procession will proceed along Abu Mahdi al Muhandis Street toward the Holy Shrine of Imam Husayn (A). Following the conclusion of the Karbala ceremonies, the sacred body will be transferred to Mashhad, where the burial is scheduled to take place from the early hours of tomorrow.

Millions of mourners have been gathering in Karbala since the early morning hours, with pilgrims arriving from across Iraq and abroad. The funeral route stretches approximately 5.8 kilometers, and due to the unprecedented turnout, a journey that normally takes less than an hour is expected to continue until the Maghrib prayers. The sacred coffin is expected to reach the Holy Shrine of Imam Husayn (A) by then as millions line the route to bid their final farewell.

🇮🇷 | IRGC launches retaliation following American aggression:

The IRGC has retaliated following the intense American aggression, during which, according to CENTCOM, more than 80 targets were struck inside Iran. In response, the IRGC released footage of missile launches, including a video showing the launch of a Scud missile, and issued multiple official statements outlining its response.

The IRGC announced that it targeted military sites located within Khalifa Bin Salman Port and Naval Support Activity Bahrain, both in Manama, as well as Ali al Salem Air Base in Kuwait. It also announced that it had shot down an MQ-9A Reaper UCAV that was reportedly attempting to interfere with the operation.

Statement of the Public Relations of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps:

In the name of Allah, the Destroyer of Tyrants

Following the epic creation of the great Iranian nation in the unprecedented and glorious funeral of the unique leader and martyred commander of the Islamic Ummah, the aggressive American regime, whose dimensions of defeat are becoming more and more apparent day by day, and the global reflection of the great and million strong uprising of the proud Iraqi nation in bidding farewell to the martyred mujahid leader, considered it a greater defeat for itself. It once again repeated its habit of breaking its promises and, in a panic to overshadow this historic event, the child killing and terrorist American army, in the early hours of this morning, carried out air attacks against a number of coastal bases and civilian stations on the coasts of Hormozgan Province and Mahshahr, openly violating the ceasefire and trampling on the Islamabad Agreement.

In an initial response to this aggression, the Naval and Aerospace Forces of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, in a joint missile and drone operation, struck 85 important American military targets in Bandar Salman, the Fifth Naval Region of Bahrain, and Ali al Salem Air Base in Kuwait. The IRGC also announced that it shot down an enemy MQ-9 drone that intended to interfere with the operation.

“And victory is not but from Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Wise.”

According to the IRGC statement, Bandar Salman, Bahrain’s Fifth Naval District, and Kuwait’s Ali al Salem Air Base were attacked with 85 missiles and drones, while the American statement mentioned 80 targets. The IRGC, in a way, appeared to convey a message that further attacks could follow. Apart from the difference in the explosive warhead volume and the methods of attack employed by both sides, it must also be acknowledged that, in practical terms, the strikes on these locations have not been sufficiently effective or deterrent and have largely remained part of the usual cycle of military exchanges in the Strait of Hormuz. Last night, however, the United States demonstrated that it had moved beyond this ordinary cycle. Therefore, it is argued that the confrontation should be made more difficult and costly for the enemy through the continuous identification of enemy positions and the infliction of more effective blows.

In the current situation, Americans are not incurring serious costs. If their aerial refueling aircraft, which at times operate not far from Iran’s airspace and islands, could be shot down or at least damaged, or if the bases from which attacks originate were subjected to heavy and extensive strikes, a far more costly engagement for the enemy could be expected. It is also argued that by identifying the precise locations of P-8 maritime patrol aircraft stationed at Sheikh Isa, that area could be attacked with a high number of missiles and drones. In this view, striking a single but essential position would be more effective than dispersing missiles widely without achieving meaningful operational results.
