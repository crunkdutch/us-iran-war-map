⚡️ Al-Wefaq National Islamic Society in Bahrain:
A British court rules to reject the "sovereign immunity" of the Bahraini government in spying on dissidents in London... and the lawsuit filed against it continues.
The UK Supreme Court ruled today, Monday (July 27, 2026), that the Bahraini government cannot invoke sovereign immunity to dismiss a lawsuit filed by two dissidents residing in Britain who accuse the government of hacking their laptops using spyware.
The government had spied on the dissident and spokesman for the Bahrain Freedom Movement, Saeed Al-Shehabi, and activist Musa Abd Ali, and infected their devices with surveillance software called (FinSpy) around 2011, which allowed agents to monitor their work related to political detainees inside the country.
Al-Shehabi and Abd Ali filed a lawsuit against the Bahraini government in the High Court in London in 2020, demanding compensation for "psychological damages" that they said they suffered.
The Bahraini government has been seeking for several years to invoke sovereign immunity to dismiss the lawsuit, while denying carrying out espionage operations, as it filed an appeal last year before the highest court in Britain.
The Supreme Court today, Monday, rejected Bahrain's appeal by a majority of three votes to two, ruling that the espionage operation took place on British territory, even if it had started from abroad.
Reuters news agency said in a report on the matter that the court's decision means that the alleged hacking falls within the "personal injury" exception in British sovereign immunity law, which allows the cases filed by Al-Shehabi and Abd Ali to proceed.
Reuters quoted Abd Ali as saying: "The era of abusing victims and hiding behind sovereign immunity is over," while Al-Shehabi said: "This is not just a victory for us, but for everyone targeted by authoritarian states across borders. The court's message is clear: states that abuse people on British soil will be held accountable."
