⚡️ Direct Threat to Damascus: Approach "Hezbollah"... and We Will Cross the Border

The Israeli Broadcasting Corporation revealed that Iranian-backed militias in Iraq sent threatening messages to Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa, warning him against any military action against "Hezbollah" in Lebanon, stressing that they "will not stand idly by," even if it requires crossing the border from Iraq into Syria.

According to a report by journalist Roi Kais in "Kan News," Iraqi militias informed Damascus that they would stand by "Hezbollah" if al-Sharaa decided to engage in a confrontation with the party, amid a growing belief within the Iranian-backed axis that the Syrian President's public statements do not necessarily reflect what is being prepared behind the scenes.

The report indicated that Ahmed al-Sharaa continues to publicly affirm that he does not intend to intervene militarily in Lebanon, but he does not rule out providing other forms of support to the Lebanese government in confronting "Hezbollah," which does not dispel the doubts of the Iranian-backed parties.

The report quoted an Iraqi political official familiar with the matter as saying that the militias sent direct messages to Damascus, emphasizing that they would side with "Hezbollah" if al-Sharaa chose confrontation, explaining that these factions believe this is the path the Syrian President is preparing for.

The report noted that al-Sharaa had said, in an interview with Al Jazeera yesterday, that Syria is concerned about the spread of Iraqi militias not under Baghdad's authority on the Syrian border, at a time when Iran, the main supporter of these factions, continues to send its messages to Damascus.

He added that Iran recently targeted the al-Tanf base in the Syrian desert on the border with Iraq, under the pretext of the presence of American forces there, even though the American forces had withdrawn from the base.

The report indicated that "Kan News" had previously revealed that the Syrian security establishment is indeed preparing for the possibility of engaging in the "Hezbollah" file, despite al-Sharaa's official rejection of US President Donald Trump's proposal to act against the party within Lebanese territory.

It quoted a Syrian security source close to the authorities as saying that plans are being prepared behind the scenes to deal with "Hezbollah" in Lebanon, to be implemented when necessary, adding: "There is a gap between what is said publicly and what is happening behind the scenes."

This development reflects the increasing regional overlap in the "Hezbollah" file, amid indications that any change in the Syrian position could open the door to a confrontation that transcends the Lebanese borders.
