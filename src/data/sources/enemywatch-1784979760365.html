🥀 | Global Commemoration of Hadhrat Abbas ibn Ali (A):

Hadhrat Abbas ibn Ali (A), the son of Imam Ali (A) and the nephew of Prophet Muhammad (S), remains one of the most beloved and revered personalities in human history. He accompanied his brother, Imam Husayn (A), in Karbala and embodied every quality required of a true follower of the Imam. He never questioned the stance of Imam Husayn (A); rather, he dedicated his entire existence to serving and defending him.

It is narrated that before the birth of Abbas (A), Imam Ali (A) prayed for a son who would possess extraordinary courage and loyalty. Though many children had already been born to Imam Ali (A), Abbas (A) became known as the “Moon of the Hashimites” because of his striking appearance, noble character, courage, and spiritual excellence. He inherited knowledge, wisdom, and manners from the household of the Imams and grew into a figure admired by all.

In Karbala, Abbas (A) served as the standard-bearer of Imam Husayn’s (A) small army. Yet his greatest battle was not against swords and spears, but against his own desires and emotions. Despite being one of the most capable warriors on the battlefield, he remained bound by the command of his Imam. The pain of seeing the suffering of the family of the Prophet (S) while being unable to act freely was itself among his greatest trials.

One of the most significant moments in Karbala came when Abbas (A) reached the Euphrates after breaking through enemy lines. Surrounded by water after days of thirst, he scooped water into his hands. Yet when he remembered the thirst of Imam Husayn (A), the children, and the women in the tents, he threw the water away and refused to drink. His horse did the same...

Though the pain can hardly be understood, the hands were cut down by arrows, not swords. This meant they were pierced and left hanging by muscles and tissues. The pain was extreme.

He filled the water skin and began the journey back toward the tents. The enemy, unwilling to face him honorably, launched attacks from all directions. The tragedy that followed remains among the most painful scenes of Ashura. It is narrated that his hands were struck and severed while he struggled to protect the water skin. He held the standard with his teeth and carried the water skin in order to make it back to the tents of the children who were dying without water. It is narrated that the same killer who struck the six-month-old infant with an arrow also hit his eyes with arrows and targeted the water skin. When he tried to turn his horse back towards the tents, the water skin…The suffering was immense. Despite the unbearable pain, he continued forward, refusing to abandon the water destined for the thirsty children.

Even after losing the use of his arms, Abbas (A) remained focused on reaching the tents. He continued to protect the standard and the water skin for as long as he could. Arrows rained down upon him. One arrow pierced his blessed eye. Another struck the water skin, causing the precious water to spill onto the sands of Karbala. The hopes of the thirsty children vanished before his eyes.

As he attempted to continue, a heavy iron mace struck him. He fell from his horse onto the battlefield. The pain of the wounds, the loss of the water, and the knowledge that he could not fulfill the trust given to him by the children of the camp all converged in those final moments. His martyrdom was not simply the death of a warrior. It was the breaking of the strongest support of Imam Husayn (A) and one of the greatest tragedies of Karbala.

Among the Ahl al-Bayt (A), Hadhrat Abbas (A) attained an unparalleled rank. The Imams (A) repeatedly praised his insight, faith, loyalty, and certainty. Imam Zayn al-Abidin (A) stated that Abbas (A) possessed a station that all martyrs would envy on the Day of Judgment. His shrine in Karbala remains among the most visited sanctuaries in the world, and millions continue to turn to Allah through his remembrance.
